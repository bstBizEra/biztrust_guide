## 1. Ticket

Issue #164 asks, from primary sources only, what the FATF Recommendations and the FATF guidance for the life insurance sector, the IAIS Insurance Core Principles, and the Lao texts located under [#100](https://github.com/bstBizEra/biztrust_guide/issues/100) say about KYC, KYB, AML, consent, disclosure and advice evidence for an insurance intermediary; to state what a broker, as opposed to an insurer, is obliged to do where the sources say so; and to mark `UNVERIFIABLE` where they do not. It asks for no conclusion of law.

## 2. Purpose, and what this file is not

This file records what the named texts say, by quotation or close paraphrase with recommendation, principle or article number and URL, and lists the questions a qualified legal and compliance reviewer must answer. It draws **no conclusion of law**. Nothing here says what BizTrust may or must do. Where a source does not distinguish an intermediary (broker or agent) from an insurer, this file says so and does not infer the distinction.

## 3. What FATF says applies to insurance and to intermediaries

Source: FATF, *International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism & Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations*, adopted February 2012, "Updated June 2026" (cover and p. 3). Landing page: https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/Fatf-recommendations.html ("Adopted on 16 February 2012 and regularly updated since. Last updated in June 2026."). PDF linked from that page: https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/recommendations/fatf-recommendations-2012.pdf. A second PDF at https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/recommendations/FATF%20Recommendations%202012.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf still carries "Updated October 2025"; a text diff of the two shows changes only in the Interpretive Notes to Recommendations 6 and 7 (UN humanitarian-exemption resolutions) and in footnote numbering. None of the passages quoted below differ between the two versions; footnote numbers below follow the June 2026 file.

### 3.1 Scope: which insurance activity, and whether intermediaries are covered

- Glossary, "Financial institutions" (p. 132-133): "Financial institutions means any natural or legal person who conducts as a business one or more of the following activities or operations for or on behalf of a customer: ... 12. Underwriting and placement of life insurance and other investment related insurance." Footnote 107 to item 12: **"This applies both to insurance undertakings and to insurance intermediaries (agents and brokers)."**
- The Glossary item names life and "other investment related insurance" only. Non-life (general) insurance does not appear in the "Financial institutions" list. The FATF life-insurance guidance states the consequence in its own words (section 3.3, Guidance para 6).
- Glossary, "Beneficiary" (p. 127): "In the context of life insurance or another investment linked insurance policy, a beneficiary is the natural or legal person, or a legal arrangement, or category of persons, who will be paid the policy proceeds when/if an insured event occurs, which is covered by the policy."
- Glossary, "Beneficial owner", footnote 97 (p. 126): "This definition should also apply to beneficial owner of a beneficiary under a life or other investment linked insurance policy."
- Glossary, "Core Principles" (p. 128) refers to, among others, "the Insurance Supervisory Principles issued by the International Association of Insurance Supervisors."
- R.26 (p. 23): "For financial institutions subject to the Core Principles, the regulatory and supervisory measures that apply for prudential purposes, and which are also relevant to money laundering and terrorist financing, should apply in a similar manner for AML/CFT purposes. ... Other financial institutions should be licensed or registered and adequately regulated, and subject to supervision or monitoring for AML/CFT purposes, having regard to the risk of money laundering or terrorist financing in that sector."

The Recommendations themselves are addressed to "countries" and to "financial institutions". Apart from footnote 107, the Recommendations and Interpretive Notes quoted below do not use the words "broker" or "insurance intermediary"; they impose each duty on the "financial institution". Whether a given broker is a "financial institution" for a given activity is, on the FATF text, a question of whether it "conducts as a business" activity 12 "for or on behalf of a customer". This file does not answer that question (Q1, Q2, Q4).

### 3.2 The preventive measures, in FATF's words

- **R.1 (p. 10)**: "Countries should require financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions (DNFBPs) to identify, assess and take effective and risk-based action to mitigate their money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing risks."
- **R.10, customer due diligence (p. 14)**: "Financial institutions should be prohibited from keeping anonymous accounts or accounts in obviously fictitious names. Financial institutions should be required to undertake customer due diligence (CDD) measures when: (i) establishing business relations; (ii) carrying out occasional transactions: (i) above the applicable designated threshold (USD/EUR 15,000); or (ii) that are payments or value transfers in the circumstances covered by the Interpretive Note to Recommendation 16; (iii) there is a suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing; or (iv) the financial institution has doubts about the veracity or adequacy of previously obtained customer identification data." The measures are "(a) Identifying the customer and verifying that customer's identity using reliable, independent source documents, data or information. (b) Identifying the beneficial owner, and taking reasonable measures to verify the identity of the beneficial owner, such that the financial institution is satisfied that it knows who the beneficial owner is. For legal persons and arrangements this should include financial institutions understanding the ownership and control structure of the customer. (c) Understanding and, as appropriate, obtaining information on the purpose and intended nature of the business relationship. (d) Conducting ongoing due diligence on the business relationship and scrutiny of transactions undertaken throughout the course of that relationship ..." Timing: "verify the identity of the customer and beneficial owner before or during the course of establishing a business relationship"; countries "may permit financial institutions to complete the verification as soon as reasonably practicable following the establishment of the relationship, where the money laundering and terrorist financing risks are effectively managed and where this is essential not to interrupt the normal conduct of business." Failure: "it should be required not to open the account, commence business relations or perform the transaction; or should be required to terminate the business relationship; and should consider making a suspicious transactions report in relation to the customer." Existing customers: apply "on the basis of materiality and risk".
- **INR.10, section D, "CDD for beneficiaries of life insurance policies", paras 6-9 (p. 68)**: "For life or other investment-related insurance business, financial institutions should, in addition to the CDD measures required for the customer and the beneficial owner, conduct the following CDD measures on the beneficiary(ies) of life insurance and other investment related insurance policies, as soon as the beneficiary(ies) are identified/designated: (a) For beneficiary(ies) that are identified as specifically named natural or legal persons or legal arrangements: taking the name of the person; (b) For beneficiary(ies) that are designated by characteristics or by class ... obtaining sufficient information concerning the beneficiary to satisfy the financial institution that it will be able to establish the identity of the beneficiary at the time of the payout." Para 7: "the verification of the identity of the beneficiary(ies) should occur at the time of the payout." Para 8: the beneficiary "should be included as a relevant risk factor ... in determining whether enhanced CDD measures are applicable"; where a legal-person or legal-arrangement beneficiary is higher risk, enhanced CDD "should include reasonable measures to identify and verify the identity of the beneficial owner of the beneficiary, at the time of payout." Para 9: "Where a financial institution is unable to comply with paragraphs 6 to 8 above, it should consider making a suspicious transaction report."
- **INR.10, section F, para 11 (p. 68-69)**: circumstances "where it would be permissible for verification to be completed after the establishment of the business relationship" include "Non face-to-face business."
- **INR.10, "Lower risks", item (b) product, service, transaction or delivery channel risk factors (p. 71)**: "Life insurance policies where the premium is low (e.g. an annual premium of less than USD/EUR 1,000 or a single premium of less than USD/EUR 2,500)." and "Insurance policies for pension schemes if there is no early surrender option and the policy cannot be used as collateral."
- **R.11, record-keeping (p. 15)**: "maintain, for at least five years, all necessary records on transactions"; "keep all records obtained through CDD measures (e.g. copies or records of official identification documents like passports, identity cards, driving licences or similar documents), account files and business correspondence, including the results of any analysis undertaken ... for at least five years after the business relationship is ended, or after the date of the occasional transaction"; "The CDD information and the transaction records should be available to domestic competent authorities upon appropriate authority."
- **R.12, politically exposed persons (p. 16)**: for foreign PEPs "(whether as customer or beneficial owner)": risk-management systems to determine PEP status, senior-management approval, reasonable measures to establish source of wealth and source of funds, enhanced ongoing monitoring; for domestic PEPs and international-organisation PEPs, reasonable measures to determine status and, in higher-risk cases, the same measures; extends to "family members or close associates". **INR.12 (p. 75)**: "Financial institutions should take reasonable measures to determine whether the beneficiaries of a life insurance policy and/or, where required, the beneficial owner of the beneficiary are politically exposed persons. This should occur at the latest at the time of the payout. Where there are higher risks identified ... a) inform senior management before the payout of the policy proceeds; and b) conduct enhanced scrutiny on the whole business relationship with the policyholder, and consider making a suspicious transaction report."
- **R.15, new technologies (p. 17)**: "Countries and financial institutions should identify and assess the money laundering or terrorist financing risks that may arise in relation to (a) the development of new products and new business practices, including new delivery mechanisms, and (b) the use of new or developing technologies for both new and pre-existing products. In the case of financial institutions, such a risk assessment should take place prior to the launch of the new products, business practices or the use of new or developing technologies."
- **R.17, reliance on third parties (p. 18)**: "Countries may permit financial institutions to rely on third parties to perform elements (a)-(c) of the CDD measures set out in Recommendation 10 or to introduce business, provided that the criteria set out below are met. Where such reliance is permitted, the ultimate responsibility for CDD measures remains with the financial institution relying on the third party." Criteria: immediately obtain the CDD information; satisfy itself that copies "will be made available from the third party upon request without delay"; satisfy itself "that the third party is regulated, supervised or monitored for, and has measures in place for compliance with, CDD and record-keeping requirements in line with Recommendations 10 and 11"; have regard to country risk. **INR.17 para 1 (p. 89)**: "This Recommendation does not apply to outsourcing or agency relationships. In a third-party reliance scenario, the third party should be subject to CDD and record-keeping requirements in line with Recommendations 10 and 11, and be regulated, supervised or monitored. The third party will usually have an existing business relationship with the customer, which is independent from the relationship to be formed by the customer with the relying institution ... This can be contrasted with an outsourcing/agency scenario, in which the outsourced entity applies the CDD measures on behalf of the delegating financial institution, in accordance with its procedures, and is subject to the delegating financial institution's control of the effective implementation of those procedures by the outsourced entity."
- **R.18, internal controls (p. 18)**: "Financial institutions should be required to implement programmes against money laundering and terrorist financing." **INR.18 paras 1-3 (p. 90)**: programmes should include "(a) the development of internal policies, procedures and controls, including appropriate compliance management arrangements, and adequate screening procedures to ensure high standards when hiring employees; (b) an ongoing employee training programme; and (c) an independent audit function to test the system." "The type and extent of measures to be taken should be appropriate having regard to the risk of money laundering and terrorist financing and the size of the business." "Compliance management arrangements should include the appointment of a compliance officer at the management level."
- **R.20, suspicious transactions (p. 19)**: "If a financial institution suspects or has reasonable grounds to suspect that funds are the proceeds of a criminal activity, or are related to terrorist financing, it should be required, by law, to report promptly its suspicions to the financial intelligence unit (FIU)."
- **R.21, tipping-off (p. 19)**: financial institutions, "their directors, officers and employees" should be "protected by law from criminal and civil liability for breach of any restriction on disclosure of information imposed by contract" when reporting in good faith, and "prohibited by law from disclosing ('tipping-off') the fact that a suspicious transaction report (STR) or related information is being filed with the FIU."
- **R.35, sanctions (p. 26)**: sanctions "should be applicable not only to financial institutions and DNFBPs, but also to their directors and senior management."

### 3.3 The FATF life-insurance guidance on intermediaries

Source: FATF, *Guidance for a Risk-Based Approach for the Life Insurance Sector*, October 2018. Landing page: https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/RBA-Life-Insurance-Sector.html (dated 25 October 2018), which links https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/guidance/RBA-Life-Insurance.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf; the text below was read from the same file served at https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/guidance/RBA-Life-Insurance.pdf.coredownload.pdf. The guidance describes itself as "non-binding" (ICP 22.0.11 says the same of it).

- **Para 4 (target audience)**: "Insurers and intermediaries providing life insurance and other investment-related insurance products defined as financial institutions in the FATF Glossary". Footnote 5: "See IAIS ICP 18 for activities performed by intermediaries."
- **Para 6 (non-life)**: "This Guidance does not target: non-life insurance activities. The FATF Glossary excludes non-life insurance activities from the activities performed by 'financial institutions' which fall under the scope of the FATF requirements. As a result, the FATF Recommendations do not apply to non-life insurance (even if the non-life insurance activities are within the scope of financial sanctions regimes). ... That is why a minority of jurisdictions included non-life insurance activities in their AML/CFT framework, based on their specific, national risk evaluations."
- **Para 13**: "A significant proportion of life insurance policies are sold through intermediaries where the life insurer will have limited or no direct contact with the policy holder. ... Life insurance policies are also sold online, where there may not be any face to face interaction with the customer by the insurer or intermediary."
- **Para 14 (three kinds of intermediary)**: "Life insurance intermediation can take a number of different forms, with varying relationships with the life insurer, which may affect the nature and the extent of their AML/CFT responsibilities: Intermediaries acting as financial institutions, as defined in the FATF Glossary, and subjected as such to AML/CFT requirements: Where intermediaries act primarily on behalf of the customer, they are independent of the life insurer whose products they sell and are often referred to as 'independent agents'. These intermediaries are able to select from a range of products across the market and as they are 'financial institutions' per the FATF Glossary, they are subject to AML/CFT requirements. ... Intermediaries not acting as financial institutions: intermediaries may sell products for and on behalf of a single life insurer and are sometimes referred to as 'tied' or 'captive' agents. Where intermediaries are tied to a life insurer, they are generally required to follow the life insurer's AML/CFT policies and procedures. There are also 'trade specific agents' whose core business is not life insurance ..." Footnote 10: "The term 'broker' is frequently used in some countries (e.g. France)".
- **Para 16 (third-party reliance)**: "Where local legislation permits life insurers to rely on the identification and verification work completed by life insurance intermediaries, life insurers must comply with FATF R.17. Life insurers should satisfy themselves that the intermediary is a financial institution, as defined in the FATF Glossary, regulated, and monitored or supervised for AML/CFT, including CDD and record keeping requirements. ... As a best practice, it is recommended that life insurers receive a copy of the CDD record(s), or have access to the database where the information is held ... Ultimate responsibility for CDD remains with the life insurer even when relying on third-parties."
- **Para 17 (outsourcing)**: "When life insurers outsource a part of their AML/CFT function, including the distribution of the products, to a third party which is not regulated, supervised or monitored for AML/CFT, they should include these third parties in their own AML/CFT internal control processes, and monitor them for compliance with their AML/CFT programmes. Life insurer retains full responsibility for AML/CFT controls in such an outsourcing arrangement".
- **Para 48 (insurer's assessment of its intermediaries)**: "life insurers which distribute their products and services through intermediaries should consider the following: Size and status of the intermediary ... Legislative and supervisory approach: Some jurisdictions do not distinguish between different insurance intermediary categories, and supervision is conducted according to the activity performed. ... Role of the intermediary in handling customer's funds: When identifying the risk associated with an intermediary, the insurer should also take into account whether the intermediary handles funds directly from the customer, including in relation to handling pay-outs of the contract, or whether the intermediary plays a purely facilitating/introducing role. It should be noted that insurance intermediation may also be facilitated by digital (e.g., online internet portals and mobile phone applications, etc.) or other means". The risk-factor table before para 48 lists "Reliance on intermediaries and/or outsourcing to third parties which are not subjected to the same AML/CFT obligations as the life insurer" and "Intermediaries which manage the investments and the flow of funds on behalf of the customer on their accounts (e.g., life insurance policy sold by intermediaries accepting cash payments and/or payments on their own accounts)" as higher-risk indicators.
- **Para 56**: "Customer Due Diligence (CDD) processes are intended to ensure that life insurers and intermediaries know the identity of each customer and related third parties, understand and obtain relevant information on the type of transactions that the customer undertakes; evaluate the intended nature of the business relationship and conduct ongoing monitoring".
- **Para 61 (initial CDD, addressed to "the life insurer's or intermediary's initial CDD procedures")**: "Identify and verify the identity of each customer, or persons acting on his/her behalf, before establishing a business relationship; Identify the beneficial owner(s) of customers and beneficiary (if any), and take reasonable measures to verify their identity; As soon as the beneficiary of the contract is identified/designated, take the name of the person, or if the beneficiary is designated by category or by class, or by other means, obtain sufficient information to be able to identify the beneficiary at the time of payout but before funds are disbursed. ... Obtain appropriate information to understand the customer's circumstances and business, including the purpose and the expected nature of the relationship (for example for natural persons: income, wealth, profession, activity; for legal entities: financial statement)."
- **Para 62**: "the life insurer or intermediary should also take reasonable measures to determine whether the beneficiaries of the life insurance policy and/or, where required, the beneficial owner of the beneficiary are politically exposed persons (PEPs)."
- **Para 63 (trigger events)**: "claims notification, surrender requests, renewal of the policy, assignments and policy alterations, such as changes in beneficiaries, increase of assurance amounts."
- **Para 64**: "where life insurers and intermediaries cannot apply the appropriate level of CDD, they should be required not to enter into the business relationship or instead terminate the business relationship (R.10, para 7). In some countries, some insurance law requirements are difficult to reconcile with the obligation to terminate a business relationship (e.g. term insurance or life insurance tied to mortgage credits)."
- **Paras 73-75 (STR)**: "If life insurers or intermediaries suspect, or have reasonable grounds to suspect, that funds are the proceeds of a criminal activity, or related to terrorist financing, they should promptly report their suspicions to the financial intelligence unit. Reporting suspicious transactions is a rule-based requirement which should be made regardless of the amount of the transaction (R. 20)." "The reason for reporting and dismissing suspicious transactions should be documented." "The obligation to report suspicious transactions is not risk based, hence life insurers and intermediaries are obliged to report all suspicious transactions. The act of reporting does not discharge a life insurer or an intermediary of their other AML/CFT obligations."
- **Para 76**: "The senior management and Board of Directors (or equivalent body) are ultimately responsible for ensuring that the life insurer or intermediary establishes and maintains an effective system of internal controls."
- **Footnote 15**: "FATF R. 26 requires countries to ensure that life insurers and intermediaries are licensed or registered by a competent authority ... Second, all reporting entities, including life insurers and intermediaries, have to comply with targeted financial sanctions".
- **Para 99 and Box 2**: "R. 26 requires countries to subject life insurers and intermediaries to effective systems for AML/CFT supervision and/or monitoring." Box 2 quotes R.26 and glosses "Other financial institutions (for intermediaries) should be licensed or registered and adequately regulated, and subject to supervision or monitoring for AML/CFT purposes".
- **Paras 106-107**: "Intermediaries engaged in the business of providing life insurance products can take different forms and have different status, which will bear on their role and responsibilities in the AML/CFT approach, including for the conduct of the customer due diligence process". "Where local provisions permit life insurers to rely on the CDD conducted by life insurance intermediaries (provided that they qualify as FIs), supervisors should check that life insurers apply appropriate internal controls to these intermediaries to determine if the reliance on third parties is appropriate (R. 17)."

Where the guidance says "life insurers and intermediaries" together (paras 56-75), it does not allocate a duty to one rather than the other; the allocation in paras 14, 16 and 17 turns on whether the intermediary is itself a "financial institution" and on "local legislation".

## 4. What the IAIS principles say about intermediaries, conduct and AML

Source: IAIS, *Insurance Core Principles and Common Framework for the Supervision of Internationally Active Insurance Groups*, December 2024. Landing page: https://www.iais.org/activities-topics/standard-setting/icps-and-comframe/ (the iaisweb.org address in the ticket redirects 301 to iais.org). PDF: https://www.iais.org/uploads/2025/06/IAIS-ICPs-and-ComFrame-December-2024.pdf. The ICPs are addressed to supervisors; each Standard says what "the supervisor requires" of insurers and, where stated, intermediaries.

- **Introduction para 8**: "The ICPs only apply to the supervision of intermediaries where this is specifically indicated."

### 4.1 ICP 18 Intermediaries (p. 282)

- Principle statement: "The supervisor sets and enforces requirements for the conduct of insurance intermediaries, in order that they conduct business in a professional and transparent manner."
- 18.0.1: "this ICP applies to the supervision of those conducting the activity of insurance intermediation. ... Where an insurer's direct sales staff solicit, negotiate or sell insurance as employees of the insurer, the supervisor would apply the relevant Standards to the insurer."
- 18.0.2: wholesale intermediaries who "perform one of the functions in the chain of soliciting, negotiating or selling insurance ... are within the scope of this ICP."
- 18.0.4: "Individuals or organisations which only refer (or introduce) potential customers to an insurer or insurance intermediary, without carrying out insurance intermediation, are excluded from the scope of this ICP."
- 18.0.5: "Insurance intermediaries may also perform functions supplemental to insurance intermediation, many of which may be described as outsourced functions of the insurer. These supplemental functions may include underwriting, premium collection, administration, management of insurance claims, loss adjusting and claims appraisal. These functions are excluded from the IAIS definition of insurance intermediation. However, in some jurisdictions these supplemental functions are included in their definition of insurance intermediation."
- 18.0.13: "Intermediaries fall into two categories: i) acting primarily on behalf of the insurer; or ii) acting primarily on behalf of the customer: Where the intermediary acts primarily on behalf of the insurer and sells products for, and on behalf of, one or more insurers, they are often referred to as 'agent' or 'producer'. ... Where the intermediary acts primarily on behalf of the customer, the intermediary is independent of the insurer(s) whose products he sells. Often referred to as 'broker', or 'independent financial adviser', they are able to select products from those available across the market."
- 18.0.14: "Some supervisors do not distinguish between different intermediary categories in legislation and instead supervise according to the activity performed. ... In others, an intermediary is prevented from acting in any capacity other than the one in which it has been licensed to do business".
- 18.0.15: "Insurance intermediation may also be performed by digital means, such as website and mobile phone applications."
- 18.0.28: "ICP 19 (Conduct of Business) addresses conduct of business supervision in respect of both intermediaries and insurers, whereas this ICP addresses other aspects of supervision that are specific to intermediaries. Other ICPs that apply, generally or in part, to the supervision of intermediaries are: ICP 21 Countering Fraud in Insurance; and ICP 22 Anti-Money Laundering and Combating the Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT)."
- 18.1: "The supervisor requires insurance intermediaries operating in its jurisdiction to be licensed." 18.1.2: licensing "at the legal entity level or the individual level, or both".
- 18.2: "The supervisor ensures that insurance intermediaries licensed in its jurisdiction are subject to ongoing supervisory review." 18.2.11 lists as higher-risk areas "Insurance intermediation includes the provision of advice".
- 18.3: "The supervisor requires insurance intermediaries to maintain appropriate levels of professional knowledge and experience, integrity and competence."
- 18.4: "The supervisor requires that insurance intermediaries apply appropriate governance." 18.4.2: requirements for "sole traders and small businesses ... will differ from those for larger entities. Key areas where requirements may vary include internal controls, segregation of duties, and compliance functions."
- 18.5: "The supervisor requires insurance intermediaries to disclose to customers, at least: the terms and conditions of business between themselves and the customer; the relationship they have with the insurers with whom they deal; and information on the basis on which they are remunerated where a potential conflict of interest exists." 18.5.8: "It is particularly important that insurance intermediaries provide customers with information on their relationship with the insurers with whom they deal, specifically whether they are independent or act for one or more insurance companies, and whether they are authorised to conclude insurance contracts on behalf of an insurer or not." 18.5.9: shareholdings in an insurer or group "should be disclosed to customers." 18.5.10 and 18.5.14: status and charging information "may be provided as part of a terms of business agreement, or separately" and "may also be highlighted verbally". 18.5.15: "The supervisor may also require that, upon a customer's request to the intermediary, the customer is provided with further information on fees and commissions, including the level of fees and commissions. The intermediary should make the customer aware of his/her right to request information on fees and commissions." 18.5.17: "the intermediary has robust procedures in place to identify and avoid, or manage, conflicts of interest ... Where they cannot be avoided, or managed satisfactorily, this would result in the intermediary declining to act."
- 18.6: "The supervisor requires an insurance intermediary who handles client monies to have safeguards in place to protect these funds." 18.6.2: "Some jurisdictions have specific legal requirements in respect of the cash flows where monies are transferred via an intermediary from the customer to the insurer, and vice versa, including in determining whether the customer or the insurer is at risk in respect of such funds." 18.6.4: "In some jurisdictions, premiums are deemed to have been paid to the insurer as soon as the customer pays premiums to the intermediary." 18.6.5 lists what a client-money policy may cover: separate client accounts "held with licensed banks within the jurisdiction, or specified other jurisdictions"; no other monies in the account except stated exceptions; prompt payment in; authorisation of payments out; books and records subject to audit; regular reconciliations; no negative per-client balance; treatment of interest. 18.6.7: the supervisor "may require that the terms and conditions of such accounts are disclosed to their customers, including whether funds held in such accounts are at the risk of clients or at the risk of the insurer."
- 18.7 to 18.9: supervisory measures against licensed intermediaries; escalation; measures "against individuals or entities that conduct insurance intermediation without the necessary licence."

### 4.2 ICP 19 Conduct of business (p. 302)

- Principle statement: "The supervisor requires that insurers and intermediaries, in their conduct of insurance business, treat customers fairly, both before a contract is entered into and through to the point at which all obligations under a contract have been satisfied."
- 19.0.11: "Legislation should provide requirements with which insurers and intermediaries must comply, including foreign insurers and intermediaries selling products on a cross-border basis."
- 19.1: "The supervisor requires insurers and intermediaries to act with due skill, care and diligence when dealing with customers."
- 19.2: "... establish and implement policies and processes on the fair treatment of customers, as an integral part of their business culture."
- 19.3: "... avoid or properly manage any potential conflicts of interest."
- 19.4: "... have arrangements in place in dealing with each other to ensure the fair treatment of customers." 19.4.1: "The supervisor should require insurers to conduct business only with intermediaries that are licensed, and to verify that the intermediaries under such arrangements have the appropriate knowledge and ability with which to conduct such business."
- 19.6: "... promote products and services in a manner that is clear, fair and not misleading." 19.6.1: the insurer "should be responsible for providing promotional material that is accurate, clear and not misleading not only to customers but also to intermediaries who may rely on such information."
- 19.7: "The supervisor requires insurers and intermediaries to provide timely, clear and adequate pre-contractual and contractual information to customers." 19.7.2: "Where insurers use intermediaries for the distribution of insurance products, the insurer should be satisfied that the intermediaries involved are providing information to customers in a manner that will assist them in making an informed decision." 19.7.3: "Customers should be appropriately informed before and at the point of sale." 19.7.5: "Mandatory information should be prepared in written format, on paper or in a durable and accessible medium (electronic, for instance)." 19.7.9: "Insurers and intermediaries should be able to demonstrate to the supervisor that customers have received information necessary to understand the product."
- 19.8: "Where customers receive advice before concluding an insurance contract the supervisor requires that the advice provided by insurers and intermediaries takes into account the customer's disclosed circumstances." 19.8.1: "Advice goes beyond the provision of product information and relates specifically to the provision of a personalised recommendation on a product in relation to the disclosed needs of the customer." 19.8.2: "The insurer or the intermediary should make it clear to the customer whether advice is provided or not." 19.8.3: information to seek before advising "may, for example, include information on the customer's: financial knowledge and experience; needs, priorities and circumstances; ability to afford the product; and risk profile." 19.8.4: the supervisor "may wish to specify particular types of policies or customers for which advice is not required to be given ... simple to understand products ... or, where relevant, mandated coverage for which there are no options." 19.8.5: where the customer declines advice that would normally be expected, "it is advisable that the insurer or intermediary retains an acknowledgment by the customer to this effect." 19.8.6: "The basis on which a recommendation is made should be explained and documented ... Where advice is provided, this should be communicated to the customer in written format, on paper or in a durable and accessible medium, and a record kept in a 'client file'." 19.8.7: "The insurer or intermediary should retain sufficient documentation to demonstrate that the advice provided was appropriate, taking into account the customer's disclosed circumstances." 19.8.8: review of "client files" after the fact.
- 19.9 is addressed to insurers only ("The supervisor requires insurers to: service policies appropriately ..."); 19.9.9: where terms change "the insurer should notify the policyholder of their rights and obligations regarding such changes and obtain the policyholder's consent as appropriate." 19.10 (claims) is addressed to insurers only. 19.11: "The supervisor requires insurers and intermediaries to handle complaints in a timely and fair manner."
- 19.12: "The supervisor requires insurers and intermediaries to have policies and processes for the protection and use of information on customers." 19.12.3: "Legislation identifies the provisions relating to privacy protection under which insurers and intermediaries are allowed to collect, hold, use or communicate personal information on customers to third parties." 19.12.5 lists what such policies may cover, including "timely notification to affected customers and competent authorities" after a privacy failure. 19.12.7: "The supervisor should not allow insurers and intermediaries to use customer information that they collect and hold in a manner that results in unfair treatment."

### 4.3 ICP 22 Anti-money laundering and combating the financing of terrorism (p. 345)

- Principle statement: "The supervisor requires insurers and intermediaries to take effective measures to combat money laundering and terrorist financing. The supervisor takes effective measures to combat money laundering and terrorist financing."
- 22.0.5: "The IAIS is a FATF Observer Organisation and, accordingly, endorses the FATF Recommendations. This ICP is intended to be consistent with the FATF Recommendations; however, compliance with the FATF Recommendations does not necessarily imply observance of ICP 22 nor does observance of ICP 22 necessarily imply compliance with the FATF Recommendations."
- 22.0.7: "This ICP applies to the underwriting and placement of life insurance and other investment-related insurance. Depending upon the jurisdiction's assessment of the ML/TF risk posed by the non-life sector, the jurisdiction should consider whether and to what extent to apply this ICP to that sector as well."
- 22.0.8: "The FATF Recommendations require jurisdictions to designate a 'competent authority' or authorities to have responsibility for ensuring that financial institutions (including insurers and intermediaries) adequately comply".
- 22.0.10: Part A applies "where the supervisor is a designated AML/CFT competent authority", Part B "where the supervisor is not".
- 22.0.13: targeted financial sanctions "is not subject to the RBA described in this ICP".
- 22.1 (Part A): the supervisor "has a thorough and comprehensive understanding of the ML/TF risks to which insurers and/or intermediaries are exposed" and "applies a Risk-Based Approach (RBA) consistent with FATF Recommendations." 22.1.1: RBA includes "the specific process of supervising institutions (ie insurers and intermediaries, as applicable) that apply an AML/CFT RBA."
- 22.2: the supervisor "issues to insurers and/or intermediaries enforceable means on AML/CFT obligations consistent with the FATF Recommendations, for matters which are not in primary legislation". 22.2.1: "the FATF Recommendations require the basic obligations of customer due diligence (CDD), record keeping and the reporting of suspicion to be set in primary legislation". 22.2.3: "The supervisor should require insurers and/or intermediaries to take appropriate steps to identify, assess and understand their ML/TF risks in relation to products (including services and transactions), geography, customers and delivery channels."
- 22.3: "an effective supervisory framework to monitor and enforce compliance by insurers and/or intermediaries with AML/CFT requirements." 22.3.3: supervisory review "off-site monitoring and/or on-site inspection". 22.3.5: "require insurers and/or intermediaries to undertake AML/CFT assessments on a regular basis". 22.3.7: "require insurers and intermediaries to provide regular and timely training in AML/CFT to Board Members, Senior Management and other staff".
- 22.6 (Part B): where the supervisor is not the AML/CFT competent authority it "liaises with and seeks to obtain information from the designated competent authority relating to AML/CFT by insurers and intermediaries."

ICP 22 uses "insurers and/or intermediaries" throughout and does not separate the duties of one from the other; 22.0.7 restricts the ICP to life and investment-related insurance unless the jurisdiction extends it.

### 5.1 AML/CFT Law No. 64/NA (S22)

#100 section 3.3 locates S22, the Law on Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Financing of Terrorism (Amended) No. 64/NA of 1 July 2024, BOL unofficial English translation, https://www.bol.gov.la/en/fileupload/05-01-2026_1767607844.pdf (Gazette record https://laoofficialgazette.gov.la/index.php?r=site/display&id=2304). #100 section 4.2.7 records Art. 15 (reporting entities: "insurance companies" and "companies or agents selling and managing financial payment instruments" are named; "insurance agents and brokers are not named as such") and Art. 25 (ten-year retention), and #100 Q15 asks whether agents and brokers are reporting entities by NCC designation. Those two articles are not repeated here. The following article pointers were read on 2026-09-06 from the same BOL file, so that the legal seat can find the KYC, KYB and AML provisions without re-reading it:

- Art. 3(7): "Transaction refers to services provided by reporting entities to their customers including banking, insurance, securities, precious metals, virtual assets and etc". Art. 3(8): beneficial ownership "refers to the natural person(s) who ultimately owns or controls a customer and/or the natural person on whose behalf a transaction is being conducted." Art. 3(9): PEPs "refer to foreign politically exposed persons, state officials, and officials of international organizations". Art. 3(22): "Customer shall mean natural persons, legal persons or organizations that use the services of the reporting entities".
- Art. 8: "This law applies to natural persons, legal persons and organizations, both local and foreign, inside and outside of the territory of Lao PDR."
- Art. 16 lists the reporting entities' obligations: internal control; risk based approach; know your customer; customer due diligence; customer information collection; PEPs; correspondent banks; data collection on money transfer; record keeping; reporting; STRs; provisional measures; confidentiality. Branches and subsidiaries "in both domestic and oversea" must comply.
- Art. 17 (internal control): policies, procedures and internal monitoring; screening of employees; training; "Independent internal audit"; evaluation; periodic reports to the supervisory agency; and "Reporting entities must appoint compliance officers at the management level or senior officer with qualifications, experience in anti-money laundering ... as well as to coordinate with the Anti-Money Laundering Intelligence Office (AMLIO)."
- Art. 18 (RBA): identify, assess, monitor and mitigate risk "such as customers, existing and newly created products and services, service channels, methods and new technology in service delivery or business operations including geography or countries. The implementation mechanism of Risk-Based Approach is defined in separate regulation."
- Art. 19 (KYC): "Reporting entities must know your customer by requiring customers to show their identification documents such as identity cards, family books, passport, enterprise registration or other official documents that can be verified about the customers or those who are acting on behalf of the customers and then properly record, copy and secure storage. The implementation mechanism of Know Your Customer is defined in separate regulation."
- Art. 20 (CDD): triggers are new business relationships; "occasional, one-off or multiple transactions that are suspicious"; "Complex, high value and unusual transactions"; transactions on behalf of a customer in the name of a legal person or arrangement; suspicion of ML/TF; doubts about previously obtained data. "In addition, the reporting entities must conduct on-going monitoring ... including knowing the sources of their funds if necessary." Special attention to persons in countries without AML/CFT law or "under the sanction list of relevant international organizations". "The implementation mechanism of Customer Due Diligence is defined in separate regulation."
- Art. 21 (customer information; the KYB provision): for individuals "name and surname, date of birth, nationality, address and occupation"; "For legal persons customers, the reporting entities must collect, prove and verify information on the name and address of the companies, identification documents of directors, details of shareholders, activities and size of the business." On failure "the reporting entities must stop providing services or not establishing business relationships with that customer and consider it as suspicious transaction and report to the Anti-Money Laundering Intelligence Office as defined in Article 27". Also: collect "the purpose and objective of customers in using services" and "find out whether the customer's business relationship is in the name of the customer himself or someone else to find out the ultimate beneficiary owner".
- Art. 22 (PEPs): risk-management system to identify PEPs; senior-management approval; source of wealth and funds; enhanced ongoing monitoring; family members and close associates.
- Art. 26 (threshold reporting): cash and wire transfers "above the applicable designated threshold" set by the Bank of the Lao PDR, and "Other transactions as defined by AMLIO."
- Art. 27 (STR): where there is suspicion "or has reasonable grounds to suspect that transaction might be proceeds of a criminal activity ... including customer's attempted transaction, regardless of the amount of the transaction. The reporting entities should be required to promptly report such suspicious transaction to AMLIO."
- Art. 28 (provisional measures): "Reporting entities, postal enterprises and relevant legal persons" may suspend or withhold transactions; funds "related to terrorist and terrorist organization as per consolidated UNSCR sanction list" must be withheld or frozen "without delay".
- Art. 29 (confidentiality and tipping-off): directors, officers and employees "are prohibited from disclosing the fact that STR or related information is being filed with AMLIO"; customer-confidentiality clauses "shall not an obstacle in providing information to related the authorities"; good-faith reporters "will not be disciplined or prosecuted".
- Art. 42 (prohibitions): shell banks; "Opening anonymous accounts"; relationships with persons "under sanction list of the United Nations Security Council".
- Art. 44: the National Coordination Committee supervises, coordinating with ministries including the Ministry of Finance and the Bank of the Lao PDR.
- Art. 59: in force from 1 November 2024; replaces Law No. 50/NA of 21 July 2014.

S22 addresses "reporting entities" throughout. It does not use the words "insurance broker" or "insurance agent" except that Art. 15 names "insurance companies" and "agents selling and managing financial payment instruments". Whether a broker is a reporting entity is #100 Q15 and Q1 below.

### 5.2 Insurance Law No. 78/NA (S1) and Ministry of Finance instruments, as recorded in #100

- Agents: S1 Art. 60-63 (#100 section 4.1.4). Art. 62 `[working paraphrase]` lists the agent's duties, including "(3) to propose and collect the client's documents; (4) to explain terms; (5) to notify the premium; (6) to collect the premium from the client for delivery to the insurer within the time set". S2 (Instruction No. 3996/MOF) agent prohibitions include "advising a client to withhold material information" and "disclosing insurer information without authority" (#100 section 4.1.4).
- Brokers: S1 Art. 64-67 (#100 section 4.1.5). Art. 66 `[working paraphrase]`: "(3) honest conduct; (4) keeping confidential the secrets of the insurer and of the purchaser of insurance; ... (7) obtaining written authorisation from the client before acting as the client's representative towards the insurer, and informing the client when the contract takes effect; ... (9) explaining terms; (10) advising on claims; (11) supervising its staff; (12) reporting quarterly and annually to the Ministry." #100 section 4.2.3 records that Art. 66 has no clause on collecting or holding premium.
- Insurer: S1 Art. 82(9) `[working paraphrase]`: the insurer must keep confidential the information received from the purchaser (#100 section 4.3.1). S1 Art. 24 `[working paraphrase]`: the contract takes effect when the insurer has received the application, the information it requires, and the premium (#100 section 4.2.1).
- All three: S1 Art. 85 `[working paraphrase]` prohibits insurers, agents and brokers from "destroying information or evidence" and from "using another person's personal information for gain" (#100 section 4.1.5). S1 Art. 68 and 77: record-keeping and reporting under the Accounting Law (#100 section 4.3.2).
- Repealed S7 (Decision 3058/MOF of 2018) Art. 12(1.12) `[working paraphrase]`: an agent must notify the client of the insurer's acceptance or refusal within ten days (general) or thirty days (life); Art. 13(2.4): a broker may not place the client's premium in its own account (#100 section 4.2.4, marked repealed).
- S3, Instruction on insurance products No. 3991/MOF (2023): #100 section 3.1 records that only Articles 1-3 were read; whether it contains disclosure or advice rules is **UNVERIFIABLE** from #100 (Q9).
- Electronic Data Protection Law (S16): Art. 8-10, 16 and 17 `[working paraphrase]`, consent for use, disclosure and transfer of personal data, and no transfer outside the Lao PDR without the data owner's consent (#100 section 4.3.3). Accounting Law (S15) Art. 50: ten-year retention (#100 section 4.3.2).

Nothing in S1 or S2 as recorded in #100 uses the terms "know your customer", "customer due diligence", "advice" as a defined activity, or "suitability". No Lao text recorded in #100 requires a broker to disclose its remuneration or the identity of the insurers it acts for to the customer; that point is **UNVERIFIABLE** (Q11).

## 7. Questions the legal and compliance seat must answer

1. Is a Lao-licensed insurance broker (S1 Art. 64) a "reporting entity" under S22 Art. 15, which names "insurance companies" and "companies or agents selling and managing financial payment instruments" but not brokers, and has the National Coordination Committee issued any designation of insurance agents or brokers under the closing words of Art. 15? (Extends #100 Q15.)
2. Does the AML regime differ for non-life (general) and life business in the Lao PDR? FATF Glossary item 12 and Guidance para 6 confine the FATF standard to life and investment-related insurance, and ICP 22.0.7 leaves the non-life sector to the jurisdiction; S22 Art. 3(7) defines "transaction" to include "insurance" without distinction.
3. Which "separate regulation" implements S22 Art. 18 (RBA), Art. 19 (KYC) and Art. 20 (CDD) for the insurance sector, which authority issued it, and does it address intermediaries?
4. Under Lao law, is a broker a financial institution in its own right for CDD purposes (Guidance para 14, first category), a party whose CDD an insurer may rely on under a rule like FATF R.17, or an outsourced or agency channel under the insurer's programme (INR.17 para 1; Guidance para 17)? Who holds ultimate CDD responsibility for a policy placed through BizTrust, and where is that written?
5. For legal-person customers, does S22 Art. 21 para 2 ("details of shareholders") or its implementing regulation set a beneficial-ownership threshold or require verification of the ownership chain as FATF R.10(b) describes, and what identification documents are acceptable for foreign directors and shareholders?
6. Which retention rule governs an intermediary's KYC and advice records: S22 Art. 25 (ten years from transaction or end of relationship), S15 Art. 50 (ten years), FATF R.11 (five years), or S16 Art. 15 (delete when the purpose ends unless the law provides otherwise), and does S16 Art. 17 restrict where those records may be stored?
7. Does S22 Art. 27 (STR) and Art. 29 (tipping-off) bind an intermediary that is not a designated reporting entity, and what is AMLIO's reporting channel, format and timeline for the insurance sector?
8. S22 Art. 3(9) defines PEPs as "foreign politically exposed persons, state officials, and officials of international organizations". Does the beneficiary-PEP check at payout in FATF INR.12 and Guidance para 62 fall on the insurer, the broker, or both under Lao law?
9. Does any Lao text (S1 Art. 66(9) "explaining terms", S3 Instruction No. 3991/MOF, or another MOF instrument) require a documented needs assessment, a written recommendation, or a declined-advice acknowledgment of the kind described in ICP 19.8.5-19.8.7, and does Lao law define "advice" at all?
10. What form must the S1 Art. 66(7) written authorisation take; may it be electronic under the Law on Electronic Transactions (not analysed in #100); and must consent under S16 Art. 16-17 for use, disclosure and cross-border transfer of personal data be separate from the brokerage authorisation? Does an STR or a disclosure to AMLIO require, or override, the data owner's consent (S22 Art. 29 para 2)?
11. Does any Lao text require a broker to disclose to the customer which insurers it acts for, whether it may bind an insurer, or its commission (ICP 18.5)? None was found in #100: **UNVERIFIABLE**.
12. Which client-money rule applies to a broker today: S1 Art. 66 (silent), S1 Art. 62(6) (agents only), the repealed S7 Art. 13, or a later MOF instrument (S11(a) and S11(b) in #100 could not be downloaded)? Is premium paid to the broker deemed paid to the insurer (ICP 18.6.4) in the Lao PDR? (Extends #100 Q4 and Q5.)
13. Does the freezing duty in S22 Art. 28 ("Reporting entities, postal enterprises and relevant legal persons") and the prohibition in Art. 42(4) reach a broker, and which sanctions lists must it screen against?
14. S22 Art. 19 requires customers "to show their identification documents". Does the separate KYC regulation permit remote or digital identity verification for non-face-to-face onboarding, and does it allow verification to be completed after the relationship begins as FATF R.10 and INR.10 para 11 contemplate?
15. What does proportionality allow a small broker under S22 Art. 17 (compliance officer "at the management level or senior officer", independent internal audit) and, if the ICPs are followed, ICP 18.4.2?
16. Which authority supervises an insurance broker for AML/CFT purposes: the Ministry of Finance under S1 Art. 88, the Bank of the Lao PDR, AMLIO, or the National Coordination Committee under S22 Art. 44-46? Is the Ministry of Finance a "designated AML/CFT competent authority" in the sense of ICP 22.0.10?

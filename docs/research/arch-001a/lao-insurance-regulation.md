## 1. Ticket

Issue #100 asks for a pack, built from public primary sources only, covering: the Lao PDR insurance law and implementing regulations as published by the Ministry of Finance or its insurance regulator; licensing classes for brokers and agents; rules on premium handling, segregation, remittance deadlines or risk transfer; data residency, retention or disclosure rules touching insurance records; and published tax treatment of premium and commission. It blocks #117 and #119 and is a sub-issue of #91. ADR-014 and ADR-019 are the decisions waiting on this review.

## 2. Purpose, and what this file is not

This file locates the public primary texts, records what they say (quotation or close paraphrase, with article number and URL), and lists the questions a qualified Lao-law reviewer must answer. It draws **no conclusion of law**. Nothing here says what BizTrust may or must do; it says only what the published texts say and where the texts are silent or unreachable.

Translation status is stated for every source. Most current instruments exist only in Lao. Where a finding rests on a Lao-only text, the English wording is the researcher's working paraphrase of the Lao, marked `[working paraphrase of the Lao text]`; it is not an official translation and the legal seat must read the Lao original. Where an English text is a published "Unofficial Translation", it is marked as such with the publisher.

Secondary commentary (law-firm newsletters, summaries, tax guides) was not used as a source for any finding. Where only such commentary exists, the finding is `UNVERIFIABLE`.

## 4. Findings by topic

Conventions: "S1 Art. 62" means Article 62 of source S1 in section 3. `[working paraphrase of the Lao text]` marks the researcher's own reading of a Lao-only source. Quotations from published unofficial English translations are in quotation marks with the publisher named in section 3.

### 4.1 Licensing classes for brokers and agents

**4.1.1 The governing law is Law No. 78/NA of 29 November 2019, not the 2011 law that exists in English.**
S1 Art. 100 `[working paraphrase of the Lao text]`: this Law replaces the Law on Insurance (Amended) No. 06/NA dated 21 December 2011; insurance companies established before this Law must bring themselves into compliance within three years of its entry into force. S1 Art. 101: the Law enters into force fifteen days after publication in the Official Gazette following the presidential promulgation decree (decree dated 10 Jan 2020; Gazette 30 Mar 2020).
URL: S1.

**4.1.2 Insurance business and its two classes.**
S1 Art. 35-37 `[working paraphrase]`: insurance business consists of life insurance business and general insurance business, each of which may include reinsurance; an insurance company is established as a public company or a limited company under the Enterprise Law and must obtain a licence from the Ministry of Finance; one juristic person may operate only one class (life or general).
S1 Art. 39 `[working paraphrase]`: minimum registered capital of thirty billion kip for a life insurer and thirty billion kip for a general insurer, with a higher figure for reinsurers; S2 section on Art. 38-39 restates thirty billion kip for insurers and seventy billion kip for reinsurance companies.
S1 Art. 41 `[working paraphrase]`: a security deposit of not less than ten percent of registered capital must be placed at a commercial bank in the Lao PDR.
S1 Art. 22 `[working paraphrase]`: insurance contracts for objects insured in the Lao PDR may be concluded only with insurers licensed and operating in the Lao PDR. The Civil Code (S14, unofficial English), Art. 465, says the same: "Every type of insurance contract that its objectives are insured in the Lao PDR must be only made with the insurers who are established and operated in Lao PDR. An insurance contract must be made in writing."
URLs: S1, S2, S14.

**4.1.3 The intermediary classes named in the law.**
S1 Art. 45 `[working paraphrase]`: professions attached to insurance business include (1) insurance agent (ຕົວແທນປະກັນໄພ) and (2) insurance broker (ນາຍໜ້າປະກັນໄພ), followed by risk-management, loss-prevention, loss-assessment, claims-adjusting and related services.
S1 Art. 88(11) `[working paraphrase]`: the Ministry of Finance issues, suspends and revokes the licences of insurance companies, agents, brokers and the related professions.
URL: S1.

**4.1.4 Insurance agent: definition, registration card, conditions, duties.**
S1 Art. 60 `[working paraphrase]`: an insurance agent is an individual or juristic person authorised by an insurer, under an agency contract, to carry out agency activities on the insurer's behalf; the insurer must submit its list of agents to the Ministry of Finance for registration and issuance of an agent card (ບັດຕົວແທນ); only a person holding an agent card issued by the Ministry may act as an agent; registration is renewed annually against a fee.
S1 Art. 61 `[working paraphrase]`: an individual agent must be a Lao citizen resident in the Lao PDR, at least eighteen, hold an agent-training certificate issued by an insurer or a recognised body, and have no conviction for an intentional offence; a juristic agent must be a lawfully established enterprise whose staff meet the individual conditions; a person whose card was withdrawn for breach may not re-apply for two years.
S1 Art. 62 `[working paraphrase]`: an agent's rights and duties are (1) to sell only the products of the insurer it represents, which must be licensed in the Lao PDR; (2) to issue the insurance contract within the authority given by the insurer; (3) to propose and collect the client's documents; (4) to explain terms; (5) to notify the premium; (6) **to collect the premium from the client for delivery to the insurer within the time set (ເກັບຄ່າປະກັນໄພ ຈາກລູກຄ້າ ເພື່ອສົ່ງໃຫ້ຜູ້ປະກັນໄພ ຕາມກຳນົດເວລາ)**; (7) to assist with claims; (8) to receive commission from the insurer; (9) other duties under law.
S1 Art. 63 `[working paraphrase]`: where an agent breaches the agency contract and harms the insured, the insurer remains liable to the insured under any contract the agent arranged, and the agent must reimburse the insurer.
S2, agent section `[working paraphrase]`: an individual may be agent for only one insurer per class of insurance; commercial banks and financial institutions under Bank of the Lao PDR supervision may be agents for several insurers; other juristic persons may be agent for one insurer per class; car dealerships and car shops may act for several insurers but may sell only motor insurance products. Agent-card fees: individual 200,000 kip on issue and 100,000 kip on renewal; juristic person 1,000,000 kip on issue and 500,000 kip on renewal; the card is valid for one year. Agent prohibitions listed in S2 include selling without a card or authorisation, advising a client to withhold material information, offering premiums, benefits or discounts other than those set by the insurer, receiving or holding any part of claim monies due to a beneficiary, sitting on any insurer's board, and disclosing insurer information without authority.
URLs: S1, S2.

**4.1.5 Insurance broker: definition, licence, conditions, duties.**
S1 Art. 64 `[working paraphrase]`: an insurance broker is a juristic person established under the Enterprise Law and licensed by the Ministry of Finance to carry out insurance brokerage; the law does not provide for individual brokers.
S1 Art. 65 `[working paraphrase]`: conditions for a broker licence are enterprise registration; registered capital and a security deposit as set in regulations; certification of insurance knowledge and experience from bodies recognised by the Ministry of Finance; and other conditions in the broker regulations.
S2, broker section `[working paraphrase]`: a broker may sell the products of all insurers registered in the Lao PDR; the Ministry decides on a complete application within seven working days (three days to notify a refusal); the licence fee is 5,000,000 kip on issue and 2,000,000 kip on renewal; the licence runs for one calendar year (1 January to 31 December) and renewal must be applied for by 15 December; conditions under Art. 65 are minimum registered capital of 500,000,000 kip, a security deposit of twenty percent of registered capital at a commercial bank in the Lao PDR, a professional insurance certificate or passed examination or specialised training, written confirmation from at least two insurance companies that they will appoint the applicant as broker, no prior broker-licence revocation, and no criminal conviction; brokers licensed before S2 have two years to comply.
S1 Art. 66 `[working paraphrase]`: broker rights and duties are (1) legal protection of its activities; (2) proposing opinions to the Ministry; (3) honest conduct; (4) **keeping confidential the secrets of the insurer and of the purchaser of insurance**; (5) liability for damage caused by its own fault to insurer or purchaser; (6) recommending products of insurers in the Lao PDR; (7) **obtaining written authorisation from the client before acting as the client's representative towards the insurer, and informing the client when the contract takes effect**; (8) receiving a service fee or commission; (9) explaining terms; (10) advising on claims; (11) supervising its staff; (12) reporting quarterly and annually to the Ministry.
S1 Art. 67 `[working paraphrase]`: the broker licence is revoked for false application documents, no activity for one year without notifying the Ministry, or non-compliance with the licence conditions.
S1 Art. 69 `[working paraphrase]`: insurers, brokers and juristic-person agents must set aside reserve funds under the Enterprise Law.
S1 Art. 85 `[working paraphrase]` (prohibitions for insurers, agents and brokers) include destroying information or evidence and concealing revenue, using another person's personal information for gain, quoting premiums below the rates set by the Ministry, copying another's products without permission, and operating a class of insurance business other than the one licensed. S1 Art. 86(3) `[working paraphrase]` prohibits any person from using the name, logo or mark of an insurer, agent or broker without Ministry authorisation.
URLs: S1, S2.

**4.1.6 Historical English text (superseded).**
S10 (2011 law, unofficial English) Art. 76: "Insurance agent means an organization or individual authorized by an insurer on the basis of a contract of insurance agency ... The insurer shall submit a list of its insurance agents to the Ministry of Finance." Art. 81: "Insurance broker means an individual or organization that is authorized to conduct insurance broking activities." Art. 82: broker establishment follows "the same rules for application as for the establishment of an insurance company as defined in Article 17 of this Law", with "Details for the registered capital of the insurance broker ... defined in a separate regulation." Art. 85: "An insurance broker must purchase professional indemnity insurance for its insurance broking activities at an insurer operating in Lao PDR." These provisions were replaced by S1 (Art. 100); they are recorded only so the legal seat can see what the English text says and where S1 departs from it (individual brokers no longer appear; no professional-indemnity clause was found in S1 Art. 64-67).
URL: S10.

### 4.2 Premium handling, segregation, remittance and risk transfer

**4.2.1 When the risk transfers: contract effectiveness and premium payment.**
S1 Art. 24 `[working paraphrase]`: an insurance contract takes effect when the insurer has received the application, has received the information it requires, and the premium has been paid as agreed, unless the contract provides otherwise.
S1 Art. 31 `[working paraphrase]`: the purchaser must pay the premium within the period fixed in the contract; if none is fixed, within fifteen days from receipt of the policy document; the parties may agree otherwise.
S1 Art. 20 `[working paraphrase]`: a reinsurer may not demand premium directly from the purchaser of insurance, and the purchaser may not claim directly against the reinsurer, unless the contract provides otherwise.
S2, Art. 30-31 section `[working paraphrase]`: claims timetables for insurers (acknowledgement within three working days, payment within seven working days after complete documents, for the classes listed).
For comparison, S10 (2011 law, unofficial English) Art. 39: "An insurance contract shall be effective only when the insurance contract are concluded or when there appears evidence the insurer have accepted the insurance and the insurance purchasers have paid the insurance premium, except otherwise agreed upon in the insurance contract."
URLs: S1, S2, S10.

**4.2.2 Who may collect premium.**
S1 Art. 62(6) (agent): collect the premium from the client for delivery to the insurer within the time set (see 4.1.4). S1 Art. 66 (broker): no clause on collecting or holding premium was found; item (8) refers only to the broker's own fee or commission. S1 Art. 82(1) `[working paraphrase]`: the insurer has the right to collect premium under the contract.
URL: S1.

**4.2.3 Segregation, client accounts, remittance deadlines: nothing in force was found.**
No provision of S1 or S2 requiring an agent or broker to hold premium in a separate or trust account, or fixing a deadline for passing premium to the insurer other than "within the time set" in S1 Art. 62(6), was found. S11(a) and S11(b) (reserves/solvency; data reporting by insurers and brokers) could not be downloaded and may bear on this: **UNVERIFIABLE**.

**4.2.4 The repealed 2018 rule, recorded for the legal seat's attention.**
S7 (Decision 3058/MOF, 27 Sep 2018) `[working paraphrase]`: Art. 12(1.8), an agent who receives premium in cash or by cheque must transfer it into the insurer's deposit account or to the insurer immediately (ທັນທີ) so that the client is covered promptly; Art. 13(1.7), the same duty for a broker; Art. 13(2.4), a broker is prohibited from placing the client's premium money into the broker's own account (ເອົາເງິນຄ່າທຳນຽມປະກັນໄພຂອງລູກຄ້າ ໄປເຂົ້າບັນຊີສ່ວນຕົວຂອງນາຍໜ້າປະກັນໄພ); Art. 12(1.12), an agent must notify the client of the insurer's acceptance or refusal within ten days (general) or thirty days (life).
S2, Section IV `[working paraphrase]`: this Instruction repeals Instruction No. 539/MOF of 19 Feb 2014, Instruction No. 770/MOF of 21 Mar 2016, Decision No. 3058/MOF of 27 Sep 2018, Decision No. 3059/MOF of 27 Sep 2018 and Decision No. 3060/MOF of 27 Sep 2018, and takes effect fifteen days after Gazette publication (31 Jan 2024).
Whether any equivalent of the S7 rule survives elsewhere is a question for the legal seat (Q6).
URLs: S7, S2.

**4.2.5 Payment rails and currency.**
S20 (Payment System Law, BOL unofficial English) Art. 29: "Payment Service Provider is financial institution authorized by the Bank of the Lao PDR to provide payment services ..."; Art. 30(7): a provider may "appoint agents of payment service to provide service on its behalf"; the following article: "Commercial banks, deposit-taking and non-deposit-taking micro-financial institutions can be Payment Service Provider without obtaining payment business license"; Art. 16: "Electronic money issued by Payment Service Providers in the Lao PDR shall be in KIP"; Art. 39: "Information, documents and/or electronic records shall be kept at least for a period of Ten years following the completion of documents or transactions or expiration of contracts."
S21 (Foreign Exchange Management Law, BOL unofficial English) Art. 11: "Services consist of tourism, education, health, insurance, transport, telecommunication, labor and other services."; Art. 12: "Resident importers-exporters of the Lao PDR shall have a specific bank account for international payment for import and export goods and services at a commercial bank, located in Lao PDR."; Art. 16: "Other external income of Individuals, legal entities and organizations from insurance, gifts and rewards shall be transferred into and out of Lao PDR through payment service providers."
URLs: S20, S21.

**4.2.6 Remittance of profits by foreign intermediaries.**
S10 (2011 law, unofficial English) Art. 92 allowed foreign insurers and brokers to "remit overseas the remaining profit and dividends after fulfilling financial obligations". S1 was read in full; the equivalent 2019 provision was not identified with confidence in the pages read, so its current form is **UNVERIFIABLE** from this pack (Q7).

**4.2.7 AML status of intermediaries.**
S22 (AML/CFT Law, BOL unofficial English) Art. 15 lists reporting entities including "money transfer service companies and agents, insurance companies, ... companies or agents selling and managing financial payment instruments"; insurance agents and brokers are not named as such. Art. 25: customer and transaction records "must be kept for at least ten years from the date of completion of the transaction or termination of the business relationship with the customer."
URL: S22.

### 4.3 Data residency, retention and disclosure for insurance records

**4.3.1 Confidentiality duties in the Insurance Law.**
S1 Art. 66(4) `[working paraphrase]`: a broker must keep confidential the secrets of the insurer and of the purchaser. S1 Art. 82(9) `[working paraphrase]`: an insurer must keep confidential the information received from the purchaser. S1 Art. 79-81 `[working paraphrase]`: "information on insurance business" means the financial, accounting and other information of insurers; the Ministry of Finance must keep confidential information that could affect an insurer's business, except disclosure to relevant authorities for supervision and enforcement, or information already public; the Ministry may exchange information with foreign supervisors under memoranda of understanding. S1 Art. 85(2) `[working paraphrase]` prohibits insurers, agents and brokers from using another person's personal information for gain. S1 Art. 78 `[working paraphrase]`: insurers must disclose financial information through their website or other channels as the Ministry prescribes.
URL: S1.

**4.3.2 Retention periods in force.**
S15 (Accounting Law 2023) Art. 50 `[working paraphrase]`: an accounting unit must keep accounting documents for at least ten years; documents tied to a contract must be kept for at least ten years after the contract ends; documents are to be filed in order and kept in a secure place within the unit; electronic accounting data must be kept in a database capable of linking to the Ministry of Finance's financial information system. Art. 51 `[working paraphrase]`: units using electronic accounting systems must keep the data in a database that is stable and secure, retrievable, and conformant with the Law on Electronic Transactions. Art. 52 `[working paraphrase]`: destruction after the retention period requires an inventory, management approval and confirmation by the relevant state authority, and a record of destruction kept for inspection. Art. 7 `[working paraphrase]`: accounting records are kept in Lao and in kip; foreign language or currency may be used with Ministry approval, but reports must be in kip and Lao.
S1 Art. 68 `[working paraphrase]`: insurers and brokers must keep financial and accounting records under the Insurance Law, the Accounting Law and related regulations. S1 Art. 77 `[working paraphrase]`: insurers and brokers report under the Accounting Law's regime and the Ministry's regulations; S2 sets quarterly reports by the 20th of January, April, July and October and annual reports by 31 March, for insurers and brokers alike.
S20 Art. 39 (payment business operators) and S22 Art. 25 (AML reporting entities): ten years each, quoted in 4.2.5 and 4.2.7.
S16 (Electronic Data Protection Law 2017) Art. 15 `[working paraphrase]`: a data custodian may keep electronic data for the period needed for the purpose of collection; after that, personal data must be deleted or access to it closed, unless the law provides otherwise; Art. 20 `[working paraphrase]`: the custodian must delete data on the owner's request or when the purpose ends, and must notify the owner.
URLs: S15, S1, S2, S20, S22, S16.

**4.3.3 Disclosure and use of personal data.**
S16 Art. 8-10 `[working paraphrase]`: electronic data is either general data (accessible and usable, subject to citing the source) or specific data, which comprises official (state) data and personal data; specific data may be accessed, used or disclosed only with the owner's or the responsible organisation's permission. Art. 16 `[working paraphrase]`: a custodian may use or disclose personal data it collected only with the owner's consent, unless the law provides otherwise, and must not disclose personal data to third parties without the owner's consent or a lawful order. Art. 17 `[working paraphrase]`: sending or transferring electronic data requires the owner's consent and assurance of the recipient's ability to protect it; important data such as financial, banking, investment and accounting data must be secured in transit; **an individual, juristic person or organisation may not send or transfer personal data or official data outside the Lao PDR without the consent of the data owner or where the law prohibits it**. Art. 25 `[working paraphrase]`: for transmission of important data such as financial, banking, investment and accounting data, the custodian must apply data-security measures and use electronic signatures certified by the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications. Art. 29-33 `[working paraphrase]`: rights and duties of custodians and prohibitions on unauthorised access, collection, use, disclosure or transfer. Art. 52 `[working paraphrase]`: breaches of the prohibitions in Art. 31-33 not amounting to a crime attract a fine of 15,000,000 kip.
URL: S16.

**4.3.4 Data residency.**
No provision requiring insurance records or personal data to be stored inside the Lao PDR was found in S1, S2, S15 or S16. S16 Art. 17 restricts cross-border transfer of personal data without the owner's consent (4.3.3). S17 (Cybersecurity Law 2025) Art. 14-15 `[working paraphrase]` define critical information infrastructure and list its sectors, including "finance and banking"; Art. 38 `[working paraphrase]` requires legal persons to back up data in cloud and/or hard-disk storage; no storage-location requirement was found in Art. 1-52; Art. 53 onward not read. Whether insurance intermediaries fall within the "finance and banking" CII sector, and whether any implementing regulation under S17 sets a location rule, is **UNVERIFIABLE** from this pack (Q10, Q11).
URLs: S16, S17.

### 4.4 Tax on premium and commission

**4.4.1 Value added tax on premium.**
S23 (VAT Law 2024) Art. 12 `[working paraphrase]`: goods and services exempt from VAT include, under item 2 (domestic supply of services), 2.12 banking and financial-institution services licensed by the Bank of the Lao PDR, and 2.14 **insurance activities in health, life, personal accident, livestock and planted trees** (ກິດຈະການປະກັນໄພ ສຸຂະພາບ, ຊີວິດ, ຄົນ, ສັດລ້ຽງ ແລະ ຕົ້ນໄມ້ປູກ). Art. 17 `[working paraphrase]`: the VAT rate is ten percent for imports, domestic supply of goods and services, and purchases of goods and services from foreign juristic persons without an establishment in the Lao PDR; zero percent for exports. Art. 15(1.7) `[working paraphrase]`: the tax base for supplies through electronic platforms is the value of the goods or services supplied. Art. 23(2) `[working paraphrase]`: input VAT credit requires, among other things, declaration through the tax authority's revenue-information system (Tax RIS) and payment through a bank or payment-service provider licensed by the Bank of the Lao PDR.
General (property, liability, motor) insurance is not named in the Art. 12 exemption list read; whether it is therefore taxable at ten percent is for the legal seat (Q12).
URL: S23.

**4.4.2 VAT on intermediary services.**
S25 (Instruction 0077/MOF 2017, unofficial English, under the 2014 VAT law), section 5.1(3): "Intermediate insurance (medium insurance) refers to services and payback by the agreement to the party as a legitimate intermediate which is an exclusive commercial relationship with third person. ... intermediate party has an authority to bind the service or sell insurance to customers, which is exempt from VAT. ... Activities consist of provide the counseling that reflects the specific expertise of insurance that is exempt from VAT. Intermediate person in Insurance does not include the following: provide a standard service such as Customer Service Center, Brand Management, onsite services or other Management Services and Advertising and Information Services." The same section lists as not exempt "Management; Legal services, accounting, audit ...; Marketing, research ...; Supplying and design software or computer programs" and states "Damage monitoring must not be regarded as a specific and necessary feature of the insurance and reinsurance."
S26 (the 2021 VAT instruction) and S23 (2024 law) post-date S25; S26 was not read. Whether S25's intermediary treatment survives is **UNVERIFIABLE** from this pack (Q13).
URLs: S25, S26, S23.

**4.4.3 Income tax on commission.**
S24 (Income Tax Law 2025) Art. 15-16 `[working paraphrase]`: profit tax is levied at twenty percent on enterprises' taxable profit (with sector-specific rates listed in Art. 16). Art. 33(2) `[working paraphrase]`: taxable income of individuals and juristic persons includes income from independent professions not registered as an enterprise, such as brokerage fees (ຄ່ານາຍໜ້າ), consultancy fees, services, advertising, sports and arts. Art. 38(5.6) `[working paraphrase]`: a rate of ten percent of gross income applies to brokerage fees, consultancy fees and other service fees. Art. 43 `[working paraphrase]`: for income not covered by Art. 39-42, the payer must calculate, withhold, declare and remit the tax within fifteen days of payment. Art. 45(3) and Art. 48(2.4) `[working paraphrase]`: for non-residents, brokerage, consultancy and service fees are taxed at ten percent of gross income. Art. 44 `[working paraphrase]`: payers and payees must use their taxpayer identification number and pay through the state treasury or the banking system.
Whether commission paid by an insurer to a licensed corporate broker is profit-tax income of the broker (Art. 15) or withholding income under Art. 38(5.6), and how commission paid to individual agents is treated, is for the legal seat (Q14).
URL: S24.

**4.4.4 Tax exemptions written into the insurance law.**
S10 (2011 law, unofficial English) Art. 58: "Personal insurance is exempted from taxes as follows: 1. Value-added tax on a life insurance and health insurance contract; 2. Income tax on death or permanent disability benefits ...; 3. Income tax on health insurance and body accident insurance annuities; 4. Income tax on benefits received from endowment insurance." No equivalent article was found in S1 (2019 law); S1 Art. 82 `[working paraphrase]` lists the insurer's duty to pay taxes and other obligations to the State. The exemptions now rest, if anywhere, on S23 Art. 12 and S24 Art. 34 (income exempt from income tax; not analysed for insurance benefits).
URLs: S10, S1, S23, S24.

**4.4.5 Supervisory fees.**
S2 `[working paraphrase]`: licence fees of 50,000,000 kip for a life or general insurer and 100,000,000 kip for a reinsurer, an annual fee of 5,000,000 kip, and an annual supervisory service fee of 0.3 percent of gross written premium payable by 20 January; broker and agent fees as in 4.1.4 and 4.1.5. These are fees, not taxes; recorded because they attach to premium volume.
URL: S2.

## 5. Questions the legal seat must still answer

1. Is Law No. 78/NA of 29 November 2019 (S1) the insurance law currently in force, and has any later amendment or replacement been adopted that is not yet on the Gazette? (Section 3.5 found none.)
2. Under S1 Art. 60-63 and S2, which of BizTrust's intended activities fall within "insurance agent" and which within "insurance broker" (S1 Art. 64-67), and can a single juristic person hold both an agent card and a broker licence?
3. S1 Art. 64 defines a broker as a juristic person. Does the law leave any room for an individual to act as broker, and what is the status of a technology platform that neither issues contracts nor advises but routes applications and premium?
4. Does the "one insurer per class" rule for juristic agents in S2 apply to a platform operator, and what exactly is the scope of the bank-and-financial-institution exception?
5. What does "within the time set" in S1 Art. 62(6) mean in practice: the agency contract, an MOF regulation, or the repealed S7 timetable?
6. Decision 3058/MOF (S7) required immediate transfer of premium to the insurer's account and prohibited brokers from placing client premium in their own account. S2 Section IV lists S7 as repealed. Is there any instrument now in force that imposes segregation, a client or trust account, or a remittance deadline on agents or brokers, including S11(a)/(b), which could not be downloaded?
7. What does S1 provide on remittance of profits and dividends by foreign-owned intermediaries, in place of the 2011 law's Art. 92? (The researcher did not identify the provision with confidence.)
8. Does S1 Art. 22 and Civil Code Art. 465 prevent a Lao-established intermediary from placing risks with an insurer outside the Lao PDR, and how does this interact with reinsurance under S1 Art. 20?
9. Under S21 Art. 12 and 16, must premium collected in the Lao PDR be held in a kip account at a Lao commercial bank, and may a broker's cross-border commission or fee be paid in foreign currency?
10. Do insurance intermediaries fall within the "finance and banking" critical-information-infrastructure sector under S17 Art. 14-15, and does any implementing regulation under S17 (Art. 53 onward were not read) set a storage-location or licensing requirement for their systems?
11. Does S16 Art. 17 (no transfer of personal data outside the Lao PDR without the data owner's consent) permit hosting policyholder data on foreign cloud infrastructure with contractual consent, and what form must that consent take?
12. Is general (property, liability, motor) insurance premium subject to VAT at ten percent under S23 Art. 17, given that S23 Art. 12(2.14) exempts only health, life, personal-accident, livestock and planted-tree insurance?
13. Does the VAT treatment of insurance intermediation in Instruction 0077/MOF 2017 (S25) survive the 2021 instruction (S26) and the 2024 law (S23), and does it extend to a platform that "has authority to bind ... or sell insurance"?
14. Is commission received by a licensed corporate broker profit-tax income (S24 Art. 15-16), or withholding income at ten percent (S24 Art. 38(5.6), Art. 43), and how is commission paid to individual agents treated?
15. Are agents and brokers "reporting entities" under S22 Art. 15 by designation of the National Coordination Committee, even though the article names only "insurance companies" and "agents selling and managing financial payment instruments"?
16. Does the Lao Services Portal "Ministerial Guidelines on Insurance Business Licensing" (S13, unreachable) correspond to the repealed Instruction 770/MOF (S8), and is any English text of S1 or S2 available from the Ministry on request?
17. Do S11(a) (reserves and solvency) and S11(b) (data reporting by insurers and brokers) remain in force after S2, and what do they require of brokers?

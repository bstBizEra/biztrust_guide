## Scope and non-scope

**In scope.** The record every authorization decision produces, and the controls that make it evidence rather than a log line:

1. **The record's fields**: the evidence contract's twenty, ARCH-001 section 15's additions, and the two the research adds, integrity evidence and retention class.
2. **What is never in it**: raw tokens, secrets, request or response bodies, unnecessary personal data, and anything about a tenant other than the record's own.
3. **How it is written**: by the audit module's contract only, in the transaction of the decision it records, so that a decision without a record does not exist.
4. **How it is protected from ordinary mutation**: the grants, the policies, the migration lint and a hash chain, so that no runtime role can change or delete a record and a change by anyone else is detected.
5. **Who may read it**: the tenant's own auditor for the tenant's records, under row security; the platform auditor across tenants only through an elevation that is itself recorded.
6. **The proof that every allow and every deny produces one**: the matrix runner's rule that a row without its record has not passed, and a decision count that must equal a record count.
7. **The reason-code registry**: one list, owned here, that the P0.3, P0.4, P0.7 and P0.5 designs' codes join.

**Non-scope**, each with its owner:

- Traces, logs and metrics, and the request's reconstruction through them: P0.11 ([#146](https://github.com/bstBizEra/biztrust_guide/issues/146)); this design fixes only that the record carries the trace and request identifiers so that telemetry is found from the record and never the reverse, as the research's section 7.2 reads: the record "must carry the `trace-id` so that the observability artefacts can be found from it, not the other way round".
- Retention periods, legal hold, residency and disclosure: ADR-019; this design reserves the columns and fixes that nothing in P0 deletes a record.
- The audit viewer, the screen through which a human reads evidence: P0.13 ([#185](https://github.com/bstBizEra/biztrust_guide/issues/185)); this design fixes the read contract it calls.
- The business-authority fields' content: ADR-013 and slice S01 ([#93](https://github.com/bstBizEra/biztrust_guide/issues/93)); in P0 the three fields exist and are null, because no authoritative act exists.
- Domain events, the completed facts a command publishes: ARCH-001 section 11's event envelope and its research; an audit record is not an event and is not published.
- Database-side logging and pgAudit: corroboration the research says "cannot replace" a record; at `0.2` the P0.11 design has settled what runs, `application_name` with `log_line_prefix` and no statement logging or pgAudit, and what more might is P3's (question 4).
- The security proof's matrix, its verifier and its evidence binding: `P0.SECURITY-PROOF.md` ([#148](https://github.com/bstBizEra/biztrust_guide/issues/148)); this design supplies its audit column.

## Authorization-sequence step

P0.10 is the sequence's audit participant, `Audit evidence`, and the three messages it receives in the tenant authorization sequence of [`FLOWS.md`](../FLOWS.md) section 3, and the third column of its minimum negative proof matrix, which every row of every P0 design's controls feeds.

| Sequence step | What P0.10 does there |
|---|---|
| Record denied decision | The P0.3 chain's and the P0.4 resolver's refusals write a record with their reason code, the request and trace identifiers, the route, and the subject where a check established one; a refusal before any tenant is known writes to the platform-level table, a refusal after one to the tenant's own |
| Record isolation denial | The P0.7 design's data-access layer maps a policy error to the one denial and writes a record naming the policy's reason code; the business write is rolled back to a savepoint taken before it and the record is written after, in the same transaction (below) |
| Record action and outcome | Every allowed command writes its record in its own transaction before commit, with the outcome, the state references before and after, and the authority under which it was permitted; a command whose record cannot be written does not commit |

The matrix's audit column is the design's acceptance: "Authentication failure recorded", "Safe reason code recorded", "Audience failure recorded", "Context failure recorded", "Membership result recorded", "Mismatch recorded without data leakage", "Tampering outcome recorded", "Database denial linked to test". The manual's rule for this epic is that the proof *is* audit evidence: "every denial in the matrix must produce an attributable record, or the matrix passed silently".

## Interfaces and data shapes

### The record

Two tables in the audit module's schema, `audit`, both built on the P0.6 design's template and written through one contract. `audit.decision` is tenant-owned: `tenant_id` `NOT NULL`, enabled, forced and policed like every tenant-owned table, so that a tenant's records are visible under that tenant's key and no other. `audit.platform_event` is registered platform-level, for what happens before a tenant is known or above every tenant: a refused token, a provisioning run, an elevation, a dump by `backup_role`, which the backup job of the P0.7 design's P0.7-B records through the same contract before and after the dump, with the dump's identity, since the job and not the database is what can call it.

The fields, with their source; the evidence contract's twenty are kept under the contract's names, so that the baseline and this design read the same:

| Field | Source | Content and rule |
|---|---|---|
| `decision_id` | Contract | The record's identifier, a uuid, the template's `id` |
| `occurred_at` | Contract; AU-8 | When the decision was made, set by the application from its own clock, in UTC as AU-8 asks; the template's `recorded_at` is the record time, set by the database from its clock, and the difference between the two is control 10's measurement |
| `actor_id`, `actor_type` | Contract; AU-3 (f) | The resolved subject from the P0.3 chain, a user or an application identifier, never a name, email or free string; `actor_type` one of `user`, `application`, `platform`, `system` |
| `tenant_id`, `membership_id` | Contract | The mapped tenant of the P0.4 design and the membership row the resolver found; on `audit.platform_event` both are null, since there is none |
| `legal_entity_id` | ARCH-001 section 15 "tenant and legal entity" | The acting legal entity where the resource has one, the P0.6 design's baseline column; null otherwise |
| `organization_id`, `requested_tenant_id`, `route` | The P0.4 and P0.3 designs' hand-offs | The token's organization identifier, the tenant the route's segment named, and the contract-declared route template, never the raw path or query; these are the request's own context, what the caller sent, and are how a mismatch or a tampering observation is evidenced under the caller's own tenant |

**What is never in it.** No column holds a token, a secret, a request or response body, a header value, a query string, a person's name, email, address or document, or any data of a tenant other than the record's own; the identifiers the request itself carried, its organization and the tenant its route named, are recorded as the request's context, since the caller sent them and a mismatch cannot be evidenced without them. The contract's own sentence is the rule: "Do not record raw tokens, secrets or unnecessary personal data." The writer enforces it twice: the contract's type admits identifiers and codes and no free-text field, and a scrubber refuses a record whose any field matches the patterns the P0.12 design's log scan uses for the inventory's names, or a token shape, three base64url segments joined by periods, the compact serialization the P0.3 design's chain validates, which this design adds for the audit tables (control 7); at `0.2` the P0.9 design's outbox writer is a second caller of the same patterns and refuses a row that matches, as that design's control 7 has it, so that an event a scrubber would catch is never written and the command fails (the P0.9 design's question 12). A P0.4 tampering observation records that a header or parameter differed and which one, never the value it carried.

### The write contract

The audit module's public contract, the only path to either table, as the P0.2 design's boundary rule requires; a module that imports the audit schema or issues an insert against it fails the boundary check.

```text
record(decision: DecisionRecord) -> Receipt { decision_id, record_hash }
```

- **In the decision's transaction.** For an allowed command, the call runs on the command's own connection inside its transaction, before commit; if the insert fails, the transaction fails, and the command reports an error rather than a result (control 3). This is the research's reading of the standards: an audit record "must be written by the application at the point of the state change, on the same transaction as the business write where the store allows it".
- **For a denial with no business transaction**, the identity-access module's pipeline opens a transaction for the record alone. When the P0.4 resolver has mapped the caller's own tenant, the key is set to that tenant, never to the one the request named, and the record goes to `audit.decision`; when no tenant is known, to `audit.platform_event`.
- **For an isolation denial**, the P0.7 design's data-access layer catches the policy error inside the transaction; the record is written after a rollback to a savepoint taken before the business statement, so the business write is undone and the record is kept, then the transaction commits with the record alone. The savepoint is a duty this design adds to that layer and asks the P0.7 design's next version to carry (question 7).
- **Fail closed.** A decision whose record cannot be written is answered as an error, not as its outcome, and raises the alert the baseline's audit layer names, "Alert if mandatory evidence cannot be written safely". An allow never proceeds unrecorded; a deny is still a deny, but the caller sees an error and the alert fires, so that a silent audit outage cannot hide behind ordinary refusals. The error is not one of the P0.4 design's denials and carries none of their code, so that design's rule that the three denials share one status, shape and code is untouched by it; this design asks that design's next version to name the error class its rule does not govern (question 7).
- **The roles.** The allowed command's record is inserted by the role the command runs as, `app_role`, which holds `INSERT` on the two audit tables for that purpose and `SELECT` on them for the read contract below, and no `UPDATE`, `DELETE`, `TRUNCATE` or `REFERENCES` on them, an addition to the P0.6 design's grants table and to the P0.12 design's roles table that this design asks their next versions to carry; the denial path's record is inserted by `audit_writer`, the P0.6 design's insert-only role, on its own connection. Neither role, and no other runtime role, may change or delete a row.

### The reason-code registry

One file in the audit module, `modules/audit/reason-codes.yaml`, lists every code a record may carry, with the design that owns it and the matrix row it evidences. The initial entries are the codes the landed designs already name: the P0.3 chain's failure codes; the P0.4 resolver's `tenant_unknown`, `tenant_disabled`, `tenant_mismatch`, `membership_inactive` and its tampering observation; the P0.7 design's policy-error code, one for every `WITH CHECK` and policy error the data-access layer maps; the P0.5 design's refusals, a run whose immutable inputs differ, a second concurrent run, a mapping to a missing organization; at `0.2` the seven the P0.8 design introduces, with that design as their owner, `idempotency_key_missing`, `idempotency_key_reused`, `request_in_flight`, `version_mismatch`, `precondition_missing`, `validation_failed` and `replayed`, each with that design's own text as its meaning (the P0.8 design's question 11); and `allowed`. A code the registry does not list is refused by the writer and by a lint over the code (control 8), so that a new denial cannot be introduced without a row in the evidence.

### Protection from ordinary mutation

Four layers, each with its check, so that "ordinary mutation" has no path and extraordinary mutation is detected:

| Layer | Rule | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Grants | No runtime role holds `UPDATE`, `DELETE`, `TRUNCATE` or `REFERENCES` on an audit table; `app_role` holds `SELECT` and `INSERT`, `audit_writer` holds `INSERT`; the tenant's auditor reads through the read contract as `app_role` under row security | A new ownership test, T10, numbered after the P0.7 design's T9 and asked of the P0.6 design's next version to list: the audit schema's grants as listed |
| Policies | `audit.decision` carries the P0.7 design's select and insert policies and no update or delete policy at all, a second exception to that design's four policies per tenant-owned table beside `tenancy.tenant`, which this design asks the P0.7 design's next version to name (question 7), so that even a role granted `UPDATE` by mistake would find no permissive policy and be denied, "If there are no `PERMISSIVE` policies, then access is denied" (the P0.7 design, quoting the RLS research's F4) | The P0.6 design's T3, which asserts a policy's presence per granted command, extended for the audit tables to assert the absence of an update and a delete policy, an extension asked of that design's next version (question 7). `audit.platform_event` carries a select policy `USING (true)` and an insert policy `WITH CHECK (true)`, the shape the P0.7 design gives its platform-level exceptions, and likewise no update or delete policy |
| Migrations | The migration lint of the P0.2 design, which that design has parse `CREATE`, `ALTER`, `DROP` and `REFERENCES`, gains rules for the audit schema that this design asks its next version to carry: refuse any `UPDATE`, `DELETE`, `TRUNCATE`, `DROP`, column drop or type change on an audit table; a migration may add a column and nothing else | The lint, seen failing (control 4) |
| The chain | Each record's `record_hash` is a digest over the record's canonical fields and the `previous_hash` of the last record in the same table for the same tenant, computed by the writer inside the insert transaction with the algorithm question 2 assumes, since no ADR row names one, under a lock per tenant and table the writer takes before reading the chain's head, so that two concurrent inserts cannot read the same `previous_hash`; the chain's head per tenant and table is written daily to the evidence store as an anchor by a job running as a system identity, `actor_type = system`, whose anchors the platform auditor verifies without writing. The research reads SP 800-92's integrity control as "calculating a message digest for each file and storing the message digest securely to ensure that changes to archived logs are detected" | A verifier that walks each chain against its anchors and names the first record that does not hash (control 5) |

What the chain does not give: non-repudiation in AU-10's sense, "irrefutable evidence that an individual ... has performed" an action, which the research reads as requiring "digital signatures and digital message receipts". A signature over the anchor, and the key that would sign it, have no ADR row and are a question (question 2), not a candidate.

### The proof that every decision produces one

The manual's sentence is the test's rule: a matrix row that produced no record has not passed. Two mechanisms, both run by the security proof's runner:

1. **Per row, the record.** Every control of the P0.3, P0.4, P0.7 and P0.5 designs and every matrix row ends by reading back the record whose `request_id` is the control's request: through the read contract under the row's own tenant key for `audit.decision`, and under a test-held elevation of the security test package, the shape question 8 assumes, for `audit.platform_event`. The runner asserts the outcome, the reason code, the actor and the tenant the row expects, and fails the row if no record exists or if the record holds anything the row's expectation forbids, such as another tenant's data. This is the matrix's third column made mechanical.
2. **Across the run, the count.** The pipeline counts every decision attempt by the outcome it answered, `allow`, `deny` or `error`, in a counter the test reads; the read-back's own reads are attempts too, since the read contract is called through the pipeline. At `0.2` that counter is also exported as the P0.11 design's `biztrust.decision.count` metric, with the outcome and, on a deny, the caller-facing code as its attributes; the proof reads the counter itself, so its acceptance does not wait on the telemetry (the P0.11 design's question 9). The run's records are counted from both tables by outcome. Attempts answered `allow` or `deny` equal records with those outcomes, and attempts answered `error` equal the alerts raised, or the run fails. A decision path that forgot its record shows as a difference, whichever path it is (control 2).

## Negative controls

## Evidence contract

A control run records, per [`AGENTS.md`](../../../AGENTS.md) section 9; a record missing a field is not evidence:

| Field | Content for P0.10 |
|---|---|
| Repository and commit SHA | The platform repository and the commit whose audit module, registry, lint and tests ran; the same value the run's records carry as `source_revision` |
| Command or workflow identity | The workflow file and run identifier for the security test job, and the verifier job's identity for control 5 |
| Execution time and environment | Start and end, environment name, runtime versions, PostgreSQL version, tool versions, the clock source and the measured drift of control 10 |
| Exit status | Per control, and for the count comparison of the proof |
| Artifact or output reference | For each control the request, the response's problem details, the records read back with their hashes, the counter and the record counts, the lint's output for controls 4 and 8, the verifier's report for control 5 with the anchor it checked against, the scan of the audit tables for control 7 |
| Verifier role | The named role that ran or observed each control, not the implementer; the security risk owner, whom the manual names for `BT-G1` (label B), for the matrix's audit column |

Four things are not candidates. A separate audit sink or write-once store outside PostgreSQL: none has an ADR row and none was researched, so it is a question (question 3). pgAudit and PostgreSQL statement logging: the research records that pgAudit "is best-effort and not transactional" and "It is not possible to reliably audit superusers with pgAudit", so they corroborate and "cannot replace" a record; whether they run was P0.11's and P3's; at `0.2` the P0.11 design has answered it, neither is adopted, and only what more might run beside the record waits on P3 (question 4). ## Open questions and dependencies

1. **ADR-019 decides retention, legal hold, residency and disclosure.** Waits on: slice S01 ([#93](https://github.com/bstBizEra/biztrust_guide/issues/93)) and the register's row, `BLOCKED_BY_S01`. `ASSUMED` meanwhile: `retention_class = default`, no deletion in P0, `legal_hold` never set, and every record kept for the platform's life until the ADR says otherwise.
2. **The integrity mechanism.** A digest algorithm for the chain, and whether the anchors are signed, have no ADR row; the register needs one, which the contract map must add. Waits on: the accountable architecture owner adding the row on map #91. `ASSUMED` meanwhile: SHA-256 over a canonical serialization, inserts serialized per tenant and table by a row lock on a chain-head row the writer keeps, the anchor unsigned and stored in the evidence store under AGENTS.md section 9, and AU-10's non-repudiation recorded as not met by P0.
3. **A store outside PostgreSQL.** A write-once or external sink would give AU-9 protection against the owner role that the chain only detects. Waits on: an ADR row, which none exists for, and ADR-019's residency answer. `ASSUMED` meanwhile: none; the chain, the anchors and the grants are P0's whole protection, and the declared non-coverage says so.
4. **Database-side corroboration.** Answered at `0.2` by the P0.11 design: the API sets `application_name` per transaction to the service and the request identifier, and the server's `log_line_prefix` carries `%a`, so a statement that fails writes a line naming the request; statement logging stays off, since logged statements may reveal sensitive data, so a successful statement writes no line, and pgAudit is not adopted. Waits on: P3's operations epic for whether anything more runs beside the record. `ASSUMED` meanwhile: the corroboration is of errors, and the record still stands alone as the evidence.
5. **The state references.** `prior_state_ref` and `resulting_state_ref` name an aggregate's version; how a version is identified is ADR-015's. Waits on: ADR-015 and the bitemporal research it reads. `ASSUMED` meanwhile: `aggregate_id` and `aggregate_version` from the P0.6 design's template.
6. **The business-authority fields.** Null in P0. Waits on: ADR-013 and slice S01. `ASSUMED` meanwhile: the columns exist and every P0 record leaves them null, which the read-back asserts.
7. **The asks on five landed designs.** The same-transaction rule needs the command's own role to insert the record; the audit tables carry two policies, not four; the fail-closed error is a class no denial rule governs. Waits on: the P0.6 design's next version, asked to add `SELECT` and `INSERT` on the audit tables, and nothing else on them, to `app_role`'s grants, to extend T3 for the audit tables and to list T10; the P0.12 design's next version, asked to say the same of `app_role` in its roles table; the P0.7 design's next version, asked to name the audit tables as the second exception to its four policies and the savepoint as the data-access layer's duty; the P0.2 design's next version, asked to carry the migration lint's audit rules; the P0.4 design's next version, asked to name the fail-closed error as the class its one-denial rule does not govern. `ASSUMED` meanwhile: all four; control 4 proves what the roles cannot do.
8. **The platform auditor's elevation.** The baseline gives the seat "Independent evidence access" and "No mutation" and names no mechanism; this design models the elevation on the one the baseline requires for support access, "separately authorized, expiring and fully audited". Waits on: the P0.13 design ([#185](https://github.com/bstBizEra/biztrust_guide/issues/185)) for the surface and ADR-013's seat answers for who grants it. `ASSUMED` meanwhile: a platform-level grant row with an expiry, written to `audit.platform_event`, under which the audit module alone reads across tenants one tenant per transaction with the key set; the security test package holds a test-only grant of the same shape for the read-back.
9. **The registry's owner.** Waits on: the seat proposals ([#94](https://github.com/bstBizEra/biztrust_guide/issues/94)) for who reviews a new code. `ASSUMED` meanwhile: the security reviewer role reviews every addition, since each is a new denial in the matrix.
10. **Clock synchronization.** AU-8 asks for time stamps in UTC or with a recorded offset, and SP 800-92, as the research reads it, for clocks kept consistent; the platform's time source is an operations matter. Waits on: P3's operations epic. `ASSUMED` meanwhile: the database's clock is the record time, the application's the occurrence time, and control 10 measures their difference.

No item is `UNKNOWN_BLOCKING`: the design proceeds on its assumptions and each is reversible by the decision it names.

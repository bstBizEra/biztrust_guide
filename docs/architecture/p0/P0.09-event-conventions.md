## Scope and non-scope

**In scope.** The shape every domain event takes, where it is written before it is sent, and the check that refuses one that departs from it:

1. **The envelope**: CloudEvents' core attributes, the documented extensions the research maps ARCH-001 section 12 onto, and the four custom extensions BizTrust defines and versions itself, with the rename every ARCH-001 field undergoes at the envelope boundary.
2. **What rides in the envelope and what rides in `data`**: identifiers in context attributes, documents and personal data in `data`, as the specification's privacy rule and the primer's placement rule read.
3. **The AsyncAPI document and its lint**: the version pinned, the layout under `events/`, the rules the official parser checks, the second gate for CloudEvents conformance the research says the parser lacks, and the fixtures that prove the step can fail.
4. **The outbox rule**: no event leaves without its transaction, as a tenant-owned table the command writes in the same transaction as its state change, and the inbox that deduplicates on the consumer's side.
5. **Compatibility**: how an event type is versioned, what may change within a version, and how a consumer treats an attribute it does not know.
6. **What an event is not**: a command, an audit record, or a grant of authority, as ARCH-001 section 12 and the P0.10 design fix.

**Non-scope**, each with its owner:

- The transport, Kafka, NATS JetStream or another: no ADR row names one; ARCH-001 section 12 marks messaging technologies `IMPLEMENTATION_CANDIDATE` for spikes, so the transport is a question (question 3) and this design binds the envelope to none; the outbox is what P0 has.
- Durable workflows and the runtime that replays them: ADR-010 ([#109](https://github.com/bstBizEra/biztrust_guide/issues/109)); this design fixes only that a workflow's hop carries the causation chain unbroken.
- Events that cross to an external provider and webhooks that arrive from one: ADR-009 ([#108](https://github.com/bstBizEra/biztrust_guide/issues/108)) and P2's integration designs; the envelope is the same, the adapter's translation is theirs.
- The HTTP conventions, the request identifier and `traceparent` on a request: the P0.8 design; this design fixes what of them an event carries.
- The audit record: the P0.10 design; an event is not one, and no event is written to the audit tables.
- The business events themselves, `policy.bound` and the rest of the domain's completed facts: P1's designs; P0 declares only the tenancy and identity events the landed designs imply, a tenant provisioned, suspended or closed, a membership changed.
- Cryptographic verifiability of an event, the research's `dssematerial` extension: a v0.1 draft with no ADR row; a question (question 8).

## Authorization-sequence step

P0.9 implements no step of the tenant authorization sequence in [`FLOWS.md`](../FLOWS.md) section 3; it gives the sequence's "Record action and outcome" its published counterpart, and it is the whole of section 15, "Event publication and consumption", whose sequence this design makes concrete.

| Sequence step (section 15) | What P0.9 does there |
|---|---|
| Commit state transition; store event in same transaction | The owning module's command writes the event's envelope and `data` to the outbox table in the transaction of the state change, under the tenant key, as the fourth thing the P0.10 design's "Record action and outcome" step does after the audit record; a command that cannot write its outbox row does not commit |
| Publish completed fact | A relay reads the outbox in order and hands the event to whatever transport the platform has, marking the row sent; in P0, with no transport chosen, the relay's consumer is the in-process inbox of the consuming module, and the outbox is still the only path |
| Claim event identity; already processed or new | The consumer's inbox table takes the event's `id` and `source` as its key in the consumer's transaction; a claim that already exists is acknowledged and applied nothing; a new one is applied and its outcome recorded, "Consumers must deduplicate by stable event identity and retain processing outcomes" (ARCH-001 section 12) |

The manual's note is the design's frame: the contract conventions are "Independent of the security chain", and their proof is a broken contract failing CI. The sequence's own caveat is the design's too: the outbox and inbox are "not a guarantee of exactly-once execution. Business idempotency and reconciliation remain mandatory."

## Interfaces and data shapes

### The envelope

A domain event is a CloudEvent in the JSON format, `specversion` `1.0`, since producers "MUST use a value of `1.0`" for the specification version the research read (S1). ARCH-001 section 12's minimum metadata maps onto it as the research's mapping table reads, with every name renamed at the boundary because attribute names "MUST consist of lower-case letters [a-z] or digits [0-9] from the ASCII character set" and "SHOULD NOT exceed 20 characters" (S1), so that `tenant_id` becomes `tenantid` and `correlation_id` becomes `correlationid`; the platform's code keeps ARCH-001's names and the envelope serializer does the rename in one place.

| ARCH-001 field | Attribute | Standing | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| `event_id` | `id` | Core, REQUIRED | A UUID version 7, the P0.8 design's identifier; "MUST be unique within the scope of the producer" (S1), and with `source` it is the inbox's key and ADR-012's event identity |
| `producer` | `source` | Core, REQUIRED | An absolute URI naming the owning module, `urn:biztrust:module:<registry name>`, the P0.2 design's registry name; "An absolute URI is RECOMMENDED" (S1) |
| `event_type` | `type` | Core, REQUIRED | Reverse-DNS, `io.biztrust.<module>.<fact>`, "SHOULD be prefixed with a reverse-DNS name" (S1); a completed fact in the past tense, never a request |
| `event_version` | `dataschema` | Core, OPTIONAL, made required by the lint | A versioned URI, `https://biztrust.example/events/<type>/v<major>`, the one of the research's three options this design takes, so that the version is the schema's and a consumer resolves what it validates against; no version suffix in `type` and no custom extension for it |
| `occurred_at` | `time` | Core, OPTIONAL, made required by the lint | RFC 3339, "Timestamp of when the occurrence happened" (S1); the P0.6 design's occurrence side |
| `recorded_at` | `recordedtime` | Documented extension (S12) | "when the CloudEvent was created by a producer"; the P0.6 design's `recorded_at`, set from the outbox row's record time |
| `subject_type`, `subject_id` | `subject` | Core, OPTIONAL, made required by the lint | One string, `<aggregate type>/<aggregate id>`, the convention the research reads as supporting the specification's "string-suffix filter" (S1) |
| `tenant_id` | `tenantid` | Custom extension, BizTrust's | The tenant's identifier, opaque, a String; required on every event of a tenant-owned aggregate; absent on a platform-level event, which P0 has one of, the provisioning run's outcome |
| `correlation_id` | `correlationid` | Documented extension (S11) | "An identifier that groups related events within the same logical flow or business transaction."; the P0.10 record's `correlation_id`, the request identifier of the request that began the flow |
| `causation_id` | `causationid` | Documented extension (S11) | "The unique identifier of the event that directly caused this event to be generated."; for the first event of a flow, the request identifier |
| `traceparent` | `traceparent`, `tracestate` | Documented extension (S5) | The W3C values of the starting trace, since the extension "MUST carry the trace information of the starting trace of the transmission" and "MUST NOT carry trace information of each individual hop" (S5); the P0.8 design's `traceparent` as the request continued it |
| `authority_reference` | `authorityref` | Custom extension, BizTrust's | A URI-reference to an accepted authority profile where the fact asserts delegated or external authority, which no P0 event does; the research records that the documented `authcontext` extension carries the principal, not the authority, so the principal rides as `authtype` and `authid` (S10) and the authority as this extension |
| the principal | `authtype`, `authid` | Documented extension (S10) | `authtype` one of the extension's enumerated values, `user`, `service_account` or `system` for the platform's actors; `authid` the resolved actor identifier, never a name, since the extension says to avoid personal data; `authclaims` never used |
| `effective_at_or_period` | in `data` | ARCH-001; the primer's placement rule (S8) | Valid time is business data the transport does not route on, so it stays in `data` as the P0.6 design's `effective_from`, `effective_to` and `effective_timezone`; no custom extension |
| order | `sequence` | Documented extension (S13) | The outbox row's sequence within `source`, "a non-empty lexicographically-orderable string" (S13), monotonic per producer |
| partition | `partitionkey` | Documented extension (S6) | The tenant identifier, so that a transport that maps it to a partition keeps a tenant's events ordered; the research records that the Kafka binding "SHOULD map `partitionkey` to the record key" (S4) |
| `data` | `data`, `datacontenttype` | Core | `application/json`; the fact's own fields, validated against `dataschema`; the state references the P0.10 design uses, `aggregate_id` and `aggregate_version` before and after, and never a document, a token or a person's data beyond identifiers |

Two custom extensions carry a version of their own, `tenantid` and `authorityref`, declared in `events/extensions.yaml` with their type, meaning and the date they were defined, since the research's second decision-relevant fact is that the documented extensions "might be changed, or removed, at any time" and the custom ones the platform "must define and version" itself. The count of context attributes on a full event is the research's observation, "roughly fifteen", and the rule that follows is the research's third fact: every attribute is an identifier or a timestamp, never a document, so that binary-mode headers stay far below the limits the primer warns of.

**What never rides in a context attribute.** The specification's privacy rule is the design's: "Sensitive information SHOULD NOT be carried or represented in context attributes" (S1). No attribute holds a name, an email, a document, a claim set, a token or any value of another tenant; `authclaims` is never set; a fact's business content is in `data`, and a field the P0.12 design's inventory names is refused by the same scrubber the P0.10 design gives the audit writer (control 7).

### The outbox and the inbox

Two tables per module that publishes or consumes, built on the P0.6 design's template and carrying the P0.7 design's policies:

| Table | Columns beyond the baseline | Written by | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| `<module>.outbox` | `envelope` (the context attributes as JSON), `data`, `sequence`, `sent_at` (null until relayed), `attempts` | The owning module's command, as `app_role`, in the transaction of the state change, under the tenant key | "Store event in same transaction" (FLOWS section 15): the insert is the last statement before commit, so that a state change without its event, or an event without its state change, cannot exist; a platform-level event goes to a platform-level outbox the P0.6 design's next version lists |
| `<module>.inbox` | `event_id`, `source` (together the key), `received_at`, `outcome` (`applied`, `duplicate`, `failed`), `outcome_at` | The consuming module's handler, as `app_role`, in the transaction of its own transition | "Claim event identity" first, then apply, then "Record outcome" (FLOWS section 15); a second delivery of the same `id` and `source` finds the claim and is acknowledged with nothing applied; the outcome is kept for the retention ADR-019 decides, never deleted in P0 |

The relay reads unsent outbox rows in `sequence` order per source, delivers, and sets `sent_at`; it is a job of the publishing module, runs as a system identity with the key set per tenant as the P0.7 design's pattern for a path that touches every tenant, and retries with `attempts` counted; an outbox backlog beyond a threshold the operations epic sets raises the alert FLOWS section 15's table names, "Retain outbox and alert on backlog", and never drops a row. In P0 the transport is absent, so the relay delivers in-process to the consuming module's inbox; the tables, the ordering and the deduplication are the same as they will be with a transport, which is the point of fixing them now.

### The AsyncAPI document and its lint

The document is AsyncAPI, version `3.1.0`, the version ARCH-001 section 11 names and the version the specification's site shows as latest on the date checked [A1]; the research read the 3.0.0 text and its changes from 2, which 3.1.0 carries, and whether 3.1.0 changes anything this design relies on is `UNVERIFIED` (question 9). The layout, under the `events/` directory the P0.2 design reserves:

```text
events/
├── biztrust-events.yaml            the one AsyncAPI document: servers, channels, operations
├── extensions.yaml                 the custom extensions, tenantid and authorityref, with type, meaning and date
├── schemas/
│   ├── envelope.json               the CloudEvents JSON Schema the research names, extended with the platform's attributes
│   └── <module>/<type>/v<major>.json   one data schema per event type and major version, the dataschema URI's target
└── fixtures/                       the deliberately broken documents and envelopes, one per lint rule
```

Each event type is a Message Object whose `headers` schema is the envelope's context attributes and whose `payload` is the type's data schema, since the Message Object's `headers` field is the "Schema definition of the application headers" [A1]; whether CloudEvents' binary-mode headers count as application headers or the protocol headers the field "MUST NOT define" is the interpretation the research says the document has to make, and this design makes it: the context attributes are application headers, because the platform defines them and the transport does not. Operations are `send` for the owning module and `receive` for each consumer, on a channel per event type whose `address` is the type's name; the channel binds to no transport in P0.

The lint runs two gates, since the research's fourth decision-relevant fact is that the official rules "validate document structure, references and channel/operation consistency" and "do not validate CloudEvents conformance":

| # | Rule | Gate | What it refuses |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | `asyncapi validate --fail-severity error` passes with the official parser's core and 3.x rules, and the recommended rules `asyncapi-id`, `asyncapi-defaultContentType` and `asyncapi-servers` raised to errors | The parser | A document the specification's schema or the 3.x consistency rules reject; a document without an identifier or a default content type |
| E2 | Every message's example envelope validates against `schemas/envelope.json`, which extends the CloudEvents JSON Schema the research names (S2) with the attributes above, `time`, `subject` and `dataschema` required, `tenantid` required unless the message is marked platform-level | The second gate, a script | An envelope the CloudEvents format or the platform's profile rejects; the research records that whether the parser validates examples for 3.x is `UNVERIFIED`, so this gate does not rely on it |
| E3 | Every `type` matches `io.biztrust.<module>.<fact>` with `<module>` in the P0.2 design's registry, and every `source` is that module's URN | The second gate | An event owned by no module, or by a module other than its source |
| E4 | Every `dataschema` URI resolves to a file under `schemas/` and the message's `payload` is that file | The second gate | A version the repository does not hold |
| E5 | No attribute name outside the core set, the documented extensions the table above lists, and `extensions.yaml` | The second gate | An unregistered extension |
| E6 | No context attribute's schema admits a value longer than the length `extensions.yaml` fixes, and no attribute is an object or an array | The second gate | A document or a structure in a header |
| E7 | Every example's `data` validates against its `dataschema`, and no `data` schema declares a property named `tenant_id`, `token`, `secret` or any of the P0.12 design's inventory names | The second gate | A fact that carries its tenant twice or a secret |
| E8 | A changed schema under `schemas/<module>/<type>/v<major>.json` is additive against the base branch, a new optional field or a new enum value the schema marks extensible; a removed or retyped field fails | The second gate, a JSON Schema diff against the base | A breaking change within a major version |

The CI step is the P0.8 design's "Contract lint" job, which this design asks to run the two gates over `events/` beside the OpenAPI rules, so that the P0.2 design's next version carries one job for both contracts; and one more run over `events/fixtures/`, expecting a failure per fixture, so that a green run on the fixtures is itself a failure (control 1). The manual's row is the step's acceptance: "Linted AsyncAPI and envelope".

### Compatibility

| Rule | Basis |
|---|---|
| An event type's major version is the `dataschema` URI's; a breaking change to `data` is a new major version and a new schema file, published beside the old one for the period the deprecation rule below fixes | The research's mapping of `event_version`; the P0.8 design's path-version rule, applied to events |
| Within a major version, `data` changes additively only, E8's rule; the envelope's attributes never change within `specversion` `1.0`, and a new custom extension is added to `extensions.yaml` with its date and is optional for one major version of every type | The research's second fact: custom extensions are the platform's to "define and version" |
| A consumer ignores a context attribute it does not know and a `data` field it does not know, the convention the research records the specification does not state as a rule, adopted here as the platform's rule and proven by control 8 | The research's note that "consumers ignore unknown extensions" is "convention, not a quoted rule" |
| A consumer never reads a fact from an attribute it could read from `data`; the attributes route and deduplicate, `data` carries the fact | The primer's placement rule (S8) |
| Deprecation of a major version is carried in the AsyncAPI document as `deprecated: true` on the message and an `x-biztrust-sunset` date, the P0.8 design's extension, and enforced by the lint after the date | The P0.8 design's deprecation rule, applied to events |
| A published event is never edited or deleted; a wrong fact is corrected by a later event that supersedes it, the domain's correction rule, with `causationid` naming the event it corrects | ARCH-001 section 12, "Domain events describe completed business facts"; the P0.6 design's `supersedes_id` |

### What an event is not

Three rules from the records, made checks. An event is not a command: "A command request is not an event", so no type is named in the imperative and no consumer performs a transition it would refuse on its own contract because an event asked; the lint refuses a `type` whose last segment is not a past participle in the platform's word list, and the P0.2 design's boundary rule keeps a consumer from reaching the producer's tables. An event is not a grant of authority: "an event does not grant authority to another module to bypass its own transition rules" (ARCH-001 section 12), so a consumer applies its own preconditions and authorization to what an event tells it, and `authorityref` is a reference it may check and never a permission it inherits. An event is not an audit record: the P0.10 design's record is written in the same transaction as the outbox row and is what evidences the decision; the event tells other modules what happened, and a consumer's application of it writes its own record.

## Negative controls

Each control is seen failing before the protection exists and passing after; the verifier is not the implementer. The environment holds the P0 events document with the tenancy events, the fixtures, the synthetic tenants and two modules in the lab, a producer and a consumer.

| # | Threat or defect | Condition | Expected denial or failure | Seen failing at |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The broken document passes | The fixtures run under the lint job; then a fixture is edited to pass | Each fixture fails its rule by name; the edited fixture makes the fixture run itself fail | Before the job: CI is green with every fixture in the tree |
| 2 | An envelope outside the profile | A message example whose envelope lacks `tenantid` on a tenant event, carries `tenant_id` with an underscore, or carries a JSON object in an attribute | E2, E5 or E6 fails naming the attribute; the official parser alone passes it, which is the research's gap made visible | Before the second gate: the document validates |
| 3 | An event without its transaction | A fixture build of the tenancy command that publishes after commit rather than into the outbox; a fault injected between commit and publish | The event is lost in the fixture build, which the test asserts as the failing baseline; with the outbox rule the event is in the outbox row of the same transaction and the relay delivers it after recovery; the two states of the world never differ | Before the outbox rule: a state change with no event, or an event with no state change |
| 4 | A duplicate delivery | The relay delivers one event twice to the consumer | The inbox's claim is found; the second delivery is acknowledged with `outcome = duplicate` and the consumer's transition runs once | Before the inbox: the transition runs twice |
| 5 | An event out of order | Two events of one tenant from one source relayed in reverse `sequence` | The consumer, reading `sequence`, holds the later one until the earlier is applied, or applies both and records the order it saw; the design's rule is the first, with the second a measurement of the transport once one exists | Before `sequence`: the later fact is applied first and the earlier overwrites it |
| 6 | A cross-tenant event | The relay, with Tenant A's key set, reads the outbox | Tenant A's rows only; Tenant B's events are relayed in Tenant B's transaction and none other; the consumer's inbox row carries the event's `tenantid` and the P0.7 design's `WITH CHECK` refuses a claim under the wrong key | On the P0.7 design's baseline database: both tenants' rows read under one key |
| 7 | A secret or a document in an attribute | A fixture envelope with a bearer token in `authid` and a person's name in `subject` | E6 and E7 fail the fixture; at runtime the scrubber refuses the outbox row and the command fails, since an event that cannot be written does not commit | Before the scrubber: the row is written and relayed |
| 8 | An unknown attribute or field | The producer adds a registered custom extension and a new optional `data` field; the consumer is a build that predates both | The consumer applies the event and ignores both, recording `applied` | Before the rule: the consumer rejects the event as malformed |
| 9 | A breaking schema change | A pull request retypes a `data` field in `v1` of a tenancy event's schema | E8 fails the pull request naming the field; the change is admitted only as `v2` beside `v1` | Before E8: the pull request merges and a consumer on `v1` misreads the field |
| 10 | The transport's own deduplication | If a transport is ever chosen, its native deduplication measured against the inbox, the research's `Nats-Msg-Id` window as the example | Not a protection: a measurement, recorded as the baseline for the rule that the inbox is the deduplication and the transport's is a convenience | Not a protection: the measurement is the record |

Controls 1 and 2 are the manual's acceptance; controls 3, 4 and 6 are FLOWS section 15's sequence; control 5 is `sequence`'s reason for being; control 7 is the specification's privacy rule; controls 8 and 9 are the compatibility rules; control 10 is a measurement and runs only when a transport exists.

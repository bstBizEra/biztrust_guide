Reference · not a stage

Continuous Operations and the expansion streams

After BT-G7 is recorded and Production v1.0 is released, the platform is not finished; it is operated. Continuous Operations is that operating lifecycle: measure, learn, improve, expand. The expansion it allows is eight streams, each opening on a recorded condition of its own, none on a date, and none carrying a phase number.

Rendered from BIZTRUST-PLAN-001.md sections 8 and 11, which restate the roadmap's section 13 and propose an entry condition per stream. The two tables below are held to those sections by a test; where they disagree, the plan is right and this page is a defect. The phase overview carries one row for Continuous Operations and links here; the P3 manual ends here.

THE RULE THIS PAGE EXISTS TO KEEP

Nothing after P3 carries a phase number. A stream opens only when BT-G7 is recorded, Production v1.0 is released, and the stream's own entry condition is recorded by the decision owner it names; none is ordered among the others except as its condition names another, and none is authorised by the plan. The previous plan said post-P3 work must not be labelled P4 to P9 because a number implies an unreviewed sequencing commitment; the streams keep that rule by carrying no number at all.

01

The decision

Why a reference page, not an overview section

The operations ticket on the Guide v2 map asked whether Continuous Operations and the streams are a section of the phase overview or a page under reference/. This page is the answer, and the reasons are this track's, not the plan's.

The overview is a rendering of five phases

Its phases table is held to PLAN-001 sections 2 to 8 by a test, and its rule is that nothing after P3 is a phase. Eight stream rows with entry conditions beside five phase rows would put the streams where a reader expects phases, which is the misreading the rule exists to prevent. The overview keeps one row for Continuous Operations and links here.

A lookup has a shape the guide already uses

The artifact catalogue and the risk-tier schedule are cross-page lookups that the manuals cite rather than restate. The streams are the same kind of thing: the P3 manual, the hub and the overview each need one place to point at, and a page is a place.

Entry conditions are proposals with decision owners

Each condition names an ADR or a seat that records it. A table of proposals waiting on eight different owners belongs beside the other lookups, not inside the manual of a phase whose gate has its own owner.

02

The loop

Measure, learn, improve, expand

PLAN-001 section 8 names the loop in four words and no more. How the guide's stage manuals carry each word is this track's reading, offered so that the loop is not a fifth track to maintain.

Measure

The Operate stage's service objectives and telemetry floor, read against the indicators and objectives the P3 manual's P3F.1 defined. Error budget consumed per objective is the first number the loop reads.

Learn

The Learn stage: postmortems with follow-up actions owned, and the promotion bar that decides what a package's evidence has earned.

Improve

A Work Package. Every change after v1.0 still runs the full lifecycle from Discover to Learn; Continuous Operations changes what the packages are about, not how one moves.

Expand

A stream below, opened on its recorded condition and then delivered as Work Packages like any other capability, under whatever gate its own charter names.

Two conditions name a gate, and it is not the stream's gate

E1 waits on BT-G6 and E2 on BT-G7. Those are P3's gates, recorded by their own owners on P3's evidence; a stream's condition cites them as facts it needs, and a stream opening records nothing about them. What gate, if any, closes a stream is the stream's own charter's to say, and no charter exists.

05

The carried epics

Two epics the previous plan placed after finance

The previous plan's finance and integration phase ended with a partner API and a Tenant Pack validation foundation. PLAN-001 section 9 moves them out of the production-critical plan into E2 and E1 as each stream's first item. What each was on the previous P3 manual is carried below from that page's rows at the WP-054 merge, so the capability is not lost with the identifier; how it is delivered inside its stream is the stream's to decide when it opens.

E1's first item · Tenant Pack validation foundation

Schema, signing and compatibility validation for tenant-specific configuration — extension without forks or unrestricted code; proven when a tenant provisions and isolates through a pack with no core-code branch for either tenant; built against ADR-011 and INV-011. Note what moved with it: BT-G6 Tenant Ready no longer requires a pack. The P3 manual's two BT-G6 items ask that Tenant #2 provisions without a core-code fork and that no tenant-specific code path exists; the pack is how E1 makes that repeatable at scale, not how P3 proves it once.

E2's first item · Partner API and versioned webhooks

The partner contract family under openapi/biztrust-partner-api.yaml; outbound webhooks versioned and signed; a breaking-change policy in CI; proven by contract lint and compatibility tests where a breaking change fails CI; built against ADR-005 and INV-012. E2's condition asks for a partner contract that names the API surface it needs, so the first Work Package of E2 is scoped by a partner, not by the previous plan's row.

06

Limits

What this page does not do

  • It orders no stream and designs none; PLAN-001 section 8 puts both out of the plan's scope until Production v1.0 exists
  • It records no condition as met and no stream as open; each condition's decision owner does that, in the record the ADR or seat keeps
  • It does not carry the cross-cutting tracks of PLAN-001 section 12, which run through the phases and are not streams
  • It does not say what gate closes a stream; no stream has a charter yet, and a charter is where that belongs
  • It is not the hub's sentence on Continuous Operations; the hub's roadmap section at v2.0 is its own ticket on the Guide v2 map, and may link here

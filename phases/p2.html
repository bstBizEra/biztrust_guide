Delivery phases · P2

Payment and Finance

P2 closes the loop from insurance to money to insurer with records that are attributable and reconcilable. Payment, ledger, commission, settlement and reconciliation are each their own thing, and ten concepts never collapse into one object. It is the phase where a wrong model is most expensive, because the records it creates are the ones an auditor, an insurer and a regulator will read, and the phase in which a payment provider's word must never become a statement about cover.

Rendered from BIZTRUST-PLAN-001.md §6, whose nine rows are the previous plan's finance epics renumbered into the roadmap's six capability groups, with the payment, client-money, payment-to-policy and journal flows from FLOWS.md §12 to §14, the financial boundary from ARCH-001 §13, and the primary-source facts of the reconciliation research where this page names them. The P3 page still renders the previous plan's finance and integration phase, nine of whose epics are the rows below under their old identifiers, until its ticket moves it to Production.

THE RULE THIS PHASE EXISTS TO ENFORCE

Payment state cannot invent coverage state; any premium condition affecting cover is evaluated by the authority-aware insurance workflow (INV-020). Posted ledger records are immutable; corrections use reversals or compensating entries (INV-008). Client-money and office-money balances, movements and reconciliations remain distinguishable under the approved operating regime (INV-021). And invoice, payment, payment intent, payment attempt, payment allocation, ledger, commission, settlement, reconciliation and refund are ten records, never one (the roadmap's core rule, carried as P2's design constraint by PLAN-001 section 6).

01

Preconditions

Entry criteria

BT-G2 and BT-G3 (label C) are recorded

The broker core ran its slice and the lifecycle passed its failure scenarios. P2 posts money against workflows that have already been shown to survive duplicates and conflicts; it does not add durability to them, and it does not add cover to anything.

The money regime is decided, by qualified people

ADR-014 answers, per tenant arrangement, whose money the intermediary holds, when premium counts as received by the insurer, who bears transit risk, which accounts are segregated and when commission may be withdrawn. The contract marks it JURISDICTION_DEPENDENT. Read its row in the register; until that row reads ACCEPTED with the legal and finance seats recorded, a generic premium journal is not evidence.

The ledger, adapter and idempotency ADRs are accepted

ADR-008 (immutable double-entry subledger), ADR-009 (adapters), ADR-012 (idempotent financial and binding mutations) and ADR-018 (premium conditions and non-payment without payment systems inventing cover). Provider selections — insurer, payment provider, bank — are on the deliberately-deferred list and each arrives as its own ADR.

03

Execution order

The ledger before anything that posts to it

PLAN-001 lists the nine epics unordered. This order is the track's proposal, carried from the previous manual: the ledger first, because every later epic posts to it.

  1. P2B.1 — the subledger. Posting rules, balanced drafts, atomic rejection, append-only. Every later epic posts here; a ledger designed after the payment code will be shaped by the payment code.
  2. P2A.1 — invoices, the first source transactions the ledger receives.
  3. P2A.2 — payment state, with the four dimensions separate from the first migration.
  4. P2A.3 with P2F.1 — the first adapter and the boundary it lives behind. Build the SDK contract from the first adapter, then prove it with a second. The payment-to-policy safety workflow is exercised here: a callback that arrives before authority evidence waits, retries or escalates, and never activates cover.
  5. P2A.4 — refunds, reversals, chargebacks. Compensating entries against a ledger that already refuses edits.
  6. P2C.1 — commission, with its retained inputs, and clawback under ADR-020.
  7. P2D.1 — settlement and remittance.
  8. P2E.1 — the three reconciliations, each proven by a planted difference. Then BT-G4.
  9. The fault-injection suite across P2A.3 and P2F.1 — the provider-facing failure cases of Gate D, each a test, against a provider sandbox; the ledger, reconciliation and settlement cases were proven under their own epics before BT-G4. Then BT-G5.
04

What the agent codes

Money that cannot invent cover

The safety workflow is the P2 equivalent of P0's bypass test: the single sequence every reviewer walks. It is rendered here as the steps a callback goes through; the modules it asks may refuse at every step.

  1. Verify the provider signature and deduplicate the event by the provider's own identifier before anything else reads it.
  2. Emit PaymentConfirmed as a fact about the payment. It says nothing about cover.
  3. Query the binding module for authority and coverage-confirmation state.
  4. If authoritative confirmation is missing: wait, retry or escalate. No coverage transition. The premium is held under the regime ADR-014 accepted, not applied to a cover that does not exist.
  5. If authority evidence and the effective period are valid: request policy registration or activation from the policy module; on PolicyCoverageChanged, request an approved journal posting from the ledger.
  6. If payment succeeded and binding failed: the approved compensation policy decides hold, void or refund. The workflow does not invent a financial outcome.

The bank-versus-ledger control closes on a statement, not a notification

ISO 20022's camt.053 statement contains booked entries only and carries balances; camt.052 and camt.054 may report pending and booked items and camt.054 carries no balance. Those are the publisher's words (research §3). That a period closes on the statement and a notification only advances a status is this track's reading of research §7.

Two keys on every bank transaction

The platform's EndToEndId, passed unchanged through the whole chain and usable for reconciliation, and the bank's own AcctSvcrRef (research §3). Timing difference can be defined on the entry's booking date versus its value date versus the ledger's effective date (this track's reading of research §7).

Outsourcing card capture does not take the platform out of PCI DSS

The PCI Security Standards Council says so directly; written agreements and annual monitoring of the provider remain the platform's obligations, and an embedded card form is scoped differently from a redirect. Which self-assessment applies is the compliance-accepting entity's call, not this page's (research §4).

Lao rails, as the Bank of the Lao PDR publishes them

The bank-reporting standard lists ISO 20022 messages at version 001.02, so a current-catalogue parser needs version negotiation per bank; Lao QR is a BOL scheme switched by LAPNet, so the reconciled fact is the bank statement, not the QR acknowledgement (research §5). Whether a given Lao bank delivers statement files to corporate customers is UNVERIFIABLE from BOL's pages, and the research marks twelve such items (research §7 and §8).

  • Provider payloads never become domain schemas. The adapter maps them to the canonical model and retains the source as immutable evidence under retention policy
  • No hidden offset between client and office money, no allocation beyond cleared funds, no settlement completion with an unowned difference — each is a database-level constraint or an atomic rejection, not a report
  • Every financial mutation is idempotent under retry and concurrency — key scope, retention, replay response and race behaviour are ADR-012's evidence, and P2's tests
  • Historical figures recompute from retained inputs. A commission or a journal that cannot be reproduced from what was stored is a number, not a record
  • The ten records stay ten. A payment row with a refund column, an invoice with a settlement status, a ledger entry that is also the reconciliation verdict — each is the collapse the roadmap's core rule forbids

Stop signal: a payment callback activated cover

The exact failure INV-020 names. The fix is not a check in the callback; it is the workflow that should have been between them.

Stop signal: a posted journal was edited

An UPDATE or DELETE that succeeds against a posted record means the ledger is a table, not a ledger.

Stop signal: client and office money offset each other

A convenient net figure that hides which money paid for what. Under most regimes it is also the finding an auditor is looking for.

Stop signal: a provider's status value appears in the domain model

The adapter boundary has been crossed; replacing that provider now means a schema migration.

Stop signal: a period closed on a notification

A reconciliation marked matched from a callback or a QR acknowledgement, with no statement entry behind it, has reconciled the platform to itself.

Daily, then weekly

Reconciliation drift as telemetry, read by the reconciliation owner; the state of each of the three controls; provider sandbox test results after any adapter change; whether any P3 concept — an environment, a release control, a runbook — or any stream item — a partner API, a tenant pack — has appeared in a P2 branch.

07

Halt

Stop conditions

  • ADR-014 is not accepted by qualified legal and finance review for the operating jurisdiction — there is no regime to post under
  • A payment callback, or any payment state, changes coverage state
  • A posted ledger record is edited or deleted, by any path
  • Client money and office money are offset, netted or held in one class
  • A provider's payload or status vocabulary reaches a domain schema
  • Two of the ten records are stored as one
  • Scope drifts into P3 — an environment, a pipeline, a release control — or into a stream — a partner API, a tenant pack — inside a P2 package
  • A page or a record presents a journal as accounting advice; every entry here is conceptual until the chart of accounts is approved

Delivery phases · P3

Production

P3 is not primarily feature development. Its objective is to prove that the whole system can be operated: from working software, through production engineering, to a reliable service. Every epic here produces evidence that a human reads before recording the last gate, and the phase ends only when the production-readiness review is done by someone other than the people who built the thing being reviewed.

Rendered from BIZTRUST-PLAN-001.md §7, whose ten rows are new in that plan from the roadmap's section 7, with the definitions the epics rest on from the map's research: release engineering, reliability and disaster recovery and mobile release pipelines, each cited to its section where this page uses it. This is the last phase page to move, so every phase manual now renders PLAN-001, and the previous plan's phase sections are retired to pointers.

THE RULE THIS PHASE EXISTS TO ENFORCE

A gate is recorded by a human on revision-bound evidence; a passing pipeline records nothing. A backup is not evidence, a restore is. A deployment is not a release until the service objectives it must meet exist and are measured. And three definitions this phase uses have no primary source: the incident severity scale, rolling deployment, and rollback outside the blue-green case. Each is a local decision on this page and is labelled one, as PLAN-001 section 7 requires.

01

Preconditions

Entry criteria

BT-G4 and BT-G5 (label D) are recorded

The money reconciles and the adapters survive fault injection. P3 operates a system whose financial and integration evidence already exists; it does not produce that evidence, and it does not build features to fill gaps the earlier gates should have found.

Service objectives before any release gate

An SLI is the measure, an SLO is the target on it, and the error budget is the rate at which the SLO may be missed; the SRE book couples release velocity to that budget and the workbook requires a written budget policy with actions and owners. P3 cannot define a release gate before it defines its indicators and objectives, so P3F.1 is a precondition of P3C.1, not a parallel epic (release research, decision-relevant facts, first).

Recovery objectives are a design input

Recovery point and recovery time are two numbers, and the second is bounded by a third, the maximum tolerable downtime; NIST places backup and recovery strategy in the development phase of the lifecycle, not after launch. P3G.1 states all three per system, with the source of the downtime figure (release research, definitions table and readiness-review section).

03

Execution order

Measure before you release, restore before you promise

PLAN-001 lists the ten epics unordered. This order is the track's proposal, and two of its dependencies are the research's: objectives before a release gate, and a tested restore before any recovery claim.

  1. P3A.1 — environments. Six of them, one artifact, promotion recorded. Nothing else in P3 can be proven without a production-like target to prove it on.
  2. P3B.1 — the pipeline. The roadmap's sequence, with the build failing on the security baseline and on vulnerable dependencies, and the release-evidence step first: every later epic's proof rides in it.
  3. P3D.1 — observability. The correlation identity through every hop, before the indicators that will read it exist.
  4. P3F.1 — indicators, objectives, budget policy. Measured, targeted, owned. This is the step the release gate waits for.
  5. P3E.1 — reliability controls, each fault-injected, and the continuous restore test switched on.
  6. P3G.1 — disaster recovery. The three numbers per system and the exercise on an alternate platform. The evidence is the restore record.
  7. P3C.1 — release engineering, now that objectives exist to evaluate a canary against and a budget exists to halt on.
  8. P3I.1 — security operations, on a pipeline that already fails on findings and telemetry that already carries the signals.
  9. P3J.1 — incident management, rehearsed against a planted incident, with the severity scale labelled local.
  10. P3H.1 — mobile production, last among the epics because its store review is the one gate whose duration is not the platform's, and its prerequisites belong in the evidence list weeks before the first reviewed build.
  11. The tenant proof and the review. Tenant #2 provisions without a fork and the P0 proof passes against both, recorded as BT-G6; then the production-readiness review by someone other than the implementers, and the security risk owner's dated record: BT-G7.
04

What the agent codes

Definitions with their sources, and three without

P3's failure signature is a word used without a definition: a "rollback" that turns out to be an edit in production, a "severity" nobody can assign consistently, a "DR test" that was a backup job. The table below gives each term the definition its source gives it, and marks the three the research found no source for.

The pipeline cites two authorities for two things

SAMM describes the pipeline: SAST in the build, the build failing on a predefined security baseline, a dependency bill of materials through build failure on vulnerable dependencies, DAST at deploy with stop-or-reverse on criticality, signature verification before deploy. ASVS describes the product and says it does not dictate how the application is built; its dependency requirements 15.1.1, 15.1.2, 15.2.1 and 15.2.4 are what the product must satisfy. Cite SAMM for a pipeline step and ASVS for a product requirement, never the reverse (release research, decision-relevant facts, fifth).

Running a tool is not a gate

ASVS warns that automated SAST and DAST cover only the simpler requirements and that "testable using automation" is not the same as running an off-the-shelf tool. The pipeline's security steps are evidence for the security risk owner; they do not record a gate (release research, ASVS section).

The twelve initial indicators

API availability, authentication success and failure, tenant authorization denial rate, quote latency, insurer integration success, payment success rate, policy binding latency, webhook backlog, reconciliation exceptions, claim workflow latency, error rate, mobile crash-free sessions (roadmap section 7, P3F). Each gets a measured value and an objective before any release gate is defined.

Mobile: the store is the last gate and it is not ours

Apple's review is mostly under a day and Google's runs hours to a week or more; Apple allows one version per platform under review at a time, so a hotfix replaces a pending release. Gradual rollout exists on both stores and rollback on neither: the reversal is halting the rollout and shipping forward, with server-side flags as the real revert. Forced update is Android-only and consent-based; minimum-version enforcement is app logic on both. The regulated-financial prerequisites, a legal-entity submitter, a working demo account, licence documents, the Data safety declaration and the closed-test requirement, belong in P3H.1's evidence list, not in launch week (mobile research, decision-relevant facts).

Biometric unlock is a data-binding decision

Both platforms return only success or failure and never biometric data; the documented pattern binds keychain or keystore material to the current biometric set so that an enrolment change invalidates it. That is the mobile identity foundation's design, the fourteenth epic of P1A; P3H.1 proves it survives the store pipeline (mobile research, biometric section).

  • Every gate item is bound to a revision. The release-evidence step of P3B.1 is where AGENTS.md section 9's binding happens; an item with evidence and no revision is not on the list
  • Deployments are performed by computers, not humans, from reproducible, hermetic, automated builds of small self-contained artifacts (SRE Workbook ch. 16 and SRE Book ch. 8, via the release research)
  • A local definition is labelled wherever it is used — in a runbook, a test name, an alert, a postmortem field — never only on this page
  • A restore that was not performed is not evidence, and a recovery time that was not measured is not an objective met
  • No tenant-specific branch, directory or flag appears in anything P3 ships; tenant differences are configuration, and the tenant pack that validates them is the first item of expansion stream E1, not a P3 epic

Stop signal: a release went out with no objective to judge it by

A canary with nothing to evaluate against is a deployment with a delay. The fix is P3F.1, not a longer delay.

Stop signal: a "DR test" that was a backup

A job that wrote a backup proved that a backup was written. The evidence P3G.1 asks for is a restore record with a measured time.

Stop signal: a rollback that edited production

A configuration change made in place to "get back" to the last good state is not a rollback under the local definition; it is a new, unrecorded release.

Stop signal: a severity assigned from the roadmap's names alone

SEV-1 to SEV-4 are names; without the local criteria on this page they mean whatever the assigner meant, and the postmortem cannot be compared with the last one.

Stop signal: a store submission surprised by its prerequisites

A rejection for a missing demo account, licence document or declaration is a planning failure P3H.1's evidence list exists to prevent.

Weekly

Error budget consumed per objective; the restore test's last successful run; the pipeline's security findings past their remediation time frame; whether any expansion-stream item, a partner API, a tenant pack, a new adapter, has appeared in a P3 branch.

07

After P3

Continuous Operations, and eight streams with no phase number

After BT-G7 is recorded and Production v1.0 is released, Continuous Operations runs: measure, learn, improve, expand. PLAN-001 section 8 converts the roadmap's later proposals into eight expansion streams, E1 Tenant Scale to E8 Ecosystem, with entry conditions in section 11 and no phase number, which is what the previous plan's horizon required. The two epics the previous plan placed after finance, the partner API and the tenant pack, are the first items of E2 and E1. Where Continuous Operations and the streams are rendered in this guide is the operations ticket's decision on the Guide v2 map.

Production is not a phase

After BT-G7 the platform is not finished; it is operated. Every package that ships from then on still runs the full lifecycle, and the Operate and Learn stages run continuously against the service objectives, the telemetry floor and the promotion bar those pages define. The phase track ends here; the stage track does not end.

08

Halt

Stop conditions

  • A release gate is defined before the indicators and objectives it judges by exist
  • A backup job is presented as disaster-recovery evidence, or a recovery objective is stated without the tolerable downtime it derives from
  • Rolling deployment, rollback or a severity level is used without the local definition and its label
  • A pipeline step records a gate, or a gate is recorded on a green pipeline without a human
  • Anything tenant-specific appears as code; a partner API, a tenant pack or a new adapter appears inside a P3 package
  • Feature work is scheduled into P3 to fill a gap an earlier gate should have found
  • The production-readiness review is executed by the people who built what it reviews
  • Work after P3 is numbered as a phase

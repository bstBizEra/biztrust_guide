Delivery phases · P3

Financial and integration platform

P3 closes the loop from insurance to money to insurer with records that are attributable and reconcilable, and ends by proving that a second independent tenant provisions without a core-code fork. It is the phase where a wrong model is most expensive, because the records it creates are the ones an auditor reads.

Rendered from DELIVERY_PLAN.md §6, with the payment, client-money, payment-to-policy and journal flows from FLOWS.md §12–§14 and the financial and integration boundaries from ARCH-001 §13–§14.

THE RULE THIS PHASE EXISTS TO ENFORCE

Payment state cannot invent coverage state — INV-020. Posted ledger records are immutable; corrections are reversals or compensating entries — INV-008. Client-money and office-money balances, movements and reconciliations stay distinguishable under the approved regime — INV-021. And the examples in the architecture are conceptual until jurisdiction and accounting review approve a chart of accounts: nothing here is accounting advice.

01

Preconditions

Entry criteria

BT-G3 is recorded

The lifecycle passed its failure scenarios. P3 posts money against workflows that have already been shown to survive duplicates and conflicts; it does not add durability to them.

The money regime is decided, by qualified people

ADR-014 answers, per tenant arrangement, whose money the intermediary holds, when premium counts as received by the insurer, who bears transit risk, which accounts are segregated and when commission may be withdrawn. It is JURISDICTION_DEPENDENT and BLOCKED_BY_S01 until the legal and finance seats record their review. A generic premium journal is not evidence.

The ledger, adapter and idempotency ADRs are accepted

ADR-008 (immutable double-entry subledger), ADR-009 (adapters), ADR-012 (idempotent financial and binding mutations) and ADR-018 (premium conditions and non-payment without payment systems inventing cover). Provider selections — insurer, payment provider, bank — are on the deliberately-deferred list and each arrives as its own ADR.

03

Execution order

The ledger before anything that posts to it

  1. P3.5 — the subledger. Posting rules, balanced drafts, atomic rejection, append-only. Every later epic posts here; a ledger designed after the payment code will be shaped by the payment code.
  2. P3.1 — invoices, the first source transactions the ledger receives.
  3. P3.2 — payment state, with the four dimensions separate from the first migration.
  4. P3.3 with P3.9 — the first adapter and the boundary it lives behind. Build the SDK contract from the first adapter, then prove it with a second. The payment-to-policy safety workflow is exercised here: a callback that arrives before authority evidence waits, retries or escalates, and never activates cover.
  5. P3.4 — refunds, reversals, chargebacks. Compensating entries against a ledger that already refuses edits.
  6. P3.6 — commission, with its retained inputs, and clawback under ADR-020.
  7. P3.7 — settlement and remittance.
  8. P3.8 — the three reconciliations, each proven by a planted difference. Then BT-G4.
  9. P3.10 — the partner API and webhooks, on adapters and contracts that already pass fault injection. With P3.3 and P3.9, BT-G5.
  10. P3.11 — tenant packs, and the proof that Tenant #2 provisions without a fork. BT-G6.
04

What the agent codes

Money that cannot invent cover

The safety workflow is the P3 equivalent of P0's bypass test: the single sequence every reviewer walks. It is rendered here as the steps a callback goes through; the modules it asks may refuse at every step.

  1. Verify the provider signature and deduplicate the event by the provider's own identifier before anything else reads it.
  2. Emit PaymentConfirmed as a fact about the payment. It says nothing about cover.
  3. Query the binding module for authority and coverage-confirmation state.
  4. If authoritative confirmation is missing: wait, retry or escalate. No coverage transition. The premium is held under the regime ADR-014 accepted, not applied to a cover that does not exist.
  5. If authority evidence and the effective period are valid: request policy registration or activation from the policy module; on PolicyCoverageChanged, request an approved journal posting from the ledger.
  6. If payment succeeded and binding failed: the approved compensation policy decides hold, void or refund. The workflow does not invent a financial outcome.

  • Provider payloads never become domain schemas. The adapter maps them to the canonical model and retains the source as immutable evidence under retention policy
  • No hidden offset between client and office money, no allocation beyond cleared funds, no settlement completion with an unowned difference — each is a database-level constraint or an atomic rejection, not a report
  • Every financial mutation is idempotent under retry and concurrency — key scope, retention, replay response and race behaviour are ADR-012's evidence, and P3's tests
  • Historical figures recompute from retained inputs. A commission or a journal that cannot be reproduced from what was stored is a number, not a record
  • A tenant pack is configuration under a schema, signed and version-checked. It is not a plugin, not a script, and not a branch

Stop signal: a payment callback activated cover

The exact failure INV-020 names. The fix is not a check in the callback; it is the workflow that should have been between them.

Stop signal: a posted journal was edited

An UPDATE or DELETE that succeeds against a posted record means the ledger is a table, not a ledger.

Stop signal: client and office money offset each other

A convenient net figure that hides which money paid for what. Under most regimes it is also the finding an auditor is looking for.

Stop signal: a provider's status value appears in the domain model

The adapter boundary has been crossed; replacing that provider now means a schema migration.

Stop signal: a branch, directory or flag named after a tenant

That is the fork BT-G6 exists to rule out. Tenant differences live in packs.

Daily, then weekly

Reconciliation drift as telemetry, read by the reconciliation owner; the state of each of the three controls; provider sandbox test results after any adapter change.

07

After P3

The horizon is uncommitted, on purpose

The plan names what may expand after P3 — claims and renewal depth, partner scale, additional isolation tiers, analytics, AI assistance — and says in the same sentence that they are roadmap candidates until business value, risk, dependencies and authority are defined. They must not be labelled P4 through P9, because a number implies a sequencing commitment that has not been reviewed.

Production is not a phase

After BT-G6 the platform is not finished; it is operated. Every package that ships from then on still runs the full lifecycle, and the Operate and Learn stages run continuously against the service objectives, the telemetry floor and the promotion bar those pages define. The phase track ends here; the stage track does not end.

08

Halt

Stop conditions

  • ADR-014 is not accepted by qualified legal and finance review for the operating jurisdiction — there is no regime to post under
  • A payment callback, or any payment state, changes coverage state
  • A posted ledger record is edited or deleted, by any path
  • Client money and office money are offset, netted or held in one class
  • A provider's payload or status vocabulary reaches a domain schema
  • Anything tenant-specific appears as code rather than as a validated pack
  • Work after P3 is numbered as a phase

Delivery phases · P1

Insurance

P1 builds broker-native insurance operations as one macro-phase in three controlled sub-phases: P1A, a broker core that is usable before any insurer exposes an API; P1B, the professional brokerage lifecycle of placement, servicing, claims advocacy and renewal; P1C, a product engine that makes insurance products versioned configuration, and the straight-through digital model that runs on it. Automation of insurer APIs is not required. Correct state, evidence and ownership are — and they are what the two gates measure.

Rendered from BIZTRUST-PLAN-001.md §5, whose P1A and P1B rows are the previous plan's broker core and lifecycle phases renumbered and whose P1C rows come from the roadmap, with the lifecycle and state machines from FLOWS.md §1, §2 and §6 to §11 and the conceptual model from DOMAIN_MODEL.md §2. The P2 page still renders the previous plan's lifecycle phase, which is P1B here, until its ticket moves it to Payment and Finance.

THE RULE THIS PHASE EXISTS TO ENFORCE

A bind request records an instruction to seek or exercise authority for cover. Coverage is represented as confirmed only from retained evidence produced by the authority holder for that tenant and product arrangement — INV-005, INV-016 and INV-018. A broker bind request is not a carrier bind confirmation; a broker policy record is not the insurer's authoritative policy; a broker claim status is not the insurer's adjudication status. An application role does not grant authority to conclude an insurance contract.

01

Preconditions

Entry criteria

BT-G1 (label B) is recorded, independently

The security risk owner has recorded Security Ready on a proof run by someone other than its implementer. P1 puts real business records on the substrate; the substrate must already be proven, not promised.

The authority and product ADRs are accepted, not blocked

ADR-013 (who may create authoritative cover), ADR-017 (product, wording, rating and quote authority), ADR-006 (product versions, which P1C's engine implements), ADR-007 (broker versus insurer facts) and ADR-015 (effective and record time). Read each one's current row in the register; a row that reads BLOCKED_BY_S01 or DRAFT_REQUIRED is not an accepted one, and the binding code cannot be written to it.

The pilot tenant's authority profile is accepted

Slice S01 answered, for this tenant and product, whether every bind is REQUEST_ONLY or whether delegated or master-policy authority exists. The binding state machine cannot be built without that answer, and an agent may not infer it. For the straight-through model of P1C the same answer decides whether a certificate is issued by the platform under a master policy or only requested.

03

Execution order

The vertical slice first, then the lifecycle, then the engine

The previous plan's acceptance path is twelve steps and remains P1A's; PLAN-001 keeps it; the sub-phase order below is this track's proposal, not the plan's: P1B when the slice runs, P1C's engine alongside P1B, its straight-through workflow last, where the roadmap draws the three strictly in sequence. Build each step as thin as it can be while still being real, and do not widen a step until the one after it runs. A wide P1A.1 with no P1A.10 behind it is a client database, not a broker core.

  1. Create tenant, create broker user — on the P0 substrate; no new tenancy code. If this needs a change to P0, P0 was not finished.
  2. Create client — P1A.1, with the ownership baseline on the aggregate from the first migration.
  3. Capture risk — P1A.3, and the snapshot rule that makes a submission immutable.
  4. Select a sourced distribution version — P1A.4. This step decides whether provenance is a property of the model or a comment in it; build the insurer-owned side first.
  5. Submit risk — P1A.5, with the transition table tested both ways.
  6. Record authority offers — P1A.6. Three concepts. The temptation is one table with a kind column; that is how an indication becomes a quote in a report.
  7. Compare and recommend — P1A.7, P1A.8, with the disclosures.
  8. Record client acceptance — P1A.9.
  9. Request bind — P1A.10, the state machine up to PENDING_CONFIRMATION. Stop and test: nothing so far means insured.
  10. Record authority-supported cover — P1A.10, the CONFIRMED transition and its evidence requirements, per the accepted authority profile.
  11. Register the broker policy representation — P1A.11, referencing the bind, the evidence and the versions.
  12. Documents, audit, portal — P1A.12 alongside every step above; P1A.13 last, driven by the contracts. Then the trace, end to end, for BT-G2.
  13. P1B opens: placement first — P1B.1 and P1B.2, because every later lifecycle transition re-enters placement; then P1B.3 revisions on the quote model P1A left.
  14. Make binding durable — P1B.4 wraps the P1A.10 machine; its fault-injection suite is the reason P1B has a gate of its own.
  15. Servicing, claims, renewal — P1B.5, P1B.6, P1B.7 in that order, each on effective and record time, each keeping the broker's status apart from the insurer's.
  16. Tasks, compliance, events — P1B.8 and P1B.9 alongside; P1B.10 before any consumer depends on delivery guarantees the outbox does not yet give.
  17. P1C's engine, alongside P1B — P1C.1 first, then P1C.2, P1C.3 and P1C.8 as its rules, pricing and channel faces; P1C.5 once versions exist to rank.
  18. The mobile foundation, then the clients — P1A.14 before either client; P1C.7 for agents, then P1C.6 for customers, each driving the same contracts as the portal.
  19. Straight-through last — P1C.4 runs only on an accepted authority profile, with the payment hand-off returning a fact from P2 and the insurer's confirmation received. Then the trace for BT-G3.
04

What the agent codes

State that cannot lie about authority

Every state machine in P1 follows the contract template: a transition table, preconditions, emitted events, and the failure signature the Architect stage names — one field asked to carry several independent states. The binding rules below are the ones a reviewer checks first, because they are the ones the whole broker-versus-insurer boundary rests on; the product-engine rules follow, because they are how P1C avoids becoming a second application.

  • REQUESTED, AUTHORITY_RESOLVED, SENT_TO_AUTHORITY, REFERRAL_REQUIRED and PENDING_CONFIRMATION do not mean insured. No read model, document, portal screen or mobile screen may render them as cover
  • CONFIRMED requires the accepted authority-agreement or profile reference, the source identity, effective start and end, recorded time and retained evidence — as mandatory fields, not optional ones
  • Under REQUEST_ONLY, only insurer-authoritative evidence can confirm. A broker user with the binding:confirm permission and no applicable authority profile is denied, and the denial is audited
  • Under delegated or master-policy authority, the command proves product, territory, limit, period and referral constraints before confirmation; outside any one of them the state is REFERRAL_REQUIRED
  • Duplicate confirmations are idempotent; conflicting ones go to manual review. The latest message does not silently win
  • Policy registration references the completed bind order, the coverage evidence and the sourced product and wording versions
  • Every transaction pins the versions it used — product, wording, pricing and commission rule — so it can be reproduced exactly later (INV-006)
  • Every material transition emits an event with the minimum metadata of ARCH-001 §12, including the authority reference when the fact asserts delegated authority
  • A product is configuration, never code. A tenant that needs a different product gets a different version, not a branch; a version referenced by any transaction is immutable (P1C.1, ADR-006, ADR-011)
  • A price is labelled by the authority behind it. What the engine computes is an indication; what an insurer or a documented delegated authority returns is a quote; the label follows the source, never the code path (P1C.3, ADR-017)
  • Payment state cannot advance cover. The straight-through workflow hands off to P2 and receives a fact back; a paid premium with no insurer confirmation is a paid premium, not a policy (P1C.4, INV-020, ADR-018)
  • The broker's status and the insurer's status are two fields on a claim, a bind and a policy alike; the platform never writes the insurer's (P1B.6, INV-005)
Two operating models, one core

Professional brokerage runs risk, market, several quotes, broker advice, bind. Straight-through digital insurance runs customer input, eligibility, pricing, quote, acceptance, payment, insurer confirmation, policy or certificate. They differ by workflow configuration on the product version, not by code (roadmap section 5, restated in PLAN-001 section 5.3).

Surfaces this phase defines

The broker workstation grows from P1A.13 across P1A and P1B; the customer and agent mobile clients are P1C.6 and P1C.7 on the foundation of P1A.14; every surface drives the same contracts and performs no transition the API refuses.

A domain reviewer is not optional in P1

The Build stage lists a domain reviewer who must not be skipped because a change looks technical. In P1 every change is domain. The reviewer is someone who has placed insurance, and their review is scenario-based: they walk a bind under this tenant's arrangement and say whether the states mean what the business means.

Stop signal: something confirms cover from a user action

A button, a status change, an import or a payment callback that reaches CONFIRMED without authority evidence is the failure this phase exists to prevent, whatever it is called in the interface.

Stop signal: a price the broker computed is labelled a quote

It is an indication. The label is the defect; the fix is in the model, not the wording.

Stop signal: a product version was edited in place

Every prior transaction that pinned it is now unreproducible. Versions are immutable; changes are new versions.

Stop signal: an insurer's status is simulated

A broker-side record that asserts an insurer decision the insurer never made is a fabricated fact, however convenient for the demo.

Stop signal: a product became code

A conditional in application code that names a tenant or a product is a fork by another name; P1C exists so that it is configuration.

Weekly

Which step of the order is in progress and in which sub-phase; whether the domain reviewer has been in every review; whether any P2 concept — an invoice, a payment allocation, a ledger posting, a settlement — has appeared in a P1 branch.

06

Transition

Exit gates · BT-G2 Broker Core Ready and BT-G3 Lifecycle Ready (label C)

Label C is two gates. BT-G2 closes P1A on the vertical slice; BT-G3 closes P1B and P1C on the lifecycle's failure scenarios and the engine's reproduction. Both are recorded by a human; neither is recorded by a passing suite. The roadmap's Gate C list, restated in PLAN-001 section 10.3 as a proposal until the gate's owner accepts it, is the last block.

07

Halt

Stop conditions

  • The tenant authority profile for the pilot tenant is not accepted — the binding machine has no arrangement to implement
  • ADR-013 or ADR-017 is still BLOCKED_BY_S01 or DRAFT_REQUIRED
  • Any path reaches CONFIRMED without authority evidence, or renders an earlier state as cover
  • An insurer adjudication, acceptance or issuance is simulated on the broker side
  • A payment callback advances cover state, or a certificate issues under no recorded master-policy authority
  • A product or a tenant appears as a conditional in application code
  • Scope drifts into P2 — an invoice, a payment allocation, a ledger posting, a settlement — inside a P1 package
  • A domain reviewer was skipped on a change that touches state, provenance or disclosures

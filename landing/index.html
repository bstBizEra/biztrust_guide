Insurance infrastructure and distribution platform for Laos

BizTrust is being designed so that insurers, brokers and distribution channels in Laos can share one trusted infrastructure without any of them building the whole stack. Brokerage is one operating model on it, not the definition of it.

The architecture contract's own words: “BizTrust is a multi-tenant insurance brokerage, distribution and insurance API platform. It is not an insurer policy-administration or underwriting core.”

What BizTrust is being designed to become

One platform that several organisations would use at once, each within its own boundary, keeping its own records, and working with insurers through the same contracts. The design is meant to support five ways of operating on it.

Insurance software as a serviceAn organisation would run its insurance operations on BizTrust rather than on software it builds and hosts itself.
BrokerageA broker would manage clients, risks, submissions, placement, recommendations, policy records, claims advocacy and renewals.
Broker tenant platformA second and third brokerage would join without a copy of the platform being made for each.
Insurer agency and distributionWhere an insurer has granted the authority in writing, an organisation would distribute that insurer's products within the granted scope.
Interfaces for partnersOther systems would connect through published, versioned contracts rather than through private arrangements.

Source: architecture contract, sections 1, 6 and 10; capability map and product architecture. The five models are the positioning recorded on the landing brief and proposed to the glossary; the contract's sentence above is the definition of record until that changes.

Who it is designed for first

Two organisations are the first intended deployments of the platform model. They are its first uses, not its definition, and their arrangements are the first questions the design needs answered.

UniTrust, a corporate brokerage

Commercial insurance placed for business clients: several lines, several insurers, and the full lifecycle from submission to renewal.

A government-linked travel scheme

Travel cover issued to many people under one master policy, which raises the question of who has the authority to issue each certificate and what that obliges.

A request is not a confirmation

When a broker records that a customer asked for cover, that record says what was requested. It does not say that an insurer accepted the risk, issued the policy, or is now liable. BizTrust is designed to keep those two facts apart at every step, because a system that blurs them will eventually tell someone they are covered when they are not.

“An operating record of a request is not a confirmation that the request was granted. Where the two are conflated, the interface will eventually tell someone they are covered when they are not.”Handbook, Architect stage

This one rule shapes how quotations would be labelled (a broker's estimate is not an insurer's price), how policies would be recorded (a broker's record points at the insurer's document rather than replacing it), who is accountable for each fact, and which system is the owner of each record. It is why the design treats an insurer's confirmation as something received and kept as evidence, never something the platform could generate for itself.

Source: architecture contract, section 1 and invariant candidates INV-005, INV-016, INV-018 and INV-022.

Each organisation within its own scope, every decision with its evidence

The design gives each organisation on the platform its own boundary. People and systems would act only within the scope they have been granted, and an organisation's records would not be visible to another. Every action that matters is meant to be attributable to an authorised actor and supported by evidence that can be traced afterwards.

The design also separates two things that are easy to confuse: permission to use the platform, and the legal authority to create an insurance fact. Being allowed to press a button is never the same as being licensed or delegated to make cover effective. The platform is designed so that configuration alone cannot create that authority.

How the design would achieve this, from identity context to database-level isolation to audit records, is the implementation handbook's subject.

Source: architecture contract, invariant candidates INV-001 to INV-004, INV-007, INV-015 and INV-022; the handbook's flows document, section 3. Mechanisms: FLOWS.md.

What is decided, and what remains open

The architecture has been written down, sequenced, and mapped question by question. Nothing in it is accepted yet, and the first questions it is waiting on belong to the tenants, not the engineers.

Proposed and recorded, awaiting acceptance

Positions the architecture contract takes, each with an architecture decision record still to be reviewed.

  • Start as one application with strict internal boundaries, and split it only on evidence.ADR-001
  • Use a dedicated identity service; an organisation there would be the security boundary for a tenant here.ADR-002, ADR-003
  • One shared database with row-level isolation as the default, to be proven by tests that try to cross it.ADR-004
  • Broker records and insurer decisions are owned by different systems, and the platform is designed never to pretend otherwise.ADR-007
  • When cover starts and when it was recorded are two dates, and corrections never erase what was asserted before.ADR-015

Needs a tenant principal's answer before the contract can be frozen

Five questions, in plain language, each deciding the shape of stored data.

  • When your team confirms cover to a client, is that binding on the insurer at that moment, or must an insurer still say yes afterwards?Asked separately for each organisation
  • Before the premium reaches the insurer, whose money is it, and may commission be deducted first?
  • Does cover often start on a different date from the day it is typed in, and does it ever matter what the record said last week?
  • Is one risk ever placed with several insurers, by share or by layer?
  • When a figure is given to a client, is it the insurer's agreed price or the broker's estimate?

Source: the ADR register (twenty decisions registered, none accepted); the tenant authority questions on issue 15; the two decision maps, the contract set and the P0 design pack.

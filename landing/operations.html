After Production v1.0 the platform would be operated, not finished

Projects BIZTRUST-PLAN-001 1.0-draft, sections 8 and 11, BIZTRUST-ROADMAP-001 operator draft, sections 13 and 14, the P3 manual, section 07, and the Continuous Operations reference page, sections 01 to 06; read at e608e4b, 2026-09-06.

The plan's phases end at P3. What follows has no phase number, by rule. The plan's sentence, restated without its cross-reference: after BT-G7 is recorded and Production v1.0 is released, Continuous Operations runs: measure, learn, improve, expand. BT-G7 is the gate the plan labels E, Production Ready; its gate table asks for the security risk owner's dated record, and which human records a gate is, in the plan's words, the gate owner's record to name and never inferred.

The P3 manual says why the numbering stops: after that gate the platform is not finished; it is operated. Every package that ships from then on still runs the full lifecycle, and the operate and learn stages run continuously against the service objectives, the telemetry floor and the promotion bar. The phase track ends there; the stage track does not end. The roadmap's canonical names give the two things that follow their plain titles: Continuous Operations is measure, learn and improve; the expansion streams are scaling tenants, partners, integrations and intelligence.

Measure, learn, improve, expand

The plan names the loop in four words and no more. The Continuous Operations reference page reads each word against the handbook's stages so that the loop is not a fifth track to maintain, and marks that reading as the track's, not the plan's. The four cells below restate that reading in this page's words.

MeasureThe operate stage's service objectives and telemetry floor, read against the indicators and objectives P3 would have defined. The first number the loop reads is the error budget consumed per objective.
LearnThe learn stage: postmortems with their follow-up actions owned, and the promotion bar that decides what a package's evidence has earned.
ImproveA Work Package. Every change after v1.0 still runs the full lifecycle from discovery to learning; Continuous Operations changes what the packages are about, not how one moves.
ExpandA stream, opened on its recorded entry condition and then delivered as Work Packages like any other capability, under whatever gate its own charter names.

Source: BIZTRUST-PLAN-001 section 8 for the four words; the Continuous Operations reference page, reference/continuous-operations.html section 02, for the reading of each word against the Operate and Learn stages and the Work Package, which that page marks as the track's reading. The cells are this page's restatement of it.

Eight streams, no phase number

The roadmap's earlier drafts had proposed phases P4 to P9 for claims and renewal, a tenant pack kit, a partner API, workflow and event architecture, dedicated tenant isolation and artificial intelligence. Its section 13 keeps the capabilities and drops the numbers, converting them into streams that follow Production v1.0, because P3 already means Production. The plan carries the eight streams as its section 8, copied here cell for cell; the third column names the two epics of the previous plan that a stream would carry as its first item.

E1 Tenant ScaleTenant Pack SDK, white-labelling, product packsP3.11 Tenant Pack validation foundation
E2 Embedded InsuranceDeveloper portal, partner APIs, SDKsP3.10 Partner API and versioned webhooks

Two of the previous plan's finance-phase epics live on here. The tenant pack validation foundation, which would let a tenant extend the platform through signed configuration rather than forks or code, becomes E1's first item; the partner API with versioned, signed webhooks becomes E2's. The reference page notes what moved with the pack: the gate that proves a second tenant onboards without a fork, BT-G6 Tenant Ready, judged inside label E, no longer requires a pack. The pack is how E1 would make that repeatable at scale, not how P3 proves it once.

What each stream would wait on, and who records it

The plan's rule for opening a stream is one sentence: each opens only when BT-G7 is recorded, Production v1.0 is released, and its own entry condition is recorded; none is ordered among the others except as its condition names another; and none is authorised by the plan. The conditions are the plan's proposals, copied here cell for cell; the third column is the decision, an architecture decision record or a seat, that each waits on.

StreamEntry conditionDecision it waits on
E1 Tenant ScaleBT-G6 recorded, and a third tenant's authority profile under S01ADR-011
E2 Embedded InsuranceBT-G7 recorded, and a partner contract that names the API surface it needsADR-005, ADR-009
E3 Integration ScaleA counterparty (insurer, payment provider or bank) with a documented API and a signed sandbox agreementADR-009
E4 Workflow ScaleWorkflow volume or fault evidence from P1B and P2 showing the P0 runtime choice insufficientADR-001's extraction criteria; ADR-010
E5 Enterprise IsolationA tenant's regulatory or contractual requirement for a dedicated schema or database, recorded by the legal seatThe contract's section 18 dedicated-tenant trigger; ADR-004; ADR-019
E6 Advanced BrokerageP1B operating data on one tenant covering a full renewal cycle (a measurement window, not a schedule), or a second tenant's need recorded—
E7 IntelligenceThe data-governance track's owner, classification and consent rows exist for every dataset a model would readADR-019; the compliance track
E8 EcosystemE2 open and at least one partner live—

In plain words: tenant scale waits for a third tenant whose arrangement with its insurers has been answered; embedded insurance for a partner with a contract that says which interface it needs; integration scale for a counterparty that documents its interface and signs for a sandbox; workflow scale for evidence from real volume or real faults that the runtime chosen in P0 is no longer enough; enterprise isolation for a tenant whose regulator or contract demands its own schema or database, recorded by the legal seat; advanced brokerage for a full renewal cycle of operating data on one tenant, or a second tenant's recorded need; intelligence for governance rows on every dataset a model would read; and the ecosystem for embedded insurance to be open with one partner live.

Two conditions name a gate, and the reference page is careful about what that means: E1 waits on BT-G6 and E2 on BT-G7, which are P3's gates, recorded by their own owners on P3's evidence; a stream's condition cites them as facts it needs, and a stream opening records nothing about them. What gate, if any, would close a stream is the stream's own charter's to say, and no charter exists.

Where the streams stand

No stream is openEvery stream waits on BT-G7, Production Ready, label E, and on its own condition; neither the gate nor any condition is recorded. The handbook's continuity state records architecture acceptance and production platform implementation as not granted.
None is ordered or designedThe plan puts the ordering and design of the streams out of its scope until Production v1.0 exists; the reference page orders none and designs none.
None has a charterWhat gate closes a stream, and where its packages land, belong to a charter each stream would write when it opens. The reference page carries no candidate locations for that reason.

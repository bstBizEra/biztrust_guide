The architecture: a contract that can be violated

Projects BIZTRUST-ARCH-001 0.1-draft, sections 1, 4, 5, 6, 16, 17 and 18; read at 34bb72e, 2026-09-06.

BizTrust's architecture is written as a contract: vocabulary, ownership, boundaries and invariants that a test could show were broken. It is a draft for contract freeze, proposed and not yet accepted, and it grants no authority to implement anything. Its position, in the contract's own sentence, is the definition of record:

BizTrust is a multi-tenant insurance brokerage, distribution and insurance API platform. It is not an insurer policy-administration or underwriting core.

Source: BIZTRUST-ARCH-001, header (version 0.1-draft; status draft for contract freeze; implementation authority not granted by this document) and section 1, the position sentence quoted.

A broker core, beside an insurer's core it never pretends to be

The contract draws two sides. BizTrust would coordinate the broker's side of the lifecycle: clients, risks, submissions, placement, quotes, recommendations, binding coordination, broker policy records, servicing, claims advocacy, renewals, premium collection, commissions, settlement, reconciliation and partner distribution. Insurers would normally remain authoritative for underwriting decisions, risk acceptance, issued insurance contracts, insurer policy records, claim adjudication, insurer reserves and insurer accounting.

A tenant could perform a narrowly scoped authoritative act only where an accepted authority profile and an operative agreement prove delegated or master-policy authority; the contract says application configuration cannot create that authority. The overview's principle, that a request is not a confirmation, is this same line, drawn there from the reader's side and here from the contract's; the comparison is this page's.

Source: BIZTRUST-ARCH-001, section 1, second and third paragraphs. The roadmap's doctrine of a broker core and an insurer core is the operator's draft (roadmap section 2); the doctrine ticket on the Guide v2 map (#158) would carry it into the contract, and until it does the contract's sentence above is the definition of record.

The shape: three planes over an integration plane, and the capability map behind them

The roadmap draws BizTrust as a control plane, a broker core and an API platform, all standing on an integration plane that faces insurers, banks, payment providers and partners. The contract's capability map is the record those planes summarise: four domain groups of candidate modules, which the contract says are logical ownership boundaries to refine in domain workshops and not a count of services.

How it would be built, and what BizTrust would own

The proposed style is a modular monolith behind explicit module contracts, supported by external infrastructure services; a logical boundary would not automatically become a separately deployed service, and a module could be extracted only on evidence of a need such as independent scaling, fault isolation, regulatory or security separation, team ownership or materially different availability. That is the first of twenty decisions, proposed and not accepted.

What BizTrust should own, the contract says, is the broker insurance domain model; the versioned insurance product model; placement, advice and broker workflow semantics; the premium, commission and settlement subledger model; and the tenant-pack and insurer, payment and partner interoperability contracts. Identity, gateway, workflow runtime, messaging, database and observability should remain replaceable infrastructure behind adapters and operational contracts.

Source: BIZTRUST-ARCH-001, section 4.1 (the proposed style, marked as ADR-001's proposed decision) and section 4.2 (strategic intellectual property, its list and its closing sentence).

Twenty-two invariants, in plain words

An invariant is a claim that something is always true, and the contract keeps only claims whose violation a test could catch. All twenty-two are candidates until the architecture authority accepts them. In plain words, and in the contract's order:

  1. INV-001 Every business record a tenant owns has exactly one tenant owner.
  2. INV-002 A tenant's authority comes from validated identity, never from an identifier a client sends on its own.
  3. INV-003 One tenant cannot read or change another tenant's protected data.
  4. INV-004 The application's authorization and PostgreSQL's row-level security each enforce isolation on their own.
  5. INV-005 A broker's records never impersonate an insurer's decisions or contracts.
  6. INV-006 A past transaction keeps the exact product, wording, pricing and commission-rule versions it used.
  7. INV-007 Every material insurance or financial transition can be attributed and reconstructed later.
  8. INV-008 A posted ledger record is never edited; a correction is a reversal or a compensating entry.
  9. INV-009 Retrying a financial or binding operation is safe.
  10. INV-010 A provider's particular behaviour stays behind an adapter.
  11. INV-011 A second tenant would join without a copy of the platform's code being made for it.
  12. INV-012 Every public and inter-module interface is a versioned contract written first.
  13. INV-013 A long-running workflow survives duplicate messages, delay, restart and partial failure.
  14. INV-014 A sensitive action needs an explicit permission and, where configured, an approver's authority.
  15. INV-015 No claim that something is implemented or compliant is accepted without evidence bound to a revision.
  16. INV-016 Every representation of effective cover names the legal authority, the effective period and the kept evidence behind it.
  17. INV-017 For any fact that affects cover or money's effect, when it took effect and when it was recorded are two times, and a correction never erases what was asserted before.
  18. INV-018 A product, a wording, a rating, a broker's indication, an insurer's quote and a delegated offer each keep their authority and provenance.
  19. INV-019 One risk or contract placed across insurer shares, layers or certificates is still one, neither duplicated nor overwritten.
  20. INV-020 A payment's state cannot create a state of cover; a premium condition that affects cover is judged by the workflow that knows who holds authority.
  21. INV-021 Client money and office money stay distinguishable in every balance, movement and reconciliation, under the approved regime.
  22. INV-022 Permission to use the platform is never the same as legal, insurer-delegated or scheme authority.

Source: BIZTRUST-ARCH-001, section 5, all twenty-two rows, each restated in plain words by this page; the identifiers and the order are the contract's, the words are not. The contract's own wording and the mechanical proof it expects for each are in the section.

What the contract must answer before it can be frozen

The roadmap's Gate A asks fourteen questions of the architecture: who owns each datum; which tenant owns each record; who may perform each operation; which module owns each state transition; what the authoritative insurance state is; what the authoritative financial state is; how a quote becomes bound coverage; how money becomes premium; how commission is calculated and posted; how a carrier integrates; how Tenant #2 onboards without a fork; how cross-tenant isolation is mechanically proved; how failures are recovered; how a transaction is reconstructed from evidence. The contract's own exit criteria are seventeen, and they govern; the fourteen correspond to them and are fewer. The contract may be marked accepted only when reviewers can answer every one of these without relying on undocumented behaviour:

  1. Who owns every major data class?
  2. Which tenant owns every protected business record?
  3. What establishes tenant authority?
  4. Who may perform each sensitive operation, over which scope and with whose approval?
  5. Which module owns each state transition?
  6. What is broker-authoritative and what remains insurer-authoritative?
  7. Under each tenant arrangement, who may legally conclude cover and within what scope?
  8. How does a submission become authority-supported effective cover and then an issued policy representation?
  9. How are effective time, record time, backdating, future dating, correction and supersession represented?
  10. How are insurer shares, layers, facilities, master policies, certificates and bordereaux represented?
  11. How are insurer product/wording/rating, broker distribution configuration, broker indication and insurer quote distinguished?
  12. How does a payment become an allocated premium without bypassing insurance authority?
  13. Whose money is held at each point, which account class contains it, and which reconciliation proves it?
  14. How are ledger, commission and settlement results reproduced historically?
  15. How do external integrations fail without corrupting state?
  16. How does Tenant #2 onboard without a core-code fork?
  17. How is every material transaction reconstructed from evidence?

Critical findings must be closed or explicitly accepted by the correct human risk owner; test success does not self-approve the architecture.

Source: BIZTRUST-ARCH-001, section 17, the seventeen criteria verbatim and the closing two sentences; the roadmap's section 3 Gate A for the fourteen questions; BIZTRUST-PLAN-001, section 10.3, for the correspondence ("they correspond to, and are fewer than, the contract's seventeen section 17 exit criteria; the contract's list governs").

What is decided, and what is not

Twenty decisions registered, none accepted

The contract names the architecture decision records it requires, twenty as the map counts them, from a modular monolith first to how endorsement, cancellation, refund, commission clawback and renewal are modelled as historical transactions.

  • Fourteen require a draft.ADR-001 to ADR-012, ADR-015, ADR-020, marked DRAFT_REQUIRED
  • Six wait on the first slice of the freeze, the tenant questions the overview lists.ADR-013, ADR-014, ADR-016 to ADR-019, marked BLOCKED_BY_S01
  • The register says in its own status line that no ADR is accepted by that file; a mention in the handbook or the contract is not acceptance.

The questions the freeze must resolve

From the initial operating jurisdiction and licensing model to build-versus-adopt criteria for workflow, messaging and fine-grained authorization; among them the money precision, day count, rounding, tax, risk-transfer and client-money rules, the authority and evidence required before coverage is shown as active, and the data residency, retention and deletion requirements.

  • Questions that change legal authority, money ownership or stored-data shape cannot be waived by an implementation team.The contract's own rule
  • The freeze itself is the first slice of one Work Package, and its next step is an operator's waiver on the contract map before any drafting ahead of the tenant answers.NS-041, the handbook's primary next action

The roadmap: five phases to Production v1.0, and what follows

Projects BIZTRUST-PLAN-001 1.0-draft, sections 2, 8, 10 and 11; read at 94f35ea, 2026-09-06.

BizTrust is designed to be delivered in five phases, one after another, each closed by a gate that a named human records on evidence. No phase is authorised as a whole: work inside it is granted one Work Package at a time, after the gate before it is recorded. What each phase is for is a kind of trust, and the order is the order in which that trust would have to exist.

Source: BIZTRUST-PLAN-001, section 2 (the five phases) and section 10 (a gate is recorded by a human; failure at any gate blocks dependent authorisation).

After P3 comes Continuous Operations: measure, learn, improve, expand. It is an operating lifecycle, not a sixth phase, and nothing after P3 carries a phase number, because a number would imply a sequencing commitment nobody has reviewed.

Source: BIZTRUST-PLAN-001, section 2, the table cell for cell and the paragraph after it.

The gates a human records, and the labels a presentation may use

Records name gates by identifier, from BT-G0 (label A) to BT-G7 (label E). A presentation such as this page may also use the roadmap's five executive labels, A to E, but never a label without its identifier; two gates share a label, and one gate is judged inside another's.

LabelRoadmap nameIdentifiersCloses

CInsurance End-to-End ProvenBT-G2 and BT-G3P1 (P1A at BT-G2; P1B and P1C at BT-G3)

EProduction ReadyBT-G7, with BT-G6 judged inside itP3

BT-G6, Tenant Ready, keeps its identifier and its meaning, that a second tenant would onboard without a copy of the platform being made, because an invariant depends on it; it is recorded before or together with BT-G7, never skipped because label E does not name it. Each gate names the evidence that blocks it: for BT-G1, negative tests that prove one tenant cannot reach another; for BT-G2, an end-to-end broker trace and a practitioner's acceptance; for BT-G4, balanced journals and reconciliation evidence; for BT-G7, revision-bound evidence for every item of the production list, an independent readiness review, and the security risk owner's dated record. A passing test suite records nothing; a human does.

Source: BIZTRUST-PLAN-001, section 10.1 (the labels table cell for cell and the BT-G6 rule) and section 10.2 (the gate table's requirement and evidence columns for BT-G1, BT-G2, BT-G4 and BT-G7, paraphrased; "a gate is recorded by a human ... a passing test suite records nothing").

What each label asks for before it can be recorded

A, Architecture ReadyThe architecture can answer who owns each datum, which tenant owns each record, who may perform each operation, which module owns each state transition, what the authoritative insurance and financial states are, how a quote becomes bound coverage, how money becomes premium, how commission is calculated and posted, how a carrier integrates, how a second tenant onboards without a fork, how isolation is proved, how failures are recovered and how a transaction is reconstructed from evidence; the contract's own exit criteria govern, and are more.
B, Identity and Tenant ProvenThe chain from authenticated identity through validated tenant context, authorised operation and tenant-isolated data access to immutable audit evidence is demonstrated mechanically, and the P0 proof passes independently of the people who built it.
C, Insurance End-to-End ProvenOne trace from tenant to client, risk, submission, placement, quotes, comparison, recommendation, acceptance, bind coordination, carrier confirmation and policy, then endorsement, claim and renewal, proven with authorisation, product and version reproducibility, tenant isolation, deterministic state transitions, an audit trail, web and mobile coverage, API contracts, documents, and the insurer's authority kept separate from the broker's records.
D, Financial Integrity ProvenOne trace from customer to invoice, payment, provider confirmation, ledger, commission, insurer payable, settlement, reconciliation and policy evidence, and ten failure cases proven handled, among them a duplicate payment, a missing webhook and a carrier settlement difference.
E, Production ReadyRevision-bound evidence for eighteen things, from security and tenant isolation through backup restore, disaster recovery and rollback to runbooks, on-call and compliance evidence; a readiness review by someone other than the implementers; the security risk owner's dated record.

Source: BIZTRUST-PLAN-001, section 10.3, each label's list paraphrased in fewer words with every item kept; the counts, ten failure cases and eighteen items, are the P2 and P3 manuals' (phases/p2.html, phases/p3.html); the lists themselves are proposals until each gate's owner accepts them.

P2 would close the loop from insurance to money to insurer, with records that can be reconciled

Projects BIZTRUST-PLAN-001 1.0-draft, sections 6, 10 and 12.1, BIZTRUST-ROADMAP-001 operator draft, section 6, and the P2 manual, sections 01, 02 and 04 to 07; read at 530f7b2, 2026-09-06.

The third phase is where money enters the platform. Its objective, in the plan's words, is to close the insurance-to-money-to-insurer loop with attributable, reconcilable records, so that payment, ledger, commission, settlement and reconciliation are each their own thing. The roadmap gives the phase its fuller name: payment, ledger, commission, settlement and reconciliation.

The plan's label table gives the phase one executive label and two gates: label D, Financial Integrity Proven, is BT-G4 Financial Ready and BT-G5 Integration Ready. The manual's first entry condition is that the previous phase's two gates, BT-G2 and BT-G3, label C, are recorded. Its reason is the phase's whole discipline in one sentence: P2 posts money against workflows that have already been shown to survive duplicates and conflicts; it does not add durability to them, and it does not add cover to anything.

The first sentence of the first paragraph is this page's.

Ten concepts that must never collapse into one object

The roadmap's core rule for the phase is a list, and the plan carries it as P2's design constraint. Each of the ten is a separate record with its own life; the failure the rule prevents is the one where a single "payment" row is asked to say what was owed, what was tried, what arrived, what it paid for, and what the insurer is due, and ends up saying none of them reliably.

  1. Invoice
  2. Payment
  3. Payment Intent
  4. Payment Attempt
  5. Payment Allocation
  6. Ledger
  7. Commission
  8. Settlement
  9. Reconciliation
  10. Refund

In plain words: an invoice says what is owed and when. A payment intent says someone means to pay it; an attempt is one try, by one method, which may fail; a payment is what actually arrived. An allocation says which debt the money that arrived pays, in whole or in part. The ledger is the double-entry record of every movement. Commission is the intermediary's share, computed under a versioned agreement. Settlement is the insurer's money going to the insurer. Reconciliation is the comparison of the platform's records with the provider's, the bank's and the insurer's. A refund is money going back, with its own life and its own duplicates to guard against. The manual's stop conditions include storing two of the ten as one.

Source: the roadmap's section 6, the core rule, copied name for name and held by tests/test_showcase_p2.py; BIZTRUST-PLAN-001 section 6 for the rule carried as P2's design constraint; the P2 manual's section 07 for two of the ten stored as one as a stop condition. The plain-words paragraph and the description of the failure the rule prevents are this page's.

The transaction flow, and the trace Gate D would demand

The roadmap draws P2's transaction flow as one spine with two fan-outs. An insurance transaction raises an invoice; the invoice gets a payment intent; a payment orchestrator offers the intent to a bank, a QR scheme, a card or a wallet; the provider's confirmation is verified, then allocated; the allocation posts to a double-entry ledger, which feeds premium, commission and settlement; reconciliation compares what was posted with what the outside world says; and the result goes back to the insurance workflow. The roadmap's Gate D turns that flow into the trace the phase must be able to show for one activity, copied here in its order.

  1. Customer
  2. Insurance Transaction
  3. Invoice
  4. Payment
  5. Provider Confirmation
  6. Ledger
  7. Commission
  8. Insurer Payable
  9. Settlement
  10. Reconciliation
  11. Policy/Insurance Evidence

The plan keeps a second sentence as P2's exit condition, carried word for word from the previous plan: end-to-end evidence must trace customer/partner activity through indication/offer, payment, authority-supported coverage confirmation, policy representation, ledger, commission, settlement and reconciliation. The manual renders it as the last item of its exit checklist.

Source: the roadmap's section 6, the P2 transaction flow, for the spine and the two fan-outs, paraphrased in the roadmap's order in this page's words; the roadmap's Gate D block for the trace, copied step for step and held by tests/test_showcase_p2.py; BIZTRUST-PLAN-001 section 6 for the end-to-end evidence sentence quoted and for the manual rendering it as the last exit item.

Money that cannot invent cover

The contract's invariant candidate INV-020 reads: payment state cannot invent coverage state; any premium condition affecting cover is evaluated by the authority-aware insurance workflow. The manual builds the phase around one sequence every reviewer would walk, the P2 equivalent of P0's bypass test: what happens when a payment provider calls back to say money has arrived. The six steps below are this page's grouping of the manual's sequence.

1. Verify, then deduplicateThe provider's signature is checked and the event deduplicated by the provider's own identifier before anything else reads it.
2. Record a fact about the paymentA payment-confirmed event is emitted. It says nothing about cover.
3. Ask the binding moduleThe workflow queries binding for authority and coverage-confirmation state; it does not decide either itself.
4. Without confirmation, waitIf authoritative confirmation is missing: wait, retry or escalate. No coverage transition. The premium is held under the accepted money regime, not applied to a cover that does not exist.
5. With valid authority, register and postIf authority evidence and the effective period are valid, policy registration or activation is requested from the policy module, and on the policy's own coverage-changed event an approved journal posting is requested from the ledger.
6. If payment succeeded and binding failedThe approved compensation policy decides hold, void or refund. The workflow does not invent a financial outcome.

The manual's first stop signal is the same rule stated as a failure: a payment callback that activated cover is the exact failure INV-020 names, and the fix is not a check in the callback but the workflow that should have been between them.

Source: BIZTRUST-ARCH-001 section 5 for INV-020's text, quoted; the P2 manual's section 04, the safety workflow, for the steps restated in this page's grouping and words; its section 05 for the stop signal on a payment callback activating cover, with its sentence on where the fix lies.

The ledger, commission, settlement and reconciliation, in plain words

Four of the ten concepts carry most of the phase's proof. The plain words beneath each are this page's; the lists are copied from the roadmap.

The ledger

A double-entry record that the platform would own, and that can only grow. Every journal balances or is rejected whole; a record once posted cannot be updated or deleted, at the database itself and not only in the application; every entry keeps the rule versions that were in force when it was made; and money the intermediary holds for clients is never held in the same class, or netted against, the intermediary's own. The regime that decides whose money the intermediary holds and when premium counts as received by the insurer is a jurisdiction-dependent decision the manual reserves to qualified legal and finance reviewers, and until it is accepted, the manual says, a generic premium journal is not evidence.

Commission

The intermediary's share, computed under a versioned agreement rather than a formula in code, so that a commission computed a year ago recomputes to the same figure from the inputs kept with it. The roadmap's engine would support broker commission, the insurer commission agreement, agent, sub-broker and referral commission, revenue share, incentives, overrides, clawbacks and reversals.

Settlement

The insurer's money reaching the insurer. In plain words: premium collected from a client is allocated to the invoice it pays; the part that belongs to the insurer becomes a payable to that insurer; the intermediary's commission is taken under its agreement; what is due is gathered into a settlement batch; the batch is remitted to the carrier; and the remittance is reconciled against what the carrier confirms. The roadmap draws it so:

  1. Premium Collected
  2. Allocation
  3. Insurer Payable
  4. Commission
  5. Settlement Batch
  6. Carrier Remittance
  7. Reconciliation

Reconciliation

The comparison of the platform's records with the payment provider's, the bank statement's and the carrier's settlement. In plain words: each money fact the platform holds is set beside the provider's confirmation, the bank's statement entry and the carrier's settlement; when key and amount agree on every side the item is matched; when a fact is present on one side only, or the amount or the count disagrees, the difference is named by its status, given an owner, and closed only when the owner's decision and the adjustment it produced are recorded. Each outcome carries one of the roadmap's statuses:

  1. MATCHED
  2. UNMATCHED
  3. AMOUNT_MISMATCH
  4. MISSING_INTERNAL
  5. MISSING_PROVIDER
  6. DUPLICATE
  7. PENDING_INVESTIGATION
  8. RESOLVED

The manual adds one rule the roadmap does not state: a period closes on a bank statement, and a notification only advances a status, because a reconciliation marked matched from a callback or a QR acknowledgement with no statement entry behind it has reconciled the platform to itself.

Source: the roadmap's section 6, P2B for the platform owning an immutable double-entry subledger and historical records preserving rule versions, P2C for the commission engine's list, P2D for the settlement chain and P2E for the four things compared and the eight statuses, the two lists copied step for step and held by tests/test_showcase_p2.py; the P2 manual's section 06 for balanced journals rejected whole, posted records immutable at the database level, a historical commission recomputing from retained inputs, and client-money and office-money classes kept distinct; its section 01 for ADR-014's questions, its jurisdiction-dependent marking and the sentence that a generic premium journal is not evidence; its section 04 for the reading of matched, owned and resolved differences and for a notification only advancing a status, and its section 05 for the stop signal on a period closed on a notification, both restated. Gate D is two gates, each with its own record-keeper

Label D closes on two records. BT-G4 Financial Ready is the money's own proof: the journals, the reconciliations, the commission and the trace. BT-G5 Integration Ready is the proof that the outside world cannot corrupt it: replayed, tampered and out-of-order provider events, two adapters against one contract, and the roadmap's failure cases each run as a test. The manual's rule: each is recorded separately, by a human, and the phase is not complete until both are.

DBT-G4 Financial Ready and BT-G5 Integration Ready

The trace customer, insurance transaction, invoice, payment, provider confirmation, ledger, commission, insurer payable, settlement, reconciliation, policy evidence, is proven, and the roadmap's failure cases are proven handled: that is the plan's Gate D as it proposes it, until each gate's owner accepts it. The plan's seat table, marked as its proposal, has the broker finance and accounting reviewer recording BT-G4 with the release authority, and the security risk owner recording BT-G5; the manual proposes the same, adding the integration owner beside the security risk owner.

The failure cases are the roadmap's, restated by the plan; the manual makes each one a test and places each under an epic, a placement it marks as its own, with the provider-facing ones run against a provider sandbox and the evidence bound to a revision. They are copied here in the roadmap's order.

What the two seats would have to see is the manual's gate section. For BT-G4: every journal balancing and an unbalanced draft rejected whole with the rejection recorded; a posted record that cannot be changed at the database; all three reconciliations detecting a planted difference; a historical commission recomputed to the same figure; client and office money distinct in every balance and movement; a period closed on a statement with both keys on every entry; and the Gate D trace end to end. For BT-G5: replayed, tampered and out-of-order provider events unable to repeat or invent a mutation; two adapters passing the same contract tests without touching the domain core; the failure cases each passed; no provider vocabulary in any domain schema; and the end-to-end evidence sentence met. The manual says two of those items, the statement close and the vocabulary rule, are its own additions to the roadmap's gate.

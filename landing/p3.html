P3 would prove that the whole system can be operated, from working software to reliable service

Projects BIZTRUST-PLAN-001 1.0-draft, sections 7, 10 and 12.1, BIZTRUST-ROADMAP-001 operator draft, section 7, and the P3 manual, sections 01, 02 and 04 to 08; read at 7c19afc, 2026-09-06.

The last phase builds almost no features. Its objective, in the plan's words, is to prove that the whole system can be operated, from working software to reliable service. The roadmap says the same from the other side: P3 is not primarily feature development, and, citing its own engineering roadmap, it defines P3 as the transition from a working application to an operationally sustainable service. The previous plan had no production phase; two P0 epics, the observability baseline and secrets management, laid the baselines that P3 would scale.

The plan's label table gives the phase one executive label, E, Production Ready, and one gate, BT-G7 Production Ready, with a second, BT-G6 Tenant Ready, judged inside it. The manual's first entry condition is that the previous phase's two gates, BT-G4 and BT-G5, label D, are recorded, and its reason sets the phase's boundary: P3 operates a system whose financial and integration evidence already exists; it does not produce that evidence, and it does not build features to fill gaps the earlier gates should have found.

Measure before you release, restore before you promise

The manual puts two things before any release gate, and they are entry conditions rather than epics to run in parallel. The first is the service objectives. An indicator is the measure, an objective is the target on it, and the error budget is the rate at which the objective may be missed; in the manual's reading of its sources, release velocity is coupled to that budget and a written budget policy names actions and owners. A release gate cannot be defined before the indicators and objectives it would judge by exist, so the objectives epic is a precondition of the release-engineering epic. The manual's first stop signal is the failure the order prevents: a release that went out with no objective to judge it by, where a canary with nothing to evaluate against is a deployment with a delay.

The second is recovery. Recovery point and recovery time are two numbers, and the second is bounded by a third, the maximum tolerable downtime that the business would have to state; the manual's source for those numbers places backup and recovery strategy in development, not after launch, and the manual asks that all three be stated per system with the source of the downtime figure. The roadmap's own sentence is the shortest form: a backup is not considered a DR control until restore has been tested.

Source: the P3 manual's section 01 for the two entry conditions, the definitions of indicator, objective and error budget, P3F.1 as a precondition of P3C.1, and the three recovery numbers with their placement in the lifecycle; its section 05 for the stop signal on a release with no objective; the roadmap's section 7, P3G, for the sentence quoted. The paragraphing and the phrase about what the business would have to state are this page's.

What operating the system would mean, in plain words

The groups the epic table lists after the pipeline, less the objectives already covered above, are the controls a reliable service would run on. Each is described here in this page's words from the roadmap's lists and the manual's definitions; the roadmap's own lists are longer than these summaries.

Release engineeringFeature flags that roll a change out gradually and revert it alone; a canary that serves part of the traffic for a bounded time and is judged against the rest; blue-green, where two instances stand ready and rollback is a reversal of the routing; rolling deployment and rollback under definitions the manual has to supply itself; and compatibility of the database, the APIs and the mobile clients across a release.
ObservabilityEvery meaningful transaction would produce a log, a metric, a trace and an audit event, with a dashboard signal and an alert when needed, so that one activity can be reconstructed by correlation from the mobile client through APISIX, insurance, payment, the provider, the ledger and the policy workflow.
ReliabilityScaling out, queueing, retries with backoff, circuit breakers, dead-letter processing, database replication, backup with restore actually tested, failover, handling of a degraded provider, and graceful degradation.
Disaster recoveryA recovery point and a recovery time per system, the recovery time derived from a tolerable downtime the business states and the recovery point separate from it, and a restore performed on an alternate platform with the time measured against the objective; a backup log is not that record.
Mobile productionDeveloper build, internal test, quality assurance, beta, store review, production and crash and performance monitoring; a minimum supported version and a forced security upgrade. Neither store offers a rollback for a released build, so the evidence is a halted rollout and a fix shipped forward.
Security operationsContinuous detection of abnormal authentication, cross-tenant anomalies, privilege escalation, API abuse, payment anomalies, suspicious refund activity, fraud indicators, vulnerable dependencies, exposed secrets and configuration drift.

  1. Detect
  2. Triage
  3. Contain
  4. Restore
  5. Investigate
  6. Correct
  7. Postmortem
  8. Prevent Recurrence

Three words the sources do not define

The plan records that its production epics rest on definitions sourced by the map's research, and that the research found no primary source for three of them: the incident severity scale, rolling deployment, and rollback outside the blue-green case. The plan's rule is that each is a local decision and must be labelled one; the manual labels each where it defines it, and its definitions are proposals until the seat the manual names accepts them.

In plain words, the manual's local definitions say: a rolling deployment replaces the serving version in successive subsets of instances, checking the indicators between subsets, with both versions serving until the last subset; a rollback returns production to the previous known-good release artifact and its compatible configuration by redeploying that artifact, never by editing what is running, with data changes rolled forward by a compensating migration; and a severity is assigned from written criteria, because the roadmap's four names alone mean whatever the assigner meant. The manual's stop signals name the failures: a rollback that edited production, and a severity assigned from the names alone.

Source: BIZTRUST-PLAN-001 section 7, the paragraph after the epic table, for the three findings and the rule that each local definition is labelled; the P3 manual's section 04 for the definitions of rolling deployment and rollback marked as local decisions, restated here in this page's words, and its section 05 for the three local definitions as an authorisation reserved to a human and for the two stop signals named.

Revision-bound evidence exists for every item of the list; the production-readiness review is executed by someone other than the implementers; the security risk owner records the gate with a date: that is the plan's Gate E as it proposes it, until the gate's owner accepts it. BT-G6 is recorded before or together with it, never skipped because the label does not name it: a second tenant provisions without a core-code fork, the P0 proof passes against both tenants, and no tenant-specific branch, directory or code path exists.

The plan adds four things to the roadmap's gate and labels them its proposals: every item bound to a source revision; BT-G6 recorded; a production-readiness review executed by someone other than the implementers; and the security risk owner's dated record. The manual proposes the seats: the security risk owner with the production-readiness reviewer for BT-G7, and the architecture authority for BT-G6; the plan's seat table, marked as its proposal, has the same two seats, the security risk owner and the accountable architecture owner, on the same two gates.

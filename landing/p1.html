P1 would build the broker-native insurance platform, in three controlled sub-phases

Projects BIZTRUST-PLAN-001 1.0-draft, sections 5, 10 and 12.1, BIZTRUST-ROADMAP-001 operator draft, section 5, and the P1 manual, sections 01, 02 and 04 to 07; read at 21b1d3e, 2026-09-06.

The second phase is where insurance enters the platform. Its objective, in the plan's words, is to build the broker-native insurance operating platform as one macro-phase with three controlled sub-phases, so that the previous plan's P1 and P2 keep their content and the product engine the roadmap adds has a home. The sub-phases are P1A, the broker core; P1B, the professional brokerage lifecycle; and P1C, the product engine and digital insurance.

The plan's label table gives the phase one executive label and two gates: label C, Insurance End-to-End Proven, is BT-G2 Broker Core Ready, which closes P1A, and BT-G3 Lifecycle Ready, which closes P1B and P1C. The manual's first entry condition is that the previous phase's gate, BT-G1, label B, has been recorded independently: in its words, P1 puts real business records on the substrate, and the substrate must already be proven, not promised.

Two sentences in the plan set the bar for this slice: automation of insurer APIs is not required for P1; correct state, evidence and ownership are required. Three boundaries the platform must never blur

The plan states three boundaries P1 must keep, drawn from the roadmap and from three of the contract's invariant candidates, and notes that the landing page states them as one principle: a request is not a confirmation.

A broker bind request is not a carrier bind confirmationAsking an insurer to bind cover would be a broker's record. Only the insurer, or a documented delegated authority, could turn it into cover.
A broker policy record is not the insurer's authoritative policyThe platform would keep the broker's representation of a policy. The policy itself is the insurer's.
A broker claim status is not the insurer's adjudication statusWhat the broker has done for a claim and what the insurer has decided about it are two different facts, and the platform would never write the second.

The manual turns those boundaries into rules a reviewer checks first. In plain words: the states a bind passes through before confirmation do not mean insured, and no screen or document may show them as cover. A confirmed cover would have to carry the authority it rests on, who issued it, when it runs from and to, when it was recorded, and the evidence kept for it, as required fields. Where the tenant's arrangement with its insurers allows requests only, only the insurer's own evidence could confirm cover: a broker user holding the permission to confirm but no applicable authority profile would be refused, and the refusal recorded. Where a delegated or master-policy authority exists, the platform would prove the product, territory, limit, period and referral limits before confirming, and anything outside them would go to referral. A price the engine computes is an indication; only an insurer, or a documented delegated authority, gives a quote. And a paid premium with no insurer confirmation is a paid premium, not a policy.

Source: BIZTRUST-PLAN-001 section 5.4 for the three boundaries, their origin in the roadmap's section 5 and in the contract's invariant candidates INV-005, INV-016 and INV-022, and its note that the landing page states them; the roadmap's section 5, the important authority boundary, for the three pairs in its words; the P1 manual's section 04 for the binding rules restated in the plain-words paragraph, which is this page's wording, as are the glosses beneath the three boundaries.

Two operating models on one core

The roadmap's reason for a product engine is that insurance products must not become hard-coded application branches; the engine then enables two ways of selling insurance on one platform. The professional model is a broker working a market for a client; the straight-through model is a customer buying a configured product directly. Both chains are copied from the roadmap.

Professional brokerage

  1. Risk
  2. Market
  3. Multiple Quotes
  4. Broker Advice
  5. Bind

Straight-through digital insurance

  1. Customer Input
  2. Eligibility
  3. Pricing
  4. Quote
  5. Acceptance
  6. Payment
  7. Insurer API
  8. Bind Confirmation
  9. Policy / Certificate

In the roadmap's words: same BizTrust core; different workflow configuration. The plan states the condition: products are versioned configuration rather than hard-coded branches. The manual sharpens it into rules: a product is configuration, never code, and every transaction pins the versions it used so that it can be reproduced later. Two of the manual's stop signals are the same rule seen from the other side: a product that became code, and a version edited in place. The straight-through chain's payment step is a hand-off; the plan's row for that workflow says payment execution is P2's.

Web for the broker, mobile for the customer and the agent

The roadmap draws a broker workstation on the web and two mobile clients. The plan does not make the workstation an epic: it is the surface the basic broker portal, P1A.13, grows into across P1A and P1B, and the roadmap's list of what it shows runs from the dashboard and client 360 through risk, products, submission, placement, quotes, recommendation, binding, policy, endorsement, renewal and claims to documents, insurers, agents, compliance and reports.

The two mobile clients are P1C's. The customer client would let a person discover, compare, quote, apply, hold a policy and certificate, make a claim, renew and receive notifications; the agent client would support prospect onboarding, client verification, quote support, document capture, servicing, policy lookup and claim assistance. Both stand on the mobile identity foundation, P1A.14. The roadmap keeps a pay step in the customer client and marks it: payment execution becomes authoritative in P2.

Gate C is two gates, each recorded by a human

Label C closes on two records. BT-G2 Broker Core Ready closes P1A when the vertical slice above runs end to end as one trace; BT-G3 Lifecycle Ready closes P1B and P1C when the lifecycle's failure scenarios and the engine's reproduction have been shown. The manual's sentence is the rule: both are recorded by a human; neither is recorded by a passing suite.

CBT-G2 Broker Core Ready and BT-G3 Lifecycle Ready

The trace tenant, client, risk, submission, placement, quotes, comparison, recommendation, acceptance, bind coordination, carrier confirmation, policy, then endorsement, claim and renewal, is proven with authorisation, product and version reproducibility, tenant isolation, deterministic state transitions, an audit trail, web and mobile coverage, API contracts, documents, and insurer-authority separation. The manual proposes the release authority as the seat that would record BT-G2, and the release authority with SRE for BT-G3; the plan's seat table has the insurance-domain practitioner co-recording BT-G2.

What the recording seat would have to see is the manual's gate section. For BT-G2: the slice as one trace bound to a source revision, every confirmed cover carrying its authority, a requests-only profile refusing broker-created confirmation with the refusal audited, indications, quotes and delegated offers kept as distinct records, versions reproduced unchanged after they have moved on, and domain acceptance testing signed by a practitioner as scenarios under the tenant's accepted arrangement. For BT-G3: delayed, duplicate and conflicting confirmations, endorsements and renewals injected and broker state left correct, changes kept as historical transactions with nothing overwritten, the broker's and the insurer's claim status as two fields with the second never written by the platform, two products differing by configuration only, a straight-through trace run under an accepted authority profile with the insurer's confirmation received and not generated, the mobile clients performing no transition the API refuses, and no P2 capability built.

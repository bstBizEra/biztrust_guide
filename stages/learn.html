09

Delivery lifecycle · Learn

Learn

Learn decides whether the eight stages before it run again the same way. Only what this stage changes settles that.

Four things belong here: judging a decision apart from its outcome; naming which stage's gate should have stopped each escaped defect; applying a promotion bar before a soft lesson becomes blocking; and retiring controls, so the set is bounded from above. Incident handling, service objectives, error budgets and the control-versus-attention measure belong to Operate; assurance layers and whether each control can still fail belong to Assure — linked here, never restated.

THE RULE THIS STAGE EXISTS TO ENFORCE

Grade a decision on what was knowable when it was taken; grade its outcome separately. A loop that grades decisions by outcomes is learning luck, and will teach luck back.

01

Preconditions

Entry criteria

All four must hold.

The window is named first

One released Work Package, one incident, or a fixed period; undeclared, it drifts to the loudest grievance.

Legible without the people

Decisions, evidence, timings and approvals readable from artifacts. Otherwise the session grades a memory the outcome has already revised.

Inputs assembled beforehand

Escape list, drawn from Operate’s incident records and the narrow-form findings Assure keeps; decision records from the stages that took them, including each revise-or-defend outcome on an Operate objective; and any near-miss entries that exist — see the note in Promotion; no stage owns a register for them, so do not treat their absence as evidence of none. A session spent reconstructing events adjourns on a resolution.

Someone can amend a gate

Name the role permitted to add, alter or remove a blocking check.

The diff test

Of each proposed output ask which file changes, and who changes it. Where neither can be answered it is not an output: make it an Improvement Work Package with acceptance criteria, or a counted register entry.

03

Judged separately

Decision quality versus outcome quality

A decision is a bet under uncertainty; an outcome is one draw from the distribution it bet on. Grading by outcome punishes sound bets that lost and ratifies reckless ones that won.

The four cells, and what each licenses the review to conclude
DecisionOutcomeWhat Learn may conclude
SoundGoodKeep the method; record what made it sound
SoundBadNothing about the decision. Test only whether reversal cost what was claimed — variance, not signal
UnsoundGoodThe result argues for the method that produced it. Decline that argument
UnsoundBadThe only cell where the outcome is evidence. Name the missing information, not the diligence

This works only on a record left when the decision was taken; a narrative retrofitted afterwards is composed by the party being graded. Two fields carry it:

Options

The alternatives genuinely available then. Architectural ones belong in an ADR — see Architect · ADR discipline.

Expectation

What the decider expected, written before it happened, specific enough to turn out wrong. Without it no review can separate a decision that was right from one narrated as right.

04

Where the gate was

Defect-escape analysis

A defect reaching production has passed seven exit gates — Discover through Release. Operate is production and Learn comes after it, so neither can have been passed by a defect that has just arrived. The question is not who wrote it, but: at which gate could a check have caught this, and in what condition was that gate?

Assure keeps the narrow form, scoped to its verification layers. Learn holds the wide form: an escape may belong to a requirement never written or a contract never frozen. Read it there, then ask the question one stage earlier. Each escape takes one of three verdicts, and the verdict picks the remedy.

No gate existed

Nothing watched for this class of defect. The output is a candidate control, which must clear the promotion bar below — not an immediate blocking check, or the checklist grows by one whenever anything goes wrong.

A gate existed and was skipped

The check was available and did not run. A second check does not repair that: make the skip visible, or the gate unbypassable without a trace.

A gate existed, ran, and passed

Mis-aimed, vacuous, or watching something adjacent. Before an amended check is trusted again it must be made to fail on purpose — see Assure · Controls.

Assign each escape to the earliest stage that could have held it

Not the last stage it passed through. Assigning by final opportunity makes the late stages the only ones that ever improve, while the ambiguous requirement that caused the defect stays exactly as it was. (Release, not Assure, is the last gate an escape passes — it has its own exit gate and its own smoke checks. Assigning to either is the error.)

05

The bound on growth

The promotion bar

Feed the candidate register from the escape ledger above, and from the near-miss signal in Assure — "every near-miss records which control caught it, and which did not". That signal is one line in one section: no stage owns a near-miss register, and Operate keeps none. Until one exists, the escape ledger is the only queue that reliably fills, and any judgement below that needs the near-miss distinction cannot be made. Where a near-miss entry exists, read it through one filter: an entry saying a control caught it needs no promotion; one saying a person noticed means no control caught it, and becomes a candidate. Absent the register, that filter has nothing to run over.

Enforce every candidate and the checklist grows without limit. A soft lesson therefore stays advisory until it clears one of these thresholds, cited with its occurrences in the control change set, during the retrospective rather than mid-task.

  • (a) Three clean applications across distinct Work Packages
  • (b) The failure it prevents has recurred twice — recurrence separates a pattern from an incident
  • (c) Skipping it caused a merged change to be reverted or hot-fixed — one occurrence suffices

Promote one rung at a time.

Enforcement ladder — a lesson rises one rung per promotion
RungFormCost per cycle
1A counted note, or an advisory playbook lineNothing until read
2An item a reviewer must checkReviewer attention, not spent elsewhere
3An automated check that reports, not blocksRuntime; becoming background noise
4A blocking gateEvery run, every author, until retired
Below the bar is not rejected

A lesson that misses stays in the register, count incremented: the next occurrence is the second, not the first again.

06

The step with no natural pressure

Retiring controls

A control added after every incident and never removed becomes a ritual — performed because it is on the list, by people who no longer know what it watches for — while its cost is charged on every run. The classes below sit outside the cost argument, whatever their cost.

Grounds for retirement

  • The condition it guards is now structurally impossible — a constraint or withheld grant does the work
  • Subsumed by the first, and kept only to name the error: it cannot fail. A check that cannot fail while its condition is still live is mis-aimed, not redundant — the gate-ran-and-passed verdict applies, and the remedy is to amend it and make it fail on purpose. Deleting it removes coverage of a live condition and leaves the ledger showing nothing, which reads like success
  • Its cost is charged every cycle and the near-miss record shows it has never been the control that caught anything — not merely that no defect escaped, which is what a control doing its job looks like. Absence from the escape ledger is evidence the control is working, never evidence it is unnecessary. This requires the near-miss distinction between "a control caught it" and "a person noticed" — and no stage currently owns a register for it. Until one does, this ground is unusable and no control may be retired under it. The escape ledger cannot answer the question and must not be substituted

Never retired on cost grounds

  • Anything standing between an operator and irreversible data loss — named here because Architect does not name it
  • Any control implementing a hard invariant — those belong to Architect and are not copied here, because two copies of one list drift and only one of them is the contract
Every retirement names its re-add trigger

State the observation that would bring the control back, before removing it.

THE ASYMMETRY THIS STAGE CORRECTS

Growth in the control set is driven by events; shrinkage by nothing. Unless the retirement review is scheduled beside the promotion review and owned, the set can only grow — converging on being ignored rather than safe.

08

Transition

Exit gate

Learn exits to Discover; the loop closes. The exit condition is "controls improved" — a diff someone can open.

An empty control change set is a finding, not a clean bill

Where the escape ledger has rows, or the register an entry that met the bar, and the change set is empty, the cycle has not exited.

09

Halt

Stop conditions

Stop the session and record the reason.

  • Every output names a resolution; none names a file, check or gate
  • A defect-escape row names an individual, not a gate
  • A lesson is promoted to blocking with no stated trigger or occurrence count
  • The candidate lesson register holds entries no cycle has re-counted
  • A control is retired on the grounds of never having caught anything, while no stage owns a near-miss register — the ground is unusable and the retirement is not permitted
  • A control is retired with no re-add trigger, or implements a hard invariant
  • A decision is graded by its outcome, with no record of what was expected
  • No role is authorized to amend a blocking check
  • Findings are left as comments, not Improvement Work Packages

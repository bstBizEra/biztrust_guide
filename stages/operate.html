Delivery lifecycle · Operate

Operate

Operate is the stage that does not end. Every stage before it happens once per change; this one happens continuously, and it is where most of a system's lifetime is spent.

This page is the single home for steady-state operational guidance: objectives, telemetry, on-call, incident handling, recovery, cost and vulnerabilities. Other stages must link here rather than restate it — a stage that restates guidance creates a copy, and copies drift silently, because nothing fails when two pages disagree. Assure keeps a steady-state section of its own, scoped strictly to continuous verification: which layers re-run, and whether each control can still fail.

THE RULE THIS STAGE EXISTS TO ENFORCE

A system nobody can observe is not in production. It is merely running.

01

Preconditions

Entry criteria

Telemetry is arriving

Configured is not arriving. Confirm the signal reaches the destination and can be queried, before it is the only thing standing between you and a blind incident.

Someone is accountable

A named role owns this service's availability, with the access and the authority to act. A rota with an empty slot is not a rota.

A runbook exists for the known failures

Written for the person who was not there when it was built, at three in the morning, under time pressure.

02

What "working" means

Service objectives

An objective is useful when it can be missed. "High availability" cannot be missed; it can only be disagreed with. Define what a user experiences, then the threshold, then what happens when the threshold is breached.

ElementWhat it isCommon mistake
IndicatorA measurement of something a user feels — a request served correctly, within a time boundMeasuring whether a process is alive, which users do not experience
ObjectiveThe proportion of those that must succeed, over a stated windowA number chosen because it looks impressive rather than because it is affordable
Error budgetThe failure the objective permits — the amount of risk available to spendTreating any failure as unacceptable, which makes the objective advisory
ConsequenceWhat changes when the budget is exhausted, agreed in advanceNo consequence, which makes the whole exercise decorative
ReviewA standing cadence at which actual burn is read against the objective, and the objective revised or defendedSetting objectives once and never reading the burn, so a budget is exhausted before anyone notices
For BizTrust, correctness objectives matter more than uptime ones

A brokerage platform that is available and quotes the wrong premium, or shows a request to bind as confirmed cover, has failed more seriously than one that was briefly unreachable. Objectives should cover financial correctness and state accuracy, not only whether requests returned.

03

The floor

Minimum telemetry

  • Request-level: outcome, latency, tenant context, correlation and causation identifiers, and the revision serving it
  • Domain-level: every material state transition, emitted as a business fact rather than inferred from logs
  • Integration-level: outcome, latency and the provider's own reference for every external call, so their record and yours can be reconciled
  • Financial: every posting, allocation and reversal, traceable to its source transaction
  • Audit: who did what, to which resource, under what authority, protected from ordinary application modification
Sensitive data does not belong in telemetry

Identifiers, references and categories — not personal details, credentials or payment data. Telemetry is copied, forwarded, retained and read by more people than the database is.

04

Who answers

On-call

  • Every alert is actionable. If the response is to look and do nothing, it is not an alert — it is a dashboard entry, and it should be moved there
  • Alert on symptoms, not causes. Users experience slow and wrong; they do not experience high memory usage
  • Alert fatigue is a system failure. A rota that has learned to ignore pages has been trained to, by whoever configured them
  • Escalation is named in advance. Who is called second, and after how long
  • The rota is humane. An exhausted responder makes worse decisions than an absent one, because they act
05

When it breaks

Incident response

The order matters, and it is not the intuitive one. Stop the harm first; understand it second.

Detect

Ideally from a signal, not from a customer. Time-to-detect is worth measuring on its own, because it bounds everything downstream.

Declare

Say the word "incident" out loud and name a coordinator. Ambiguity about whether this is an incident costs more time than any technical step.

Mitigate

Restore service before understanding the cause. Disable the flag, roll back, shed load. Diagnosis performed while users are affected is diagnosis performed badly.

Communicate

What is known, what is not, and when the next update comes. The third is what people actually need; without it they ask, which costs the responder time.

Learn

Blameless review. The output is a change to a control, not a resolution to be more careful. Every incident adds the check that would have caught it, and every recurrence promotes a soft lesson into a blocking one.

THE MEASURE THAT MATTERS

Count what a control caught, not what attention caught. If the ratio shifts toward attention, the controls have stopped covering the surface that is changing.

06

When it breaks badly

Recovery

  • State the objectives: how much time may pass before service returns, and how much data may be lost. Both are business decisions, not technical ones
  • A backup that has never been restored is not a backup. It is an untested hypothesis with a storage cost
  • Drill on a schedule, and write up the result — including the parts that went badly, which are the only parts with information in them
  • Rehearse the human path too: who has access, who authorizes, how they are reached when the usual channel is the thing that is down
  • Recovery for a multi-tenant system is per-tenant. Restoring everything to recover one tenant's data is not a recovery plan
07

Sustainability

Cost and capacity

  • Attribute cost per tenant and per capability, so a growing bill can be explained rather than absorbed
  • Forecast capacity against measured growth, not against the hope that whatever headroom exists is permanent
  • Put rate limits and quotas in place before they are needed — adding them under load changes behaviour for everyone at once
  • Investigate an unexpected cost movement as a signal. It can be the first visible symptom of a defect
08

Staying safe over time

Vulnerability management

  • Inventory and monitor every dependency. An unknown dependency cannot be assessed when an advisory lands
  • Agree a patching timeline by severity before the urgent case arrives, not during it
  • Rotate secrets on a schedule, and exercise the rotation rather than assuming it works
  • Review access periodically, and look first for accounts nobody has needed for a year
  • Publish a disclosure route and answer it. Security researchers who cannot reach you publish instead

Who does what

Roles in this stage

RoleDoesMust not

FinanceOwns cost attribution per tenant and per capability, and the forecast against measured growthAbsorb an unexplained cost movement as a cost of doing business

10

Transition

Continuous exit to Learn

Operate does not exit once. It feeds Learn continuously, and the quality of that feed is what stops the same incident recurring.

11

Halt

Stop conditions

These are conditions to stop shipping, not to stop operating: a service under any of them should keep serving while the condition is fixed, because withdrawing it usually harms more than the condition does.

  • Telemetry has stopped arriving and the system is running unobserved
  • No named person is on call, or the rota has an unfilled slot
  • A restore has never been successfully performed for the current data model
  • The error budget is exhausted and releases are continuing regardless
  • An incident produced no control change and the same failure remains possible
  • A known vulnerability is past its agreed patch timeline with no recorded decision
